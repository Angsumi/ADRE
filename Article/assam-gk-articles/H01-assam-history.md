## Ancient Assam: The Kamarupa Legacy

The earliest well-documented kingdom of the region was **Kamarupa**, which flourished from roughly the 4th to the 12th century under successive dynasties — the Varmans, Mlechchhas, and Palas. Bhaskaravarman, the last great Varman king, is remembered for hosting the Chinese pilgrim **Hiuen Tsang**, whose accounts describe the kingdom's wealth and learning. The region later fragmented into smaller kingdoms — the **Chutia**, **Kachari (Dimasa)**, **Kamata**, and **Baro-Bhuyan** polities — a political situation that the incoming Ahoms would exploit to build their state [1]. For a deeper chapter, study the [history of Assam](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/history-of-assam/) topic page and the [history and heritage of Assam](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/history-heritage-of-assam/) collection.

## The Ahom Dynasty: 600 Years of Sovereignty

The defining chapter of Assam history began in **1228**, when **Sukaphaa** entered the Brahmaputra valley from Mong Mao (in present-day Myanmar) through the Patkai hills with his family and retinue, established his capital at **Charaideo**, and forged alliances with the Moran and Barahi tribes [1] [2]. What followed was one of the longest unbroken regional dynasties in Indian history — nearly six centuries of Ahom rule that ended only with the Treaty of Yandabo in 1826 [3].

The Ahom state was remarkable for its **assimilation policy**: rather than subjugating the indigenous tribes, it absorbed them, creating a genuinely multi-ethnic kingdom. Its administration rested on the **Paik system** (rotational state labour organised into *gots* of four), the **Khel system** (occupation-based groupings), and powerful offices such as the **Borphukan** (western frontier viceroy) and **Barbarua**, both created under Pratap Singha (1603–1641) [1] [2].

The dynasty's great rulers form a repeatable question pattern: **Suhungmung** (the Dihingia Raja, who took the title Swarganarayan and expanded westward), **Pratap Singha** (the administrative consolidator), **Rudra Singha** (the builder-king of Sivasagar's Rang Ghar and Talatal Ghar), and **Purandar Singha** (the last king) [1].

## The Mughal–Ahom Wars and Saraighat

The Ahoms' finest military hour came in the 17th century. After a temporary setback — the **Treaty of Ghilajharighat (1663)** signed by Jayadhwaj Singha under Mughal pressure — the kingdom recovered, and on **26 January 1671** the Ahom forces under **Lachit Borphukan** defeated the Mughal army of Ram Singh I in the **Battle of Saraighat** on the Brahmaputra near Guwahati, one of the finest naval battles in Indian history [4] [5]. Mughal ambitions in the region ended permanently by 1682, and the Ahom boundary stretched to the Manas river [2].

## Decline: Moamoria Rebellion to Yandabo

The late 18th century undid six centuries of stability. The **Moamoria Rebellion (1769–1805)** — a rising of followers of the Mayamara sattra against the Ahom state — killed roughly half the kingdom's population and destroyed its economy [6]. Weakened Assam fell to **Burmese invasions (1817–1826)**, and the British intervened. The **Treaty of Yandabo, signed on 24 February 1826**, ending the First Anglo-Burmese War, ceded Assam (with Manipur, Arakan, and Tenasserim) to the British and ended Ahom sovereignty [3] [7]. Each of these events has a dedicated article on this site: [Moamoria Rebellion](#), [Treaty of Yandabo](#), and [Ahom Kingdom](#).

## British Rule and Its Transformations

British Assam (1826–1947) brought the tea industry, railways, and a new administrative identity — but also cultural imposition. The **attempt to impose Bengali in schools and courts (1836)** triggered the revival movement led by Anandaram Barooah, Hem Chandra Barua, and Gunabhiram Barua, culminating in the **Jonaki era (1889)**, when the magazine *Jonaki* rebirthed Assamese literature. The **Assam Province** was created in **1874** as a Chief Commissioner's province with Shillong as capital, and the **Assam Association (1903)** gave the region its first organised political body [1].

## The Freedom Movement in Assam

Assam's contribution to the independence struggle includes the **Patharughat peasant uprising of 1894** against increased land revenue, the fiery Quit India participation of **Kanaklata Barua** (who died at 17 hoisting the Congress flag at Gohpur in 1942) and **Kushal Konwar**, and above all the political leadership of **Gopinath Bordoloi**, who ensured Assam remained with India in 1947 despite intense pressure [8]. The [freedom movement in Assam](#) article covers every name the exam asks for.

## Post-Independence: From Statehood to the Accord

After 1947, Assam's modern history is defined by three struggles: the **language movement** that restored Assamese, the **division of the state** beginning with Meghalaya's separation (1970/1972) and the northeastern reorganisation, and the **Assam Movement (1979–1985)** against illegal immigration, which ended with the **Assam Accord on 15 August 1985** — the subject of the final chapter of this series, with full coverage of the NRC and citizenship clauses in the [Assam Accord](#) article.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which is the most asked topic in Assam history for ADRE?

The Ahom dynasty — its founder Sukaphaa (1228), major kings, the Battle of Saraighat (1671), and the Treaty of Yandabo (1826) — along with the Assam Movement (1979–85) and Assam Accord (1985).

### How long did the Ahom dynasty rule Assam?

Nearly 600 years, from Sukaphaa's arrival in 1228 to the Treaty of Yandabo in 1826, making it one of the longest unbroken dynasties in Indian history [1] [3].

### What was the ancient name of Assam?

Kamarupa, the name of the classical kingdom that dominated the Brahmaputra valley from roughly the 4th to the 12th century.

### When did British rule begin in Assam?

With the Treaty of Yandabo on 24 February 1826, which ended the First Anglo-Burmese War and ceded Assam to the British East India Company [3] [7].

### Who was the last king of the Ahom dynasty?

Purandar Singha (1818–1819 and 1833–1838); his reigns bracketed the Burmese occupation and the establishment of British control [1].

### Which battle is the most famous in Assam's history?

The Battle of Saraighat (1671), in which the Ahom general Lachit Borphukan defeated the Mughal forces on the Brahmaputra, ending Mughal expansion into Assam [4] [5].
