Assam's clothing culture is a weaving culture: every garment — the Assamese **Mekhela Chador**, the universal **Gamusa**, the tribal **Dokhona**, **Mibu Galuk**, and **Peheok** — is woven on the loin loom that has been part of every Assamese home for centuries [1] [2]. The state's silks (Muga, Pat, Eri) are GI-tagged, and the dress question cluster in ADRE is among the easiest to score. This guide maps every garment to its community and occasion.

## The Mekhela Chador and the Riha

The **Mekhela Chador** is Assam's signature women's attire: a two-piece ensemble where the **mekhela** (the cylindrical skirt) is pleated and the **chador/sador** is draped diagonally across the torso — distinct from the single-piece sari of the rest of India [1] [2]. The **Riha** is the fine, often Muga-silk under-garment worn beneath it — reserved for bridal, ceremonial, and festive use, and the centrepiece of the **Bihu dance** costume, where red-and-white Riha-Mekhela with the Gamusa defines the look [1] [2]. The red-and-white palette (the colours of the Rongali Bihu) has become the unofficial colour identity of the state [2].

## The Gamusa: More Than a Scarf

The **gamusa** — a white cotton towel with red woven borders — is the most democratic garment of Assam: presented to honoured guests, tied around the neck during Bihu, hung in the naamghar, and used as a headband by ploughmen [1] [2]. Its **riva-style (gamosa) woven variant** carries intricate floral and geometric motifs and is a **GI-tagged** product alongside Muga, Pat, and Eri silks [1] [2]. The gamusa's universality makes it the exam's favourite "symbol of Assamese culture" answer.

## The Tribal Wardrobe

Each tribe guards its own weaving identity. The **Bodo** women wear the **Dokhona** (a one-piece dress) with the embroidered **Aronai** stole; the **Mising** wear the **Mibu Galuk** and **Ege** with their striking geometric borders (a Mising woman's attire is recognisable across the riverine villages); the **Karbi** women's **Peheok** dazzles in multicoloured stripes; the **Rabha Nijra**, **Dimasa Risha-Rigu**, and **Tiwa Jwmwrwm** complete the tribal spectrum, while Adivasi tea-garden communities maintain their own distinct dress traditions [1] [2]. Almost all these textiles are handwoven on loin looms — an art concentrated in villages from Sualkuchi (the "Manchester of Assam") to the BTR's weaving cooperatives [1].

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the traditional dress of Assamese women?

The Mekhela Chador — a two-piece ensemble of mekhela (skirt) and chador (drape); the Riha is the fine bridal/auspicious under-garment [1] [2].

### What is a gamusa?

The white cotton towel with red borders used to honour guests, in Bihu dance, and daily life — a universal symbol of Assamese culture and a GI-tagged textile [1] [2].

### What do Bodo women traditionally wear?

The Dokhona (one-piece dress) with the embroidered Aronai stole [1] [2].

### What is the Mising women's dress called?

The Mibu Galuk (with the Ege), known for distinctive geometric weaving in red and black [1] [2].

### What silks is Assam famous for?

Muga (golden, exclusive to Assam), Pat (mulberry white), and Eri (ahimsa/peace silk) — all GI-tagged [1] [2].

### What is Sualkuchi known for?

Weaving — it is called the "Manchester of Assam" for its concentration of silk and cotton loom-work [1] [2].
