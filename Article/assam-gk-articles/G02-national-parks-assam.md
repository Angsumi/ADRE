Assam has **seven national parks** — the highest concentration of protected forests of any Indian state — and together they make the state a wildlife superpower: home to over 70% of the world's **one-horned rhinoceros**, the only populations of **golden langur**, **pygmy hog**, and **wild water buffalo** in protected eastern habitats, and Asia's second-largest tea-wildlife landscape at Kaziranga [1] [2]. For ADRE, park names, establishment years, districts, and superlative species are asked repeatedly. This guide covers every park in exam-ready form.

## Kaziranga: The Rhino Capital

**Kaziranga National Park**, spread across Golaghat, Nagaon, Sonitpur, and Biswanath districts on the south bank of the Brahmaputra, is Assam's crown jewel: a designated **National Park since 1974** (forest reserve from 1905), **UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1985**, and **Tiger Reserve since 2006** [1] [2]. It shelters **over 70% of the world's one-horned rhinoceros** population, holds the country's highest tiger prey density, hosts more than 60% of India's wild buffalo, and has recorded **480 bird species** — including the Bengal florican and greater adjutant [1]. Its "Big Five" — rhino, elephant, wild buffalo, swamp deer, tiger — make it one of India's premier safari destinations, accessible at Kohora and Baguri ranges.

## Manas: The Himalayan Foothill Heritage Site

**Manas National Park** sits on the **Bhutan border** in the bhabar foothills of Barpeta and Baksa districts. Originally a **game reserve from 1928**, it became a **Tiger Reserve in 1974**, a **World Heritage Site in 1985** (delisted during the Bodoland unrest in 1992 and triumphantly restored in 2011), and a **Biosphere Reserve in 1989** [1] [2]. Across its ~500 sq km, the Manas river corridor shelters nearly 60 mammal species, 42 reptiles, and over 476–500 bird species — and it is the only protected home of the **pygmy hog**, **hispid hare**, and **golden langur** together with Bengal florican habitat [1].

## Dibru-Saikhowa, Nameri, and Orang

**Dibru-Saikhowa** (Tinsukia–Dibrugarh, NP 1999) is a Biosphere Reserve famous for its **feral horses**, the endangered **white-winged wood duck**, and birdlife exceeding 380 species in a landscape reshaped by the great 1950 earthquake [1]. **Nameri** (Sonitpur, NP 1998) covers 200 sq km on the Arunachal border and is the core of the **Nameri Tiger Reserve** (344 sq km), with white-water rafting on the Jia Bhoroli [1]. **Orang National Park** (Udalguri–Darrang), often called the **"Mini Kaziranga,"** became a national park in 1999 with roughly 79 sq km and carries a healthy rhino population alongside Bengal florican and otters [1] [2].

## Dehing Patkai and Raimona: The New Parks

The two newest parks reflect Assam's expanding conservation map. **Dehing Patkai** (January 2021, ~232 sq km across Tinsukia and Charaideo) protects the state's **last rainforest** — the "Amazon of Assam" — along the Dehing river, the historic heartland of the Ahom dynasty [1] [2]. **Raimona** (June 2021, ~422 sq km in Kokrajhar) became the Bodoland region's sixth park, safeguarding **golden langur** habitat in the Ripu-Chirang reserve forests on the Bhutan frontier [1].

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many national parks are there in Assam?

Seven: Kaziranga, Manas, Dibru-Saikhowa, Nameri, Orang, Dehing Patkai, and Raimona [1].

### Which national park of Assam is a UNESCO World Heritage Site?

Kaziranga (1985) and Manas (1985) — Manas was delisted in 1992 and restored in 2011 [1] [2].

### Which park has the most one-horned rhinoceros?

Kaziranga, sheltering over 70% of the world's population of the species [1].

### Which are the newest national parks of Assam?

Dehing Patkai (declared January 2021) and Raimona (declared June 2021) [1] [2].

### Which park is known as the "Mini Kaziranga"?

Orang National Park, on the north bank of the Brahmaputra in Udalguri and Darrang districts [1].

### Which park lies on the Bhutan border?

Manas National Park (and Raimona also adjoins the Bhutan frontier in Kokrajhar) [1] [2].
