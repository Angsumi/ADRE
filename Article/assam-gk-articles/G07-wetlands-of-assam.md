## The Beel System: How Wetlands Form in Assam

A *beel* is the Assamese word for a low-lying lake or marsh. The Brahmaputra's annual floods and channel migrations leave behind crescent-shaped **oxbow lakes** and vast seasonal pools across the valley — the Kaziranga floodplain alone contains more than 150 perennial water bodies, and similar beel-fields surround the parks at Pobitora, Laokhowa, and Dibru-Saikhowa [1] [2]. These wetlands are the valley's kidneys: they store floodwater, recharge groundwater, filter pollutants, and host waterfowl that migrate from as far as Siberia each winter [1].

## Deepor Beel: Assam's Ramsar Site

**Deepor Beel**, a permanent freshwater lake on Guwahati's southwestern edge in Kamrup district, was designated a **Ramsar site in 2002** — Assam's flagship international wetland — and is an **Important Bird Area** where wintering ducks, herons, and storks gather in the tens of thousands [1] [2]. Its twin fame is ecological (it lies beside the IIT Guwahati campus and is a recognised biodiversity hotspot) and cautionary (urban encroachment and waste dumping have made it one of India's most discussed conservation cases) [2].

## Maguri Beel and the Border Wetlands

**Maguri Beel**, adjoining Dibru-Saikhowa National Park in Tinsukia, is another globally significant wetland: it shelters the endangered **Bengal florican** and the **white-winged wood duck**, and has been on India's **Ramsar tentative list** for years [1] [2]. In the south, **Son Beel** in Karimganj is one of **Asia's largest seasonal wetlands**: a summer paddy field that becomes a vast lake in monsoon, then hosts the **largest fish market in Asia** during the winter drawdown — an extraordinary natural economy that feeds hundreds of thousands [1].

## Other Notable Wetlands

The wetland map also includes **Pani Dihing** (Sivasagar, a bird sanctuary on the Dihing floodplain), the beels of **Majuli** (shrinking with the island itself), **Numaligarh's** marshlands, the tanks of **Sivasagar's** royal landscape (Rudrasagar, Joysagar, Gaurisagar — engineered water bodies of the Ahom era), and the floodplain complexes at **Laokhowa-Bura Chapori** [1] [2]. Government schemes under the Assam Wetland Policy and the national Wetlands (Conservation and Management) Rules, 2017 now protect the larger sites.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which is the Ramsar site in Assam?

Deepor Beel, designated in 2002 — a permanent freshwater lake near Guwahati and an Important Bird Area [1] [2].

### What is a beel in Assam?

The Assamese word for a lake, marsh, or oxbow wetland — most of Assam's 2,600+ wetlands are floodplain beels formed by the Brahmaputra's shifting channels [1] [2].

### What is Son Beel famous for?

Being one of Asia's largest seasonal wetlands in Karimganj, and hosting the largest fish market in Asia during its winter drawdown [1].

### Where is Maguri Beel?

Adjoining Dibru-Saikhowa National Park in Tinsukia district; it shelters the Bengal florican and white-winged wood duck and is on the Ramsar tentative list [1] [2].

### Why are Assam's wetlands ecologically important?

They store floodwater, recharge groundwater, support migratory and resident waterfowl, sustain fisheries, and sustain the livelihoods of millions in the riverine economy [1] [2].

### How many wetlands does Assam have?

More than 2,600, including beels, marshes, riverine pools, and tanks, the majority natural floodplain wetlands [1] [2].
