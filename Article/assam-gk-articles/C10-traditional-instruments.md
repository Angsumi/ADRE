Assamese music is inseparable from its instruments: the **pepa's** buzz, the **gogona's** twang, the **khol's** thunder, and the **taal's** shimmer form the signature sound of Bihu, the naamghar, and every village festival [1] [2]. ADRE's instrument questions are direct — which instrument is made from what material, and which ensemble belongs to which tradition. This guide gives you the complete instrument map.

## The Bihu Ensemble

The classic **Bihu band** is a five-member ensemble: **dhol** (the large two-headed drum driving the rhythm), **pepa** (the buffalo-horn hornpipe whose continuous buzz is Bihu's sonic identity), **gogona** (the bamboo jaw harp twanged between the teeth), **taal** (the small cymbals), and **baanhi** (the bamboo flute) [1] [2]. Mastering this quintet is the single highest-yield fact for the exam — questions on "Bihu instruments" cycle through these five every year [1].

## The Naamghar Ensemble

The devotional tradition uses a parallel set: the **khol** (the clay two-headed drum of Sankardeva's musical system, also called *mridanga* in the Borgeet tradition), the **taal** and **xaasi** (cymbals of graduated size), and vocal chanting [1] [2]. In Bhaona theatre, the large **Bhortal** cymbals mark entrances and processions, and the **dhol** underscores dramatic scenes — making the satra stage as much a percussion world as a vocal one [1] [2].

## Folk and String Instruments

The folk ballad traditions — **Tokari git** and **Dehabichar git** — are built around their eponymous strings: the **tokari** (a single-string gourd-lute) and the **dotara** (two-stringed lute) [1] [2]. Children's culture contributes the **xutuli**, a clay whistle played with breath and tongue; the **pepa** is likewise a craft of skill — buffalo horns are cut, tuned, and joined so that the player produces its continuous harmonic drone, a technique unique to Assam [1] [2]. Tribal traditions add their own: the Bodo bamboo **khom** and **serja**, the Mising flute traditions, and the Dimasa bamboo percussion of Bushu Dima.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What instruments accompany the Bihu dance?

The five-member ensemble: dhol, pepa, gogona, taal, and baanhi [1] [2].

### What is a pepa made from?

Buffalo horn — two horns cut and joined into a hornpipe producing Bihu's characteristic continuous drone [1] [2].

### What is a gogona?

A bamboo jaw harp held against the teeth and plucked — the metallic twang of Assamese folk music [1] [2].

### What is the khol?

The clay two-headed drum of the naamghar and Borgeet tradition, central to Assam's devotional music [1] [2].

### What are tokari and dotara?

Folk string instruments — the single-string tokari and the two-string dotara — that accompany the Tokari git and Dehabichar git ballads [1] [2].

### What is an xutuli?

A small clay whistle — Assam's traditional toy instrument played with breath and tongue [1] [2].
