The **satras** are Assam's most distinctive institution: Vaishnavite monasteries founded by **Srimanta Sankardeva** from the late 15th century, which became the repositories of the state's religion, literature, music, dance, and crafts [1] [2]. Over 900 satras exist across Assam today, with the greatest concentration on **Majuli** island [1]. For ADRE, satra questions test the founder, the famous monastery names, and the arts each satra preserves. This guide covers it all.

## What a Satra Is

A satra is a **Vaishnavite monastery-community**: a religious centre headed by a **satradhikar**, where resident monks (bhakats) live, worship, and practice the devotional arts of the Ekasarana tradition [1] [2]. Worship centres on congregational chanting of the divine name (harinaam) in the **naamghar** (prayer hall), and on the performance culture Sankardeva designed: the **Borgeet** devotional songs, the **Ankiya Naat** one-act plays staged as **Bhaona**, and the classical **Sattriya** dance that emerged from them [1] [2]. The satra was simultaneously a temple, a school, a theatre, a scriptorium, and a social welfare institution — which is why its influence reached far beyond religion into Assamese identity itself.

## The Great Satras

The most famous satras cluster on **Majuli**, the world's largest river island, where Sankardeva's disciples established enduring centres [1] [2]. **Auniati Satra** (founded by Banamalidev) is renowned for its Borgeet and classical dance; **Dakhinpat Satra** preserves rare manuscripts and mask-making; **Kamalabari** is famous for its *Sutradhar* (sutradhari) dance-drama tradition; **Garamur** guards the oldest Bhaona manuscripts; and **Bengenaati** maintains its own performance lineage [1] [2]. Off the island, **Barpeta Satra**, founded by Sankardeva's greatest disciple **Madhavdeva**, is arguably the most influential satra of all — the centre from which the tradition spread through the Brahmaputra valley — and **Dhekiakhowa Bornamghar** in Jorhat is among the oldest surviving foundations associated with Sankardeva himself [1] [2].

## Satras and Assamese Culture

The satras authored much of what the exam calls "Assamese culture." The **Borgeet** (devotional songs), **Ankiya Naat** (one-act plays), **Bhaona** (theatre), **Sattriya** (classical dance, recognised nationally in 2000), **mukha shilpa** (mask-making — a GI-tagged Majuli craft), and xorai-bota etiquette all flow from the satra world [1] [2]. Historically, satras also functioned as socio-political institutions — some even maintained militias during the Ahom era, a fact relevant to the [Moamoria Rebellion](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/moamoria-rebellion/) chapter, which was itself partly a conflict between the non-conformist Mayamara satra and the royalist satras [2].

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who founded the satras of Assam?

Srimanta Sankardeva in the 15th–16th century, with his disciple Madhavdeva extending the network — most famously at Barpeta [1] [2].

### What religion do the satras follow?

Ekasarana Dharma (neo-Vaishnavism or Namdharma) — devotional worship of one God, Krishna-Vishnu, through congregational chanting [1] [2].

### Who is the head of a satra?

The Satradhikar — the spiritual and administrative head of the monastery [1].

### Which are the famous satras of Majuli?

Auniati, Dakhinpat, Kamalabari, Garamur, and Bengenaati [1] [2].

### What cultural arts do the satras preserve?

Borgeet (devotional songs), Ankiya Naat and Bhaona (theatre), Sattriya (classical dance), and mukha shilpa (mask-making) [1] [2].

### How are satras connected to Assam's history?

Satras were powerful socio-political institutions in the Ahom era; the Moamoria Rebellion (1769–1805) was partly a conflict between the non-conformist Mayamara satra and the royalist satras [2].
