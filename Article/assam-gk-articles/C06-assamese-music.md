Assamese music flows through two great channels: the **classical-devotional** tradition born in the satras — **Borgeet** — and the vast **folk** universe of **bihu geet, Ojapali, Zikir, Tokari git**, and tribal song-cycles [1] [2]. Every festival, every river, and every community in the state has its own sound. For ADRE, music questions test forms and founders: who composed what, which form belongs to which community, and the cinema milestones. This guide covers the complete soundscape.

## Borgeet: The Classical Devotional Canon

**Borgeet** ("great songs") are the classical compositions of **Sankardeva** (e.g., *Mana Meri Ram Charanahi Lagu*, *Kaha Murati*) and **Madhavdeva** (*Rangiya Biraji*) — devotional songs in Ragas that serve as the vocal spine of the satra tradition and the classical music of Assam [1] [2]. Sung in the naamghars and satras with strict rhythmic discipline (taals), Borgeet bridge the devotional and the classical: they are Assam's answer to the dhrupad tradition, and every ADRE question on "Sankardeva's musical composition" expects Borgeet as the answer [1].

## The Folk Universe: Bihu, Ojapali, and Zikir

The folk traditions map the state's communities. **Bihu geet** — energetic, flirtatious, agricultural songs accompanied by dhol, pepa, and gogona — belong to everyone and define the April celebration [1] [2]. **Ojapali** is a narrative folk performance where a lead singer (Oja) with chorus (palis) enacts epics with dance — in three recognised forms: **Sukananni**, **Ramayani**, and **Byahmar Oja** [1]. **Zikir and Zari** are the devotional music of the Assamese Muslim community (Zikir being the Sufi-influenced devotional form); **Tokari git** and **Dehabichar git** are philosophical ballads of the Vaishnavite folk world; **Goalpariya Lokgeet** carries the distinctive romance of western Assam; and tribal song-dances — the Mising **Ali-ai-ligang**, the Deuri **Kherai** (often cited as a folk ancestor of Bihu), the Bodo **Bagurumba**, and the Karbi **Chomangkan** — complete the mosaic [1] [2].

## Cinema and Modern Music

Assam's music entered the modern era through film: ***Alakananda*** (Bhupen Hazarika's first soundtrack, 1949) and especially **Bhupen Hazarika** himself — "the Bard of the Brahmaputra," whose *Bistirno Parore*, *Aami Ek Jaajabor*, and *O Ganga Tu Behti Ho* became national anthems of the river's people [1] [2]. The state's film milestones — ***Bhalobasa*** (1933, first talkie) and ***Joymati*** (1935, Jyoti Prasad Agarwala's classic) — made Hazarika-era and pre-Hazarika music the soundtrack of modern Assamese identity [1].

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are Borgeet?

Classical devotional songs composed by Srimanta Sankardeva and Madhavdeva — the classical music tradition of Assam, sung in satras and naamghars [1] [2].

### What is Ojapali?

A narrative folk performance of Assam where a lead singer (Oja) with a chorus enacts epics through song and dance; types include Sukananni, Ramayani, and Byahmar Oja [1].

### Which community sings Zikir?

The Assamese Muslim (Maiman and Deshi) community — Zikir is its Sufi-influenced devotional music; Zari is the celebratory wedding form [1] [2].

### What is the origin of Bihu music according to folk tradition?

The Deuri tribe's Kherai ritual dance-song is often cited as a folk ancestor of the Bihu tradition [1].

### Who is the most famous modern Assamese musician?

Bhupen Hazarika, "the Bard of the Brahmaputra," whose songs like *Bistirno Parore* and *O Ganga Tu Behti Ho* became pan-Indian classics [1] [2].

### What are the first Assamese films?

*Bhalobasa* (1933, the first Assamese talkie) and *Joymati* (1935, directed by Jyoti Prasad Agarwala) [1].
