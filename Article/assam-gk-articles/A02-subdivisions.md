Between Assam's **35 districts** and its hundreds of **circles** sits the **subdivision** — the intermediate administrative tier headed by a **Sub-Divisional Officer (SDO)** that carries out revenue, magisterial, and coordination functions closest to the people [1] [2]. Subdivision questions in ADRE are simpler than they look: the tier structure, the SDO's role, and the subdivision's place in the field hierarchy. This guide covers the full picture.

## What a Subdivision Is

A **subdivision** is a geographic grouping of circles and revenue villages within a district, created for administrative manageability: the DC delegates revenue supervision, executive magistracy, and departmental coordination to the SDO, who acts as the DC's deputy for that territory [1] [2]. Larger districts like Kamrup Metropolitan, Sonitpur, and Cachar run four to six subdivisions; smaller ones run two or three. The count changes as the state creates new subdivisions to match population growth — recent years have seen new units in fast-growing districts of the Brahmaputra valley [1] [2].

## The SDO: Roles and Authority

The **SDO** wears three functional hats. As **sub-divisional magistrate**, the officer exercises executive magisterial powers — land disputes, Section 144-type orders, disaster response within the sub-division. As **revenue supervisor**, the SDO oversees the circle officers and the **rangia** village heads in land records, mutation, and the digitisation drives like **Mission Basudha** [1] [2]. As **field coordinator**, the SDO aligns agriculture, health, education, and social-welfare departments at the sub-divisional level — making the SDO the officer ordinary citizens meet most often for land, certificate, and relief work.

## The Tier Ladder: District to Village

The complete field ladder runs: **Government of Assam (Secretariat, Dispur) → Divisional Commissioner (5 divisions) → District (DC) → Subdivision (SDO) → Circle (Circle Officer) → Revenue village (Rangia) → Gaon Panchayat** [1] [2]. Understanding this ladder answers most ADRE hierarchy questions: the SDO reports to the DC, the CO reports to the SDO, and the rangia reports to the CO. In the Sixth Schedule districts of **Karbi Anglong and Dima Hasao**, autonomous council secretariats interpose between the division and the district tiers [2].

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a subdivision in Assam?

An intermediate administrative unit between the district and the circle, headed by a Sub-Divisional Officer (SDO) [1] [2].

### Who heads a subdivision?

The Sub-Divisional Officer (SDO), typically of the Assam Civil Service rank, exercising revenue, magisterial, and coordination powers [1] [2].

### How many subdivisions does each district have?

Most districts have 2–6 subdivisions depending on size and population; larger districts like Kamrup Metropolitan and Sonitpur have more [1] [2].

### What is the administrative tier ladder of Assam?

Secretariat → Divisional Commissioner → District (DC) → Subdivision (SDO) → Circle (CO) → Revenue village (Rangia) → Gaon Panchayat [1] [2].

### What powers does an SDO hold?

Sub-divisional magistracy (public order), revenue supervision (land records, mutation), and coordination of field departments [1] [2].

### What is a rangia?

The appointed headman of a revenue village — the base unit of Assam's revenue administration, reporting to the circle officer [1] [2].
