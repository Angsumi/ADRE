Agriculture is the occupation of nearly two-thirds of Assam's workforce and the cultural rhythm behind every Bihu festival [1] [2]. The state's farm economy runs on a triumvirate — **rice, jute, and tea** — plus a remarkable flood-adapted rice tradition, and a growing list of **GI-tagged** produce that the ADRE exam loves to ask about. This guide covers crops, seasons, GI products, and every agricultural superlative of the state.

## Rice: The Staple and Its Three Faces

Rice is both Assam's principal crop and its identity — the state's name for its new-year festival, **Bihu**, is itself an agrarian calendar marker (Rongali in April marking sowing, Kongali in October marking scarcity, Bhogali in January marking harvest) [1] [2]. Three rice types define the agronomy: **sali**, the main winter (autumn-transplanted) crop occupying the largest area; **ahu**, the summer crop; and the extraordinary **bao** (deep-water or "floating") rice, sown before the floods and rising with the water to be harvested from boats — one of the most distinctive flood-adapted farming systems on Earth [1] [2].

## Jute, Tea, and the Cash Crops

**Jute** — the golden fibre — makes Assam among the largest jute-producing states of India, concentrated in the Brahmaputra and Barak lowlands; the state's jute mills (Barpeta Road, Goalpara, Nagaon) process the fibre [1]. **Tea** is treated separately in the [tea industry article](#), but its agricultural base matters here: Assam grows roughly half of India's tea and about 17% of the world's, and its CTC (crush-tear-curl) black tea is the backbone of global blends [2]. Sugarcane, rapeseed-mustard, potato, and horticulture (banana, areca nut, pineapple, and the famous **Kaji Nemu** — the GI-tagged Assam lemon) complete the cash-crop picture [1] [2].

## GI-Tagged Produce: The Exam's Favourites

Assam's **Geographical Indication** registrations are heavily agricultural, and ADRE repeatedly tests them: **Joha rice** (the fragrant premium rice), **Boka Chaul** (sticky black rice), **Kaji Nemu** (lemon), **Muga silk** (the golden silk — sericulture agriculture), **Gamosa**, **Sarai Bhat** (a rice variety), **Tamul-Pan** (betel nut and leaf culture), and **Assam orthodox/CTC tea** [1] [2]. Memorise the list as a set; questions usually ask "which of the following is a GI product of Assam."

## Institutions and Challenges

The **Assam Agricultural University** (Jorhat, 1969) anchors research and extension; the state's flood reality — erosion of fields along the river, annual inundation destroying standing crops — is the great structural challenge, alongside fragmentation of landholdings and the shift of youth to services [1] [2]. Government programmes (PM-KISAN, soil health cards, the state's own agricultural missions) target exactly these constraints.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the main crop of Assam?

Rice — about two-thirds of the workforce is engaged in agriculture, and rice dominates the cultivated area [1] [2].

### What are the three rice varieties of Assam?

Sali (winter rice, the main crop), ahu (summer rice), and bao (deep-water floating rice grown in flooded fields) [1] [2].

### What is bao rice?

A deep-water "floating" rice variety sown before floods that rises with the water and is harvested from boats — Assam's unique flood-adapted farming [1].

### Which cash crops does Assam produce prominently?

Jute (among India's top producing states), tea (about half of India's output), sugarcane, rapeseed-mustard, and horticulture including Kaji Nemu [1] [2].

### Which agricultural products of Assam have GI tags?

Joha rice, Boka Chaul, Kaji Nemu, Sarai Bhat, Tamul-Pan, Muga silk, Gamosa, and Assam tea [1] [2].

### Which institution leads agricultural research in Assam?

The Assam Agricultural University at Jorhat, established in 1969 [1].
