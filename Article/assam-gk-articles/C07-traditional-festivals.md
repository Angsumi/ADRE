While **Bihu** unites the state, Assam's true festival richness lies in its **community calendars**: the Mising, Bodo, Rabha, Karbi, Dimasa, Deuri, Tiwa, and Ahom communities each hold spring, harvest, and ancestor festivals that ADRE tests through matching questions — "which festival belongs to which community" [1] [2]. This guide maps every major traditional festival to its people, month, and signature ritual.

## The Spring Festivals: Ali-ai-ligang, Baikhho, Rongker

The northeast Assam spring cycle begins with the **Mising** community's **Ali-ai-ligang** ("sow, we grow"), usually February–March: women in traditional dress perform the rhythmic **gosu dance**, weaving the patterns of their **mibu galuk** and Eri shawls into the choreography, while agricultural tools are ceremonially honoured before sowing [1] [2]. The **Rabha** mark spring with **Baikhho** (April–May), five days of bamboo dance, community feast, and goddess worship that echoes the wider Bihu season [1] [2]. The **Karbi** observe **Rongker** in February — a purification festival where villages cleanse themselves and their fields, and certain taboos (no fishing, no travel) are observed to honour the earth before ploughing [1] [2].

## The Ahom and the Deuri Ancestors

**Me-Dam-Me-Phi**, observed by the **Ahom** community every **31 January** — the day before Sukaphaa Divas — honours the ancestors of the 600-year Ahom kingdom with offerings of rice, meat, and wine at household altars [1] [2]. It pairs with **Sukaphaa Divas** (Assam Day, 2 December) to bookend the Ahom calendar. The **Deuri** perform **Kherai**, the ritual worship of Bathou — widely considered the folk root of Bihu itself — where priestesses dance with brass plates through the night [1] [2].

## Shrines and Harvest Feasts

Two festivals transcend community boundaries. The **Ambubachi Mela** at the **Kamakhya temple**, Guwahati (June–July, Ashad month), celebrates the earth's annual menstruation — one of India's great tantric pilgrimages, drawing lakhs during the monsoon [1] [2]. The **Dimasa** harvest festival **Bushu Dima** (January) in the hills of Dima Hasao features bamboo dances and thanksgiving feasts — the hills' answer to Bhogali Bihu [1] [2]. Modern additions include the **Dehing Patkai Festival** (upper Assam, January) and village **Dhepur Utsavs** that stage the grand communal Rongali Bihu.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which festival belongs to the Mising community?

Ali-ai-ligang — the spring sowing festival celebrated in February–March with the gosu dance [1] [2].

### What is Me-Dam-Me-Phi?

The Ahom community's ancestor-worship festival observed every 31 January, the day before Sukaphaa Divas [1] [2].

### Which festival is the folk ancestor of Bihu?

The Deuri community's Kherai ritual is widely considered the folk root of the Bihu tradition [1] [2].

### What is the Ambubachi Mela?

The annual tantric festival at the Kamakhya temple, Guwahati (June–July), celebrating the earth's fertility — among India's largest religious gatherings [1] [2].

### Which festival do the Karbi celebrate?

Rongker — a February purification festival before spring sowing, observed with taboos on fishing and travel [1] [2].

### Which festival do the Rabha celebrate?

Baikhho — a five-day spring festival in April–May with bamboo dance and community feasting [1] [2].
