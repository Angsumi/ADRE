The **Battle of Saraighat**, fought in **1671** on the Brahmaputra near Guwahati, is Assam's most celebrated military moment: the Ahom navy under **Lachit Borphukan** decisively defeated the Mughal army of **Ram Singh I**, ending Mughal expansion into the region permanently [1] [2]. It remains one of the few purely **naval battles** in Indian history, and ADRE asks about it almost every year — usually as a date, commander, or "naval battle" identification. This guide gives you the causes, the campaign, the tactics, and the exam fact sheet.

| Location | Saraighat, on the Brahmaputra river near Guwahati |
| Ahom commander | Lachit Borphukan (Borphukan, supreme western commander) |

## The Causes: Why the Mughals Returned

The conflict resumed because the Ahoms had never accepted the **Treaty of Ghilajharighat (1663)**, in which Jayadhwaj Singha, under Mughal military pressure, had ceded western territories and agreed to tribute [1]. When **Chakradhwaj Singha** became king in 1663, he rejected the treaty's terms, expelled the Mughal garrisons, and pushed the Ahom frontier back toward Guwahati [1] [2]. Aurangzeb responded by sending **Ram Singh I** — a battle-hardened Rajput commander with a large force including elite Rajput musketeers — to restore Mughal control over Kamrup [1] [4].

## The Campaign: Alaboi to Saraighat

The early phase went against the Ahoms. In the **Battle of Alaboi (1669)**, Ram Singh's forces inflicted one of the worst defeats in Ahom history [4]. Lachit Borphukan, however, refused open battle thereafter. He fortified the Saraighat narrows, built a chain of stockades on both banks of the river, harassed Mughal supply lines with hit-and-run raids, and exploited the two advantages no Mughal army could neutralise: **riverine mobility** and **disease in the monsoon** [4] [5]. The Ahom navy — hundreds of swift boats commanded by able officers like Bagh Hazarika — controlled the Brahmaputra in a way the Mughal artillery could not contest.

## The Decisive Battle

The climactic engagement of 1671 saw the Mughal army attempting to force the Saraighat channel by land and river simultaneously. Ahom forces attacked from fortified positions while their navy pressed the Mughal boats; the narrow waterway nullified the Mughal numbers, and the Rajput forces, unaccustomed to river warfare, broke under the coordinated assault [1] [5]. Tradition preserves Lachit's defining moment: gravely ill, he rose from his sickbed to lead from his boat, reportedly declaring that he had come to fight for his motherland, not to retreat. Ram Singh I withdrew, and the Mughals never mounted a serious invasion of Assam again — their regional presence ended by **1682** [1] [5].

## Why Saraighat Matters

Saraighat secured two things. Militarily, it fixed the Ahom western boundary at the **Manas river** and ended the Mughal-Ahom wars that had raged since 1615 [2]. Symbolically, it made Lachit Borphukan the archetype of Assamese courage — remembered every **24 November** on Lachit Divas [3]. For the exam, Saraighat is the anchor fact of the entire Ahom-Mughal chapter: 1671, Lachit Borphukan, Ram Singh I, naval battle, Brahmaputra, Guwahati [1] [5].

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When was the Battle of Saraighat fought?

In 1671, with the decisive engagement on the Brahmaputra near Guwahati; it ended Mughal expansion into Assam [1] [5].

### Who led the Ahom forces at Saraighat?

Lachit Borphukan, the Ahom Borphukan (supreme western commander), serving under King Chakradhwaj Singha [1] [2].

### Who was the Mughal commander defeated at Saraighat?

Ram Singh I of Amber, a Rajput general sent by Emperor Aurangzeb [1] [5].

### What type of battle was Saraighat?

Primarily a naval battle fought on the Brahmaputra river — one of the few major naval engagements in Indian history [5].

### What was the result of the Battle of Saraighat?

A decisive Ahom victory: the Mughals withdrew permanently (their presence ending by 1682) and the Ahom boundary extended to the Manas river [1] [2].

### What was the Battle of Alaboi?

The 1669 battle in which Ram Singh's forces defeated the Ahoms badly — the setback that preceded Lachit's attrition strategy and ultimate victory at Saraighat [4].
