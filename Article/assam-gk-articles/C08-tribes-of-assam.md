Assam is among India's most tribally diverse states: **14 major tribes** and dozens of communities live alongside the Assamese majority — the **Bodo** as the largest plains tribal group, the **Mising** as the largest riverine community, and the **Karbi** and **Dimasa** as the dominant hill peoples [1] [2]. Their festivals, autonomous councils, and languages form one of ADRE's most reliable question clusters. This guide gives you every tribe with its festival, council, and claim to fame.

## The Plains Tribals

The **Bodo** — the largest plains tribal community of Assam — speak a Tibeto-Burman language recognised in the **Eighth Schedule** (since 1992) and govern the **Bodoland Territorial Region** under the 2020 BTR Accord [1] [2]. The **Mising** (the largest riverine community, concentrated in Lakhimpur, Dhemaji, and Majuli) celebrate **Ali-ai-ligang** and weave distinctive Eri cotton; the **Rabha** of the western districts hold **Baikhho**; the **Sonowal Kachari** — historically the Ahom kingdom's goldsmiths, from whom Sankardeva's community drew strength — observe **Hachari**; and the **Deuri** keep the **Kherai** ritual alive as a Bihu ancestor [1] [2]. The **Tiwa (Lalung)** of Morigaon and the Karbi borderlands celebrate **Pisha**, while the **Adivasi** (Munda, Santhal, Oraon) tea-garden communities of central Assam form a large, distinct cultural block of their own [1] [2].

## The Hill Tribes

The **Karbi** — the largest hill tribe of Assam — dominate the Karbi Anglong plateau and celebrate **Rongker** (the land-purification spring festival); their hill identity is administered through the **Karbi Anglong Autonomous Council** [1] [2]. The **Dimasa** of Dima Hasao (North Cachar Hills) hold **Bushu Dima**, the January harvest thanksgiving, under the **Dima Hasao Autonomous Council** — the only hill district south of the Brahmaputra [1] [2]. Smaller hill communities include the Hmar, Hrangkhol, and Kuki settlements along the southern borders.

## The Tai Communities and the Ahom

Assam's eastern districts host a unique Tai family: the **Ahom** (who ruled for 600 years and observe **Me-Dam-Me-Phi** and **Sukaphaa Divas**), the **Tai-Phake**, **Tai-Khamyang**, and **Tai-Aiton** — all Buddhist Tai groups of upper Assam with their own scripts and monasteries — plus the **Chutia** and **Moran**, who were absorbed into the Ahom kingdom and live on in place names and surnames (Sivasagar's Sutiya legacy) [1] [2]. The Ahom language, once the court tongue, is studied today as a classical heritage of the state.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which is the largest plains tribal community of Assam?

The Bodo, who govern the Bodoland Territorial Region under the 2020 BTR Accord; the Bodo language is in the Eighth Schedule [1] [2].

### Which is the largest riverine tribe of Assam?

The Mising, concentrated in Lakhimpur, Dhemaji, and Majuli, celebrating Ali-ai-ligang each spring [1] [2].

### Which tribes dominate Assam's hill districts?

The Karbi (Karbi Anglong; Rongker festival) and the Dimasa (Dima Hasao; Bushu Dima festival) [1] [2].

### Who were the Sonowal Kachari?

A plains tribal community historically serving as goldsmiths to the Ahom kingdom — "Sonowal" literally meaning gold-worker [1] [2].

### Which Tai communities live in Assam?

The Ahom, Tai-Phake, Tai-Khamyang, and Tai-Aiton — Buddhist Tai groups of upper Assam with their own scripts [1] [2].

### What is the Ahom community's main festival?

Me-Dam-Me-Phi (31 January, ancestor worship) and Sukaphaa Divas (2 December, Assam Day) [1] [2].
