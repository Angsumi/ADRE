Assam is a state made of water. Over **50 rivers** drain it, organised into two great systems: the **Brahmaputra**, which carves the vast central valley, and the **Barak**, which governs the southern districts [1] [2]. River questions are among the most reliable marks in the ADRE geography section — origins, tributary names, and superlatives like **Majuli** and **Umananda** appear year after year. This guide gives you every river, every direction, and every exam fact in one place.

## The Brahmaputra: Son of Brahma

The Brahmaputra rises from the **Chemayungdung glacier** near the Kailash range in Tibet, travels roughly **2,900 km**, and flows about **720 km** through Assam — entering at **Sadiya** and leaving at **Dhubri** into Bangladesh, where it meets the Ganga as the Meghna system [1] [2]. It is the only major Indian river with a **male name** in Hindu tradition, and in Assam it is affectionately called **Luit** — the "Red River" of its silt-laden monsoon floods [1]. The river carries one of the heaviest sediment loads on Earth, runs as wide as **18 km** near Majuli in flood, and narrows to barely a kilometre at Guwahati [2]. It hosts **National Waterway 2 (NW-2)**, the Sadiya–Dhubri corridor of ~891 km [1].

## North Bank and South Bank Tributaries

The tributaries arrive in strict geometric order, a fact the exam loves to test. **North bank tributaries** — descending from Tibet, Arunachal Pradesh, and Bhutan — include the **Subansiri** (the longest, joining near Bandardewa), **Dibang**, **Lohit** (both meeting the Brahmaputra at Sadiya), **Jiabharali (Kameng)**, **Manas** (from Bhutan, joining at Barpeta), **Sankosh** (which forms part of the Assam–West Bengal border), **Beki**, **Puthimari**, and **Pagladia** [1] [2]. **South bank tributaries** — from Meghalaya, Nagaland, and the Manipur hills — include the **Dhansiri** (from Nagaland, flowing through Golaghat), **Kopili** (with the Umrangshu hydel project, joining at Morigaon), **Dihing/Dehing** (crossing the Dehing Patkai forest), **Disang**, **Dikhow**, **Jhanji**, **Kalang**, and **Bhogdoi** [1] [2].

## The Barak System: The Southern Lifeline

The **Barak** rises in the Manipur hills at around 2,900 m and flows roughly 564–900 km (estimates vary by source segment) through **Cachar, Hailakandi, and Karimganj** — the three Barak Valley districts culturally and linguistically distinct from the Brahmaputra valley [1] [2]. **Silchar** is its principal city. In Bangladesh it divides into the **Surma and Kushiyara** rivers, ultimately reaching the Bay of Bengal through the Meghna [1] [2]. Its main tributaries are the **Sonai, Katakhal, Jiri, Madhura (Modhura), Longai, Rukni, Chiri, and Singla** [2] [3].

## Majuli and Umananda: The Islands

Two islands anchor the exam's superlatives. **Majuli**, in the Brahmaputra off Jorhat, is the **world's largest river island** at about 880 sq km (reduced by erosion from a historical ~1,250 sq km), declared **India's first island district in 2016** and home to more than twenty Vaishnavite **satras** [1]. **Umananda**, the "Peacock Island" mid-river at Guwahati, is among the **world's smallest inhabited river islands** (~0.012 sq km) and carries the famous **Umananda Shiva temple**, reached by ferry from Kachari Ghat [1].

## Bridges, Waterways, and Floods

The river story includes its crossings: the **Saraighat Bridge (1962)**, the first railway-cum-road bridge over the Brahmaputra at Guwahati; the **Kolia Bhomora Setu** at Tezpur; the **Naranarayan Setu** at Jogighopa; and the **Bogibeel Bridge** — India's longest combined rail-road bridge, linking Dibrugarh and Sadiya [2]. Annual **flooding** remains Assam's defining environmental challenge, with the Brahmaputra's erosion reshaping islands like Majuli every decade [1].

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which are the two major river systems of Assam?

The Brahmaputra system and the Barak system; the former drains the central valley and the latter the southern districts of Cachar, Karimganj, and Hailakandi [1] [2].

### Where does the Brahmaputra originate?

From the Chemayungdung glacier in Tibet near the Kailash range, at about 5,150 m altitude [1] [2].

### What is the longest tributary of the Brahmaputra in Assam?

The Subansiri, about 442 km long, originating in Tibet and joining the Brahmaputra near Bandardewa [1] [2].

### What is Majuli known for?

Being the world's largest river island, Assam's first island district (2016), and the cultural heartland of the Vaishnavite satras [1].

### What happens to the Barak river in Bangladesh?

It splits into the Surma and Kushiyara rivers and eventually joins the Meghna system draining into the Bay of Bengal [1] [2].

### Which river is called the Red River and why?

The Brahmaputra, called Luit in Assam, for the reddish silt it carries during monsoon floods [1].
