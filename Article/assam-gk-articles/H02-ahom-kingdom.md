The **Ahom Kingdom** is the single most exam-worthy unit of Assam history. Ruling from **1228 to 1826**, it held sovereignty for nearly six centuries — longer than most dynasties anywhere in India — and built a multi-ethnic state whose administration, military, and culture still define Assam's identity [1] [2]. This guide covers everything ADRE asks about the Ahoms: the kings in order, the Paik and Khel systems, the capitals through the ages, and the Mughal wars.

## Sukaphaa and the Foundation (1228)

On **2 December 1228**, Sukaphaa reached modern-day **Namrup** on the south bank of the Burhidihing river, having travelled from Mong Mao with his three queens, two sons, and a retinue of nobles and soldiers [2]. His genius was diplomatic rather than military: he allied with the **Moran and Barahi tribes**, shared the superior technology of wet-rice cultivation, and gradually absorbed these communities into the Ahom polity rather than conquering them [2]. This assimilation policy created the multi-ethnic Assamese society of today. He established the first capital at **Charaideo** — the Ahom sacred city of mound-burials — and ruled until 1268 [1] [2].

## The Great Kings, in Exam Order

The exam rarely asks for the full list; it asks for the landmark rulers. **Suhungmung (1497–1539)**, called the Dihingia Raja, was the first to adopt a Hindu title (**Swarganarayan**) and expanded the kingdom westward, with his political centre at Bakata [1]. **Suklengmung** moved the capital to **Garhgaon** and is credited with the Ahom kingdom's first coinage [1] [2]. **Pratap Singha (Susengpha, 1603–1641)** reorganised the Paik system and created the offices of **Barbarua** (supreme internal military and judicial head) and **Borphukan** (western frontier viceroy) [1] [2]. **Jayadhwaj Singha (Sutamla, 1648–1663)** signed the **Treaty of Ghilajharighat (1663)**, ceding territory to the Mughals — a setback the kingdom later reversed [1]. **Gadadhar Singha (Supatpha, 1681–1696)** restored stability after the chaos of the weak kings and supported Vaishnavite institutions [1]. **Rudra Singha (Sukhrumpha, 1696–1714)** presided over the golden age: he built the capital at **Rangpur (Sivasagar)**, the Rang Ghar, Talatal Ghar, and Kareng Ghar, and pursued bold diplomacy [1] [2]. **Purandar Singha** was the **last Ahom king** (1818–1819, 1833–1838) [1].

## The Paik System: Administration That Lasted 600 Years

The Paik system was the economic and military backbone of the Ahom state. Every able-bodied adult male (other than nobles and Brahmins) was registered as a **paik** and grouped into **gots** of four; at any time, one paik from each got rendered state service — labour, militia duty, or crafts — while the other three worked their land, rotating in turn [1] [2]. Occupations were grouped into **Khels**, which functioned like guilds and supplied skilled labour to the state. The system gave the Ahoms a large standing militia without a cash economy — the key reason they could field armies against the Mughals for seventy years [1] [2].

## Religion, Culture, and Decline

The later Ahom kings — from Rudra Singha onward — adopted Hindu titles and practices while keeping the traditional administrative structure, a synthesis that integrated the kingdom with Assamese society [1]. The Tungkhungia period (Rudra Singha, Sib Singha, Pramatta Singha, Rajeswar Singha) produced the magnificent monuments of Sivasagar. But internal conflicts followed, then the **Moamoria Rebellion (1769–1805)** devastated the population and economy, Burmese invasions (1817–1826) broke the weakened state, and the **Treaty of Yandabo (1826)** transferred Assam to the British [3] [4].

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who founded the Ahom Kingdom and when?

Chaolung Sukaphaa founded it on 2 December 1228, after entering the Brahmaputra valley from Mong Mao through the Patkai hills and settling initially at Namrup before making Charaideo his capital [2].

### What was the Paik system of the Ahom Kingdom?

A citizen-service system in which every adult male was a paik, organised in gots of four; one paik served the state at a time in rotation, giving the kingdom free militia and labour [1] [2].

### Which Ahom king is associated with the Paik system reforms?

Pratap Singha (Susengpha, 1603–1641), who reorganised the system and created the Barbarua and Borphukan offices [1].

### What were the capitals of the Ahom Kingdom?

Charaideo (Sukaphaa), Bakata (Suhungmung), Garhgaon (from Suklengmung), Rangpur/Sivasagar (Rudra Singha), and finally Jorhat (late kings) [1] [2].

### Who was the last king of the Ahom dynasty?

Purandar Singha, who reigned in 1818–1819 and 1833–1838; the Treaty of Yandabo in 1826 ended Ahom sovereignty [1] [3].

### How did the Ahom Kingdom end?

Internal collapse from the Moamoria Rebellion, followed by Burmese invasions (1817–1826), ended by the Treaty of Yandabo (1826), which ceded Assam to the British [3] [4].
