The **Assam Movement (1979–1985)** was one of post-independence India's largest and most sustained mass agitations: a six-year, student-led struggle against **illegal immigration** into Assam that ended with the signing of the **Assam Accord on 15 August 1985** [1] [2]. For ADRE, it is arguably the single most-repeated modern history topic — the dates, the leaders, the demand, and the Accord's cutoff clauses appear in almost every paper. This guide covers the full arc: trigger, leadership, key events, martyrs, and outcome.

## The Trigger: Mangaldoi, 1979

The immediate spark was the **Mangaldoi Lok Sabha by-election of 1979**, where election observers reported an extraordinary number of alleged foreign nationals — largely migrants from East Pakistan/Bangladesh — on the voter rolls [1] [2]. Assam had absorbed successive migration waves since Partition (1947) and the Bangladesh Liberation War (1971), and the perception that these voters were altering the state's demography, economy, and politics ignited a province-wide agitation. AASU and AAGSP launched a programme of **bandhs, rallies, boycotts, and mass civil disobedience** that paralysed normal life for six years [1] [2].

## The Six Years: Events and Sacrifices

The movement's timeline is ADRE's favourite question cluster. In **1979**, the agitation began; that year the agitation's first martyrs fell, and **17 August** is now observed as the state's Martyrs' Day (Shaheed Diwas) in memory of Khargeswar Konwar and fellow agitators killed in police firing [1]. The **Nellie massacre of February 1983** — violence against Bengali-speaking villagers in Nagaon district during the boycott of elections — remains the movement's darkest episode [1] [2]. Elections were boycotted in 1983; negotiations with the central government stretched across 1983–1985, and the movement finally concluded when the young leaders signed the **Assam Accord on 15 August 1985** [1] [2].

## The Assam Accord: What Was Settled

The Accord created a three-tier framework for dealing with foreigners based on entry dates (detailed in the [Assam Accord](#) article): those who entered **before 1 January 1966** would receive full citizenship rights; those between **1 January 1966 and 24 March 1971** would be deleted from electoral rolls for ten years; and those who entered **after 25 March 1971** would be detected and deported [2]. The Accord also promised safeguards for Assamese culture and language and measures against illegal immigration. Its signing transformed Assam's politics: the leaders formed the **Asom Gana Parishad (AGP) on 14 October 1985**, and **Prafulla Kumar Mahanta** became Assam's Chief Minister in December 1985 at age 32 — India's youngest chief minister [1] [2].

## Legacy and Later Developments

The Accord's implementation — especially the foreigners issue — drove Assam's politics for four decades: the **IMDT Act (1983)**, struck down by the Supreme Court in 2005 (*Sarbananda Sonowal v. Union of India*), the long-running **NRC update** concluded in 2019, and the citizenship question that national politics inherited. For ADRE, keep the quartet intact: **1979–1985, AASU-AAGSP, Nellie (1983), Accord (15 August 1985)** [1] [2].

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When did the Assam Movement take place?

From 1979 to 1985 — a six-year agitation against illegal immigration into Assam [1] [2].

### What triggered the Assam Movement?

The detection of a large number of alleged foreign nationals in the voter list during the Mangaldoi Lok Sabha by-election of 1979 [1] [2].

### Who led the Assam Movement?

The AASU (All Assam Students' Union) and AAGSP (All Assam Gana Sangram Parishad), with leaders like Prafulla Kumar Mahanta, Bhrigu Kumar Phukan, and Samujjal Bhattacharya [1] [2].

### What is Nellie in the context of the Assam Movement?

The site of the February 1983 massacre of Bengali-speaking villagers during the agitation's election boycott — the movement's darkest episode [1].

### How did the Assam Movement end?

With the signing of the Assam Accord on 15 August 1985 between the Government of India, the Government of Assam, AASU, and AAGSP [1] [2].

### What was the political outcome of the Assam Movement?

The formation of the Asom Gana Parishad (AGP) in October 1985 and Prafulla Kumar Mahanta becoming Chief Minister in December 1985 as India's youngest CM [1] [2].
