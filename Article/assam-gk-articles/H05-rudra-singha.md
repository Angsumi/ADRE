If Sukaphaa founded the Ahom Kingdom and Lachit Borphukan defended it, **Rudra Singha** built its finest monuments and presided over its cultural zenith. Reigning from **1696 to 1714**, he moved the capital to **Rangpur** (modern Sivasagar), constructed the iconic **Rang Ghar**, **Talatal Ghar**, and **Kareng Ghar**, adopted full Hindu royal titles, and projected Ahom power through diplomacy as far as Delhi and Bengal [1] [2]. For ADRE, Rudra Singha is the "builder king" answer to questions about Sivasagar's monuments and the Ahom golden age.

## The King and His Ascent

Rudra Singha, born **Lai**, was the son of **Gadadhar Singha (Supatpha)**, the restorer-king who had stabilised the kingdom after the turbulent weak-kings period following Lachit Borphukan's era [1]. Inheriting a consolidated state with full royal authority, Rudra Singha became the first Ahom monarch to rule with the confidence of an imperial sovereign. His **Ahom name Sukhrumpha** appears in the Buranjis, while his Hindu title marks the completion of a process begun by Suhungmung two centuries earlier [1] [2].

## Rangpur: A New Capital and Its Monuments

His most visible legacy is architectural. He shifted the capital to **Rangpur**, now the heart of Sivasagar town, and there raised a complex of structures that define Assam's heritage skyline. The **Rang Ghar** — built during his reign and completed in the reign of his successor Pramatta Singha (c. 1746) — is a two-storeyed royal pavilion and one of the **oldest surviving amphitheatres in Asia**, where the kings watched buffalo fights and Bihu celebrations [1] [2]. The **Talatal Ghar** is the largest Ahom monument, famous for its seven storeys including underground tunnels used for military escape. The **Kareng Ghar** was the royal palace itself, and the surrounding royal tanks — **Rudra Sagar, Gaurisagar, Joysagar** — engineered water management alongside aesthetic grandeur [1] [2].

## Diplomacy, Religion, and Administration

Rudra Singha's ambitions extended beyond building. He cultivated diplomatic contacts with powers from Delhi to Bengal, sought to elevate the Ahom kingdom's standing among imperial courts, and reformed the kingdom's rituals to full Hindu royal form while preserving the Paik-based administration beneath [1] [2]. He patronised Vaishnavite and Shaivite institutions alike — the great **Sivadol temple** at Sivasagar, one of the tallest Shiva temples in India, belongs to his cultural milieu (its final form completed under Queen Ambika after him). His reign is routinely described as the **golden age of the Ahom kingdom** — the last great flowering before the long decline that began under weaker successors [1].

## What ADRE Asks About Rudra Singha

The exam's Rudra Singha questions cluster around four answers: the years **1696–1714**, the capital **Rangpur/Sivasagar**, the monuments **Rang Ghar and Talatal Ghar**, and the characterisation of his reign as the **golden age** [1] [2]. A trap to avoid: the Rang Ghar is sometimes attributed to Pramatta Singha, who completed it — but its construction began under Rudra Singha, and questions phrased "who built the Rang Ghar" are conventionally answered with Rudra Singha [1] [2].

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who was Rudra Singha?

The Ahom king (1696–1714, Ahom name Sukhrumpha) who moved the capital to Rangpur and built the monuments of Sivasagar, presiding over the Ahom golden age [1] [2].

### Which capital did Rudra Singha establish?

Rangpur, now the centre of Sivasagar town in upper Assam [1] [2].

### What are the famous monuments built by Rudra Singha?

The Rang Ghar (Asia's oldest surviving amphitheatre), Talatal Ghar (seven-storey structure with underground tunnels), Kareng Ghar palace, and the royal tanks of Sivasagar [1] [2].

### Which dynasty did Rudra Singha belong to?

The Tungkhungia dynasty, the last great line of Ahom kings founded by his father Gadadhar Singha [1].

### Why is Rudra Singha's reign called the golden age?

Because it combined administrative strength, monumental architecture, full adoption of Hindu royal titles, and ambitious diplomacy — the last great flourishing before the kingdom's decline [1] [2].

### What is the connection between Rudra Singha and Sivasagar?

Sivasagar is the modern name of his capital Rangpur; the town's monuments, tanks, and temples were built during and immediately after his reign [1] [2].
