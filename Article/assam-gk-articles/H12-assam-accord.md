The **Assam Accord**, signed on **15 August 1985**, is the memorandum of settlement that ended the six-year Assam Movement. Signed by the **Government of India**, the **Government of Assam**, and the student organisations **AASU and AAGSP**, it established the legal framework for detecting and deporting illegal immigrants using a three-tier date system that still governs Assam's citizenship question today [1] [2]. For ADRE, the Accord's date, signatories, and cutoff clauses are among the most-repeated questions in the entire exam — this guide covers every element.

| Fact | Detail |

| Signatories | Government of India, Government of Assam, AASU, AAGSP |
| Student signatories | Prafulla Kumar Mahanta, Bhrigu Kumar Phukan, Samujjal Kumar Bhattacharya, Ajoy Kumar Bhattacharya |

## The Problem the Accord Tried to Solve

Six years of agitation had crystallised one demand: Assam's demographic, cultural, and political character was being altered by successive waves of migration — from the Partition displacement of 1947 and the Bangladesh Liberation War of 1971 — and the electoral rolls were the battleground where the issue became visible, most dramatically at the **Mangaldoi by-election of 1979** [1] [2]. The Accord's genius — and its enduring controversy — was to convert this political demand into a **legal classification based on entry dates**, using the 1951 National Register of Citizens and the 1971 Bangladesh war as reference points [2].

## The Three-Tier Clause (The Exam's Favourite)

| Tier | Entry period | Treatment under the Accord |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Before 1 January 1966 | Regular citizens; names included in electoral rolls with full rights |
| II | 1 January 1966 to 24 March 1971 | Deleted from electoral rolls; may be restored after 10 years (later operationalised through the 1985 amendment of the Citizenship Act) |
| III | On or after 25 March 1971 | Detection and deportation in accordance with law |

The dates carry symbolism: 1971 marks the Bangladesh Liberation War, after which migration became mass-scale; 1966 was set as the generous cutoff to regularise earlier migrants who had lived in Assam for decades [2].

## Other Provisions

Beyond the foreigners clauses, the Accord promised **constitutional, legislative, and administrative safeguards** for Assam's cultural and linguistic identity, measures to speed up economic development (including the No. 7 oil refinery, now Numaligarh), and a **review of citizenship acquisition** provisions. It also paved the way for the later machinery — the updated **NRC (National Register of Citizens)** process, conducted under Supreme Court supervision and published in 2019, and the parliamentary debates that eventually produced the CAA of 2019 [1] [2].

## The Aftermath: Politics Transformed

Within two months of the signing, the movement's leaders founded the **Asom Gana Parishad (AGP) on 14 October 1985**, and in December 1985 **Prafulla Kumar Mahanta** became Chief Minister at 32 — India's youngest chief minister at the time [1] [2]. Implementation of the Accord's promises, however, stretched across decades: the **IMDT Act (1983)** — held to shield illegal immigrants — was struck down by the Supreme Court in 2005, and the NRC update consumed years of litigation. The Accord thus remains both Assam's founding political settlement and its most contested document.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When was the Assam Accord signed?

On 15 August 1985, ending the six-year Assam Movement (1979–1985) [1] [2].

### Who signed the Assam Accord?

The Government of India, the Government of Assam, and the student organisations AASU and AAGSP — the student leaders included Prafulla Kumar Mahanta and Bhrigu Kumar Phukan [1] [2].

### What are the cutoff dates in the Assam Accord?

Three tiers: entrants before 1 January 1966 (full rights), between 1 January 1966 and 24 March 1971 (removed from electoral rolls for 10 years), and after 25 March 1971 (detection and deportation) [2].

### What was the 1971 date chosen for?

25 March 1971 marks the start of the Bangladesh Liberation War, after which migration into Assam became mass-scale — hence the strictest treatment for post-1971 entrants [2].

### What happened politically after the Accord?

The Asom Gana Parishad (AGP) was formed on 14 October 1985, and Prafulla Kumar Mahanta became Chief Minister in December 1985 as India's youngest CM [1] [2].

### What is the connection between the Assam Accord and the NRC?

The Accord's demand for detecting illegal foreigners drove the decades-long process of updating the National Register of Citizens in Assam, published in 2019 [1] [2].
