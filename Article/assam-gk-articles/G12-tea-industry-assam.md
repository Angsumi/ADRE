## The Discovery: Bruce, the Botanist, and the Garden

The story begins in **1823**, when Scottish adventurer **Robert Bruce** identified the indigenous tea plant (*Camellia sinensis var. assamica*) growing wild in Assam — a discovery later confirmed botanically by **Robert Fortune**-era scholars and Nathaniel Wallich's delegation [1] [2]. Bruce's brother **Charles Bruce** pioneered cultivation with the help of **Maniram Dewan**, a powerful Assamese noble who understood both the plant and the local labour world. In **1839**, the **Assam Company** established the **Chabua garden** — India's first commercial tea estate — launching an industry that within decades would out-produce China's [1] [2]. Maniram Dewan later became the first Assamese private planter; his involvement in the 1857 uprising led to his execution, making him one of Assam's most complex colonial-era figures [2].

## The Industry Today: Scale and Structure

Assam's tea map stretches across the Brahmaputra's south bank — Golaghat, Jorhat, Sivasagar, Dibrugarh, Tinsukia, Nagaon, and Sonitpur — with over 800 estates and hundreds of thousands of workers, many descended from the indentured Adivasi labour migrations of the 19th century [1] [2]. The state specialises in **CTC processing** — crush, tear, curl — which produces the strong, malty, copper-coloured infusion that defines "Assam tea" in global blends [1]. The industry is governed through the **Tea Board of India** (Kolkata), the **Trustea/JTEA sustainability programmes**, and the GI-protected **"Assam Tea"** brand [2].

## Tocklai and the Guwahati Auction

Two institutions anchor the sector's knowledge and commerce. The **Tocklai Tea Research Centre** at Jorhat, established in **1911**, is the **world's oldest tea research institute**, responsible for the clonal varieties (TV clones) that transformed Assam's yields [1] [2]. The **Guwahati Tea Auction Centre** is the **second-largest tea auction in Asia** (after Kolkata), where Assam's output is traded to blenders worldwide [2]. Tea tourism — estate stays at Dibrugarh and Jorhat gardens — is the industry's newest chapter.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When was the first tea garden established in Assam?

Chabua in 1839 — the first commercial tea garden in India, established by the Assam Company [1] [2].

### Who discovered tea in Assam?

Robert Bruce, who identified the indigenous tea plant in 1823; cultivation was pioneered by his brother Charles Bruce with Maniram Dewan [1] [2].

### Who was Maniram Dewan?

An Assamese noble who collaborated with the Bruces on early tea cultivation, later became India's first private Assamese tea planter, and was executed in 1858 for his role in the 1857 uprising [2].

### What share of India's tea does Assam produce?

About 51% of India's output and roughly 17% of world production [1].

### What is Tocklai?

The Tocklai Tea Research Centre at Jorhat, established in 1911 — the world's oldest tea research institute [1] [2].

### What kind of tea is Assam famous for?

CTC (crush-tear-curl) black tea — malty, strong, and coppery, the backbone of global breakfast blends [1].
