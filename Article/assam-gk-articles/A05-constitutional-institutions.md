Assam's constitutional architecture mirrors India's with distinct regional features: a **unicameral legislature of 126 seats**, the **Gauhati High Court** (1948) — the only high court serving **four states** — and state-level constitutional bodies like the **Lokayukta**, **State Election Commission**, and **State Finance Commission** [1] [2]. ## The Legislature

The **Assam Legislative Assembly** has **126 directly elected members** from single-seat constituencies, and the state is **unicameral**: the Upper House (Legislative Council) existed historically but was **abolished in 1969** — the assembly's strength has since been fixed at 126, with seats reserved for SC, ST, and general categories [1] [2]. In Parliament, Assam sends **14 members to the Lok Sabha** and **7 to the Rajya Sabha** [2]. The Assembly sits in Dispur — itself famous as India's **smallest state capital** — and its legislative calendar, committee system, and question hour follow the standard state-legislature framework under Articles 168–212 [1].

## The Gauhati High Court

The **Gauhati High Court**, established on **1 September 1948** under the Government of India Act 1935, is unique: it is the only Indian high court with jurisdiction over **four states** — Assam, Nagaland, Arunachal Pradesh, and Mizoram — with permanent benches at Kohima, Itanagar, and Aizawl [1] [2]. Its first Assamese judge was **Holiram Deka**, who later rose to Chief Justice; today the court's Guwahati principal seat hears appeals from all four northeastern states and handles Assam's distinctive dockets — foreigner tribunals appeals, tribal council disputes, and Brahmaputra land litigation [1] [2].

## State Constitutional and Statutory Bodies

The **Governor** (head of state, with Raj Bhavan in Guwahati and the official residence at **Lok Bhavan**) appoints the Chief Minister and council of ministers [1] [2]. The **Lokayukta of Assam** — the anti-corruption ombudsman under the Assam Lokayukta Act — received its first appointee in **August 2023**, with an Upa-Lokayukta supporting the office [2]. The **State Election Commission** runs panchayat and municipal elections; the **State Finance Commission** periodically recommends revenue-sharing with local bodies; and the **State Human Rights Commission** investigates rights violations under the central Act [1] [2]. The **six autonomous councils** (BTC, KAAC, DHAC, Rabha Hasong, Tiwa, Deuri) operate under the Sixth Schedule and Article 244 — constitutional institutions of a different kind, covered in the [important government institutions](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-administration/government-institutions/) article.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many seats does the Assam Legislative Assembly have?

126 directly elected seats; the state is unicameral after abolishing its Legislative Council in 1969 [1] [2].

### How many parliamentary seats does Assam have?

14 Lok Sabha seats and 7 Rajya Sabha seats [2].

### When was the Gauhati High Court established?

On 1 September 1948 — with jurisdiction over four states: Assam, Nagaland, Arunachal Pradesh, and Mizoram [1] [2].

### Who was the first Assamese judge of the Gauhati High Court?

Justice Holiram Deka, who later became Chief Justice of the court [1] [2].

### What is the Lokayukta of Assam?

The state's anti-corruption ombudsman office under the Assam Lokayukta Act; its first Lokayukta was sworn in August 2023 [2].

### What are Assam's constitutional local bodies?

Panchayats (73rd Amendment) and urban local bodies (74th Amendment), supervised by the State Election Commission and State Finance Commission [1] [2].
