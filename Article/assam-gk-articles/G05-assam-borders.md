Assam's location is its defining geopolitical fact: it is the only state in India that shares boundaries with **both Bhutan and Bangladesh**, and it touches **seven Indian states** — the entire northeastern family plus West Bengal through the narrow Siliguri corridor [1] [2]. Border questions in ADRE are quick marks: the countries, the states, the border districts, and the corridors that connect the Northeast to the rest of India. This guide covers every boundary fact the exam uses.

## The International Borders

The **Bhutan border** runs along Assam's northern foothills through Kokrajhar, Chirang, Baksa, Udalguri, and Sonitpur districts — the same corridor that hosts Manas and Raimona national parks and the golden-langur sanctuaries [1] [2]. The **Bangladesh border** is the longer and more complex of the two: it curves along Assam's south and southwest through **Dhubri, Goalpara, Barpeta, Chirang, Kokrajhar** on the western arc and **Karimganj, Hailakandi, and Cachar** in the Barak Valley — the Barak river itself crossing into Bangladesh where it becomes the **Surma and Kushiyara** [1] [2]. Assam's districts therefore carry an unusual dual distinction: several touch Bangladesh, a handful touch Bhutan, and a few districts (like the river-island Majuli) touch no international boundary at all.

## The State Boundaries and the Siliguri Corridor

Every other direction is domestic. **Arunachal Pradesh** bounds Assam to the east and north, sharing the Sadiya tri-junction where the Dibang, Lohit, and Siang rivers meet the Brahmaputra. **Nagaland and Manipur** lie to the southeast, beyond the Naga hills and the Barak's upper catchment. **Mizoram and Tripura** bound the south, with the Barail range running between Assam and the Dimapur plain. **Meghalaya** wraps the west and southwest, its plateau forming the southern wall of the Brahmaputra valley — rivers like the Kopili, Kulsi, and Digaru flow north from Meghalaya into the Brahmaputra [1] [2]. To the west, the **Siliguri corridor** — the "Chicken's Neck" of West Bengal, barely 22 km wide — is the only land connection between Assam and mainland India, which is why the state's rail, road, and air links through it are strategic lifelines [1].

## Why Borders Matter for ADRE

Boundary facts connect to nearly every other Assam GK topic. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which countries share an international border with Assam?

Bhutan to the north and Bangladesh to the south and southwest — Assam is the only Indian state bordering both [1] [2].

### How many Indian states border Assam?

Seven: Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura, Meghalaya, and West Bengal [1] [2].

### Which Assam districts touch the Bangladesh border?

Dhubri, Goalpara, Barpeta, Chirang, Kokrajhar in the west, and Karimganj, Hailakandi, and Cachar in the Barak Valley [1] [2].

### Which Assam districts touch the Bhutan border?

Kokrajhar, Chirang, Baksa, Udalguri, and Sonitpur along the northern foothills [1] [2].

### What is the Siliguri corridor?

The narrow (~22 km) strip of West Bengal that is the only land connection between Assam/Northeast India and mainland India [1].

### Why is Assam's border geography strategically important?

It is India's gateway to the Northeast — connecting seven states and two countries, with the Brahmaputra and Barak river routes, national highway and rail corridors, and the Siliguri lifeline all passing through it [1] [2].
