At the top of Assam's field administration sit the **five administrative (revenue) divisions** — **Upper Assam, Middle Assam, Lower Assam, the Bodoland Territorial Region, and Barak Valley** — each supervised by a **Divisional Commissioner** and grouping several districts for coordination and revenue oversight [1] [2]. Division questions in ADRE are quick marks: the count, the names, and which districts belong to each. This guide maps the whole structure.

## The Divisional Commissioner: What the Tier Does

The **Divisional Commissioner** — a senior IAS officer — supervises the districts of the division on revenue, law-and-order coordination, and development targets, acting as the bridge between the state secretariat in Dispur and the district collectorates [1] [2]. The divisions exist mainly for **revenue and coordination** purposes (Assam's Board of Revenue tradition), unlike states with divisional courts; real executive power remains with the DCs. Assam's total — **five divisions** — is itself the standard exam answer [1] [2].

## The Bodoland Territorial Region: A Division Unlike the Others

Four divisions are conventional revenue groupings; the **Bodoland Territorial Region (BTR)** is a constitutional arrangement. Created under the Sixth Schedule and reorganised by the **Bodoland Accord of January 2020**, the BTR comprises **Kokrajhar, Chirang, Baksa, and Udalguri** and is administered by the elected **Bodoland Territorial Council (BTC)** with legislative and executive powers over listed subjects — making it the only division whose administration is council-led rather than purely commissioner-led [1] [2]. Note the overlap: **Baksa and Udalguri** appear in both Lower Assam division and the BTR.

## The Barak Valley: A Distinct Administrative and Cultural Region

The **Barak Valley** division — **Cachar, Karimganj, Hailakandi** — is geographically, linguistically (Bengali-majority), and culturally distinct from the Brahmaputra valley, and its administration centres on **Silchar** [1] [2]. The valley's district machinery additionally coordinates with the **Dima Hasao Autonomous Council** (in the neighbouring hill district) and the **Karbi Anglong Autonomous Council**, completing a patchwork where six autonomous councils operate alongside the regular divisional structure — a uniquely complex governance map among Indian states [2].

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many administrative divisions does Assam have?

Five: Upper Assam, Middle Assam, Lower Assam, Bodoland Territorial Region, and Barak Valley [1] [2].

### Who heads an administrative division?

The Divisional Commissioner — a senior IAS officer supervising revenue, coordination, and development across the division's districts [1] [2].

### Which districts form the BTR?

Kokrajhar, Chirang, Baksa, and Udalguri — administered by the elected Bodoland Territorial Council under the Sixth Schedule [1] [2].

### When was the BTR reorganised under the current Accord?

Under the Bodoland Accord of January 2020 (the fourth Bodo accord), reducing the council area from 12 to 4 districts [1] [2].

### Which division is linguistically distinct?

The Barak Valley division (Cachar, Karimganj, Hailakandi), which is Bengali-majority with Silchar as its hub [1] [2].

### Which districts belong to both Lower Assam and the BTR?

Baksa and Udalguri — they sit in Lower Assam division and within the Bodoland Territorial Region [1] [2].
