When ADRE asks about **Lachit Borphukan**, it asks about the man who stopped the Mughal Empire at the Brahmaputra. As the supreme **Borphukan** (western viceroy and commander-in-chief) of the Ahom Kingdom, he orchestrated the **Battle of Saraighat in 1671** — a naval victory over Mughal general Ram Singh I that ended Mughal expansion into Assam permanently and remains one of the finest naval battles in Indian history [1] [2]. This guide gives you his complete story, the battle details, and every exam-ready fact.

## Who Was Lachit Borphukan?

Lachit was born in **1622** into an Assamese aristocratic family loyal to the Ahom throne. He rose through the kingdom's administrative ranks — serving as *Dolakabar Barua* (supervisor of royal horses) and later *Kajal Barua* — before the Ahom king **Chakradhwaj Singha** appointed him **Borphukan**, the kingdom's highest military office on the western frontier [1] [3]. The appointment came at the worst possible moment: the Mughals, under Aurangzeb, were pressing from Bengal to reclaim the territories the Ahoms had won back after the Treaty of Ghilajharighat (1663).

## The Road to Saraighat

The war began badly for the Ahoms. In the **Battle of Alaboi (1669)**, Mughal-Rajput forces inflicted a crushing defeat on the Ahom army — one of the darkest days in Ahom history [4]. Lachit responded with a strategy of attrition: he avoided pitched battles, fortified positions along the Brahmaputra, harassed Mughal supply lines, and used naval mobility to neutralise the enemy's artillery advantage. His forces even employed the famous ruse of sending thousands of soldiers disguised to approach the Rajput units, and used geography — the river, the monsoon, and the narrow channel at Saraighat — to cancel the Mughal numbers [4] [5].

## The Battle of Saraighat (1671)

Fought in **1671** on the river near Guwahati, Saraighat was essentially a **naval battle** — a rare class of engagement in Indian history [5]. As the decisive engagement approached, Lachit fell seriously ill. Tradition records his most famous moment: when his physician advised him to rest, he reportedly rose from his bed, stood in his boat, and told his men:

> "If you want to flee, flee. The king has given you a horse to do so. The enemy's ambition is to cross the Brahmaputra. It is not for me to decide who wins, but it is my duty to die fighting for my motherland."

Fighting from his boat, the ailing commander directed the Ahom flotilla to a decisive victory; Ram Singh I retreated, and Mughal ambitions in Assam collapsed for good by **1682** [1] [4] [5]. Lachit died in **1672** at Jetai, shortly after the victory, in the prime of his service to the kingdom.

## Legacy and Remembrance

Lachit is remembered as the embodiment of Assamese courage and duty. **Lachit Divas is observed on 24 November**, his birth anniversary, across Assam with state functions. His statue stands at Saraighat on the Brahmaputra, and the Ministry of Defence awards the **Lachit Borphukan Gold Medal** every year to the best passing-out cadet of the National Defence Academy [3]. For ADRE, remember the quartet of facts the exam actually asks: 1671, Saraighat, Mughal commander Ram Singh I, and the naval nature of the battle [2] [5].

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who was Lachit Borphukan?

The Ahom Kingdom's Borphukan (supreme western commander) who defeated the Mughal army at the naval Battle of Saraighat in 1671, securing Assam's independence [1] [5].

### When was the Battle of Saraighat fought?

In 1671, on the Brahmaputra river near Guwahati, where the Ahom navy under Lachit Borphukan decisively defeated the Mughal forces of Ram Singh I [5].

### Which Mughal emperor was ruling during Saraighat?

Aurangzeb; the Mughal campaign was pushed from Bengal, and the field commander was Ram Singh I of Amber [1] [5].

### What is Lachit Divas?

24 November, the birth anniversary of Lachit Borphukan, observed across Assam as a day of remembrance for the Saraighat hero [3].

### What happened to Lachit Borphukan after Saraighat?

He died in 1672 at Jetai, shortly after the victory, reportedly from illness contracted during the campaign [3].

### Why is Lachit Borphukan important for the ADRE exam?

He is the most frequently asked Assam personality in the GK section — always with Saraighat, 1671, and the Mughal defeat — making him a guaranteed-marks topic [2] [5].
