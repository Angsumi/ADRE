**Sivasagar** — the "sea of Shiva" — is where the Ahom dynasty left its greatest physical legacy. Once called **Rangpur**, the "city of festivities", this town in upper Assam served as the capital during the kingdom's golden age and still holds the **Rang Ghar**, **Talatal Ghar**, **Kareng Ghar**, and three enormous royal tanks that together form one of the densest heritage landscapes in India [1] [2]. For ADRE, Sivasagar questions connect history and heritage in a single chapter: the monuments, the kings who built them, and the legends attached to its tanks.

## From Rangpur to Sivasagar

The city's first identity was **Rangpur**, established as the Ahom capital by **Rudra Singha (1696–1714)** after he moved it from Garhgaon [1]. His successors — Sib Singha, Pramatta Singha, and Rajeswar Singha — continued the monumental programme through the mid-18th century. The Tungkhungia kings ruled here through the kingdom's final great phase, until decline, the Moamoria Rebellion, and the Burmese wars shifted power away; the last Ahom capitals lay further east at **Jorhat** [1] [2]. The name Sivasagar today honours the great **Sivadol** temple, and the town's very geography — tank beside monument beside palace — is an open-air museum of Ahom engineering.

## The Monuments in Exam Detail

The **Rang Ghar** is a two-storeyed brick pavilion with a boat-shaped roof, where kings watched sports and Bihu; it is recognised as one of the **oldest amphitheatres in Asia** [1] [2]. The **Talatal Ghar** is the largest Ahom structure — four above-ground storeys over three underground ones, with escape tunnels connecting to the distant **Gargaon** — built for military security as much as ceremony. The **Kareng Ghar**, the seven-storeyed royal palace, anchored the civic centre, and the adjoining **Na-Ghar** was the drum house where war drums announced royal proclamations. The **Sivadol**, built by Queen **Ambika** in 1734 in memory of her husband Siva Singha, remains one of the tallest Shiva temples in India [1].

## The Three Royal Tanks and Their Legends

Sivasagar's water bodies are as exam-famous as its monuments. **Joysagar**, the largest man-made tank of the Ahom period, was excavated by **Rudra Singha** in memory of his mother **Sati Joymati** — the queen who, under torture by Laluk Borphukan for her husband Gadadhar's whereabouts, refused to betray him and died [1]. **Rudrasagar**, also by Rudra Singha, lies beside Rang Ghar and reflects the palace. **Gaurisagar** was built for **Gaurinatha**, mother of Rudra Singha. These tanks showcase the Ahom mastery of water engineering — every royal settlement paired its monuments with a tank.

## Sivasagar Today: Heritage and Tourism

The town anchors Assam's heritage-tourism circuit, with the **Charaideo Maidams** (royal burial mounds, UNESCO-recognised) nearby and the annual **Sivasagar Raas Mahotsav** drawing visitors to Rang Ghar each November. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What was Sivasagar called during the Ahom rule?

Rangpur — the "city of festivities" — the capital of the Ahom kingdom under Rudra Singha and his successors [1].

### Who built the Rang Ghar?

Construction began under Rudra Singha and was completed around 1746 during the reign of Pramatta Singha; it is among Asia's oldest surviving amphitheatres [1] [2].

### What is Talatal Ghar famous for?

Its seven storeys — four above ground and three underground — with escape tunnels, making it the largest Ahom monument [1].

### In whose memory was Joysagar tank built?

In memory of Sati Joymati, mother of Rudra Singha, who died refusing to betray her husband Gadadhar Singha under torture [1].

### Who built the Sivadol temple?

Queen Ambika in 1734, in memory of her husband Siva Singha; it is among the tallest Shiva temples in India [1].

Yes — its monuments, tanks, and builder-kings form a high-repeat cluster across the history, heritage, and Assam GK sections [1] [2].
