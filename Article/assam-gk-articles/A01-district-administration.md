District administration is where the Assam government actually meets the citizen: each of the state's **35 districts** is governed by a **Deputy Commissioner (DC)** who combines revenue, magisterial, electoral, and developmental authority in one office [1] [2]. For ADRE — especially Paper IV (current affairs and polity) — the district apparatus, its officers, and how it connects downward to panchayats and upward to the secretariat form a core question cluster. This guide explains the whole machinery.

## Quick Answer: District Administration Fact Sheet

| Office | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Deputy Commissioner (DC) | District head — revenue, magistrate, coordinator of all departments |
| Superintendent of Police (SP) | Law and order; heads district police |
| Deputy Conservator / civil surgeon | Forest and health departments at district level |
| District Development Officer (DDO) | Development schemes coordination |
| Zilla Parishad / Anchalik Panchayat | Rural local government (3 tiers: Zilla, Anchalik, Gaon Panchayat) |
| Municipal Boards / Urban Development | Urban local bodies — GMC (Guwahati), municipal boards, town committees |
| Circle officers, rangias | Sub-district field administration |

## The Deputy Commissioner: One Officer, Many Hats

The **DC** is the linchpin: as **District Magistrate**, the DC exercises executive magisterial powers (including Section 144 CrPC-type orders and disaster response under the DM Act); as **collector**, the DC supervises land revenue, land records (the **Mission Basudha** digitisation of land pattas runs through DC offices), and land acquisition; as **district election officer**, the DC runs Lok Sabha, Assembly, and Panchayat elections under the Election Commission [1] [2]. Every government department in the district — agriculture, health, education, social welfare — reports to the DC for coordination, which makes the collectorate the nerve centre of field governance.

## Sub-District and Field Administration

Below the district, Assam's field structure runs through **sub-divisions** headed by **sub-divisional officers (SDOs)** and further through **circles** administered by **circle officers**; revenue villages remain the base unit, with **rangias** (village headmen appointed under the Assam Land and Revenue Regulation) collecting revenue at the grassroots [1] [2]. In the tribal autonomous districts — **Karbi Anglong and Dima Hasao** — this machinery operates alongside (and partly through) the autonomous council governments, a Sixth Schedule arrangement unique in the plains-adjacent setting.

## Local Government: Panchayats and Urban Bodies

Rural local government follows the three-tier **Panchayati Raj** structure mandated by the 73rd Amendment: **Zilla Parishads** at district level, **Anchalik Panchayats** at the block level, and **Gaon Panchayats** at the village level — all elected bodies [1] [2]. Urban areas are governed by the **Guwahati Municipal Corporation**, eight other municipal boards (Silchar, Dibrugarh, Jorhat, Nagaon, Tezpur, Tinsukia, Dhubri, Bongaigaon), and town area committees, under the state's urban development department [2]. The state runs these bodies through the **Directorate of Panchayat & Rural Development** and the **State Election Commission**.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who heads the district administration in Assam?

The Deputy Commissioner (DC), who serves as district magistrate, revenue collector, and district election officer [1] [2].

### What is the role of the DC as District Magistrate?

Executive magisterial powers — maintaining public order, disaster management, and law-and-order coordination with the police [1] [2].

### What is the Panchayati Raj structure in Assam?

Three tiers: Zilla Parishad (district), Anchalik Panchayat (block), and Gaon Panchayat (village) — all elected under the 73rd Amendment [1] [2].

### What are the urban local bodies of Assam?

The Guwahati Municipal Corporation, eight municipal boards (Silchar, Dibrugarh, Jorhat, Nagaon, Tezpur, Tinsukia, Dhubri, Bongaigaon), and town committees [2].

### Who administers sub-divisions below the district?

Sub-Divisional Officers (SDOs), with circle officers administering revenue circles and rangias heading revenue villages [1] [2].

### How is district administration different in Karbi Anglong and Dima Hasao?

These Sixth Schedule hill districts are governed through autonomous councils (KAAC and DHAC) alongside the regular district machinery [1] [2].
