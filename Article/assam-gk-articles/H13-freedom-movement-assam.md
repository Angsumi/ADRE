Assam's role in the **freedom movement** stretches from the peasant rifles of **Patharughat (1894)** to **Gopinath Bordoloi's** statesmanship in 1947 — and produced some of India's youngest and most moving martyrs: **Kanaklata Barua**, who died at 17 carrying the Congress flag, and **Kushal Konwar**, hanged in 1943 [1] [2]. For ADRE, the Assam chapter of the independence struggle is asked both as events (Patharughat, Quit India) and as personalities (Kanaklata, Bordoloi, Tarun Ram Phookan, Chandraprabha Saikiani). This guide covers every name and date the exam uses.

## The Early Stirrings: Patharughat and the Associations

The first organised Assamese resistance to colonial policy was not political but agrarian. On **28 January 1894**, at **Patharughat** in Darrang, thousands of peasants gathered to protest the oppressive hike in land revenue (the "Dakhila" system); police firing killed scores — an event remembered as the **Patharughat Ran (peasant uprising)**, Assam's own Jallianwala-scale tragedy [1] [2]. Politically, the **Assam Association (1903)**, led by **Tarun Ram Phookan**, gave the region its first platform, later evolving into the provincial Congress organisation [2].

## The National Movements Reach Assam

Non-Cooperation (1921), Civil Disobedience (1930), and Quit India (1942) all found enthusiastic Assamese response. The **Assam Provincial Congress Committee** organised hartals, student protests, and parallel governance experiments; the **Assam Seva Sangha** spread Gandhi's constructive programme to villages; and women leaders like **Chandraprabha Saikiani** and **Pushpalata Das** carried the movement into schools, villages, and prisons [1] [2].

## The Young Martyrs: Kanaklata and Kushal

Two names carry Assam's Quit India story. **Kanaklata Barua (Borua)**, barely 17, led a procession on **20 September 1942** at **Gohpur** (Lakhimpur) to hoist the Congress flag at the police station; shot by police, she clung to the flag as she fell — one of the youngest martyrs of the national movement [1] [2]. **Kushal Konwar**, involved in the **Sarupathar railway sabotage case** (disrupting rail transport during Quit India), was arrested and **hanged in 1943**, becoming Assam's only martyr of the movement to receive the death sentence [1] [2]. **Bhogeswari Phukanani**, an octogenarian revolutionary, also died of injuries from police brutality the same year.

## Gopinath Bordoloi and the Keeping of Assam

The movement's political culmination was **Gopinath Bordoloi's** leadership. As Premier from **1946**, he faced the Muslim League's demand to merge Assam with Pakistan and the instability of Partition. His negotiations and steadfastness ensured **Assam's accession to India** in 1947 — and when the **Sylhet referendum** (July 1947) transferred most of Sylhet to East Bengal, Bordoloi secured the retention of the Karimganj region for India [2]. He is honoured as **Lokapriya** ("beloved of the people") and as the architect of modern Assam's political identity.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What was the Patharughat peasant uprising?

A 1894 (28 January) Assamese peasant protest against increased colonial land revenue, met with police firing that killed many — a landmark early resistance event [1].

### Who was Kanaklata Barua?

A 17-year-old freedom fighter who was shot dead on 20 September 1942 at Gohpur while leading a procession to hoist the Congress flag — among India's youngest martyrs [1] [2].

### Who was Kushal Konwar?

An Assamese revolutionary arrested in the Sarupathar railway sabotage case during Quit India and hanged in 1943 — Assam's only death-sentence martyr of the movement [1] [2].

### What role did Gopinath Bordoloi play in 1947?

As Premier, he kept Assam in India despite Muslim League pressure and secured the retention of Karimganj after the Sylhet referendum [2].

### When was the Assam Association founded?

In 1903, under Tarun Ram Phookan — the region's first organised political platform [2].

### Who were the women leaders of Assam's freedom movement?

Chandraprabha Saikiani, Pushpalata Das, and Bhogeswari Phukanani, among the most prominent women activists and martyrs [1] [2].
