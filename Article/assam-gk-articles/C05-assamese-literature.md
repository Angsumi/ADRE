**Assamese literature** is one of India's oldest literary traditions — beginning with the Buddhist **Charyapada** verses of the 10th–12th centuries, crowned by **Madhava Kandali's Ramayana** (the first vernacular Ramayana of India), transformed by **Sankardeva's** devotional works, modernised by the **Jonaki era** of 1889, and honoured by the first Indian **Jnanpith Award** in Assamese (1979) [1] [2]. For ADRE, literature questions span every era: the firsts, the landmark books, the societies, and the award-winners. This guide organises the entire timeline in exam-ready form.

## The Early Centuries: Charyapada and the Ramayana

The **Charyapada**, mystical Buddhist songs of the 10th–12th centuries composed between Bengal and Assam, are counted among the earliest evidence of the Assamese language — a fact that makes "Charyapada" the single most repeated literature answer in ADRE [1] [2]. **Hem Saraswati's *Prahlada Charitra*** (c. 13th–14th century) is attributed to the first Assamese poet, and **Rudra Kandali's** *Drona Parba* followed [1]. The century-defining achievement came from **Madhava Kandali**, whose **Saptakanda Ramayana** is the **first Ramayana in any Indian vernacular** — predating Tulsidas by over a century — later revised by Sankardeva [1] [2].

## The Bhakti Age: Sankardeva and Madhavdeva

**Srimanta Sankardeva** (1449–1568) transformed Assamese letters with devotional classics — the ***Kirtan Ghosa***, *Gunamala*, and his Ankiya Naat plays — while his disciple **Madhavdeva** (1489–1596) wrote the ***Namghosa***, arguably the greatest devotional poem in the language [1] [2]. Together their works standardised a literary Assamese used in worship, drama, and verse — the foundation on which everything modern was built.

## The Modern Turn: Hemkosh and Jonaki

The colonial-modern turn came in three movements. **Hemchandra Barua** compiled the ***Hemkosh*** (1900), the first Assamese dictionary — rooted in Sanskrit etymology. **Anandaram Barooah** championed the language's cause in administration and education. Then came the **Jonaki era**: in **1889**, the magazine ***Jonaki***, launched by **Chandra Kumar Agarwala, Lakshminath Bezbaruah, and Hemchandra Goswami** (the "Trimurti"), ushered in romantic modernism — the short story (*Surabhi*), humour, and the literary manifesto that made Assamese self-confident again [1] [2]. In **1917** the **Assam Sahitya Sabha** was founded at Sibsagar with **Lakshminath Bezbaruah** as its first president — the guardian institution of the language to this day [1] [2].

## The Award Era and Beyond

Assam's national literary honours are landmark answers. **Birendra Kumar Bhattacharya** won the **Sahitya Akademi Award in 1979** for *Mrityunjay* (the first Indian novel to win) and the **Jnanpith Award the same year** for *Iyaruingam* — the first Indian to hold both [1] [2]. The state's literary galaxy also includes **Jyoti Prasad Agarwala** (poet-dramatist-filmmaker of *Joymati*), **Bishnu Rabha** (revolutionary poet), **Homen Borgohain**, **Arupa Kalita Patangia**, and **Anurag Bora**, while **Jyoti Prasad's *Joymati*** (1935) marks the cinema-adjacent literary milestone [1] [2].

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the earliest known Assamese literature?

The Charyapada — Buddhist tantric devotional songs of the 10th–12th centuries, among the earliest evidence of the Assamese language [1] [2].

### Who wrote the first Assamese Ramayana?

Madhava Kandali, in the 14th century — the first Ramayana in any Indian vernacular, later revised by Sankardeva [1] [2].

### What is the Jonaki era?

The romantic modernist movement in Assamese literature inaugurated by the *Jonaki* magazine in 1889, led by Chandra Kumar Agarwala, Lakshminath Bezbaruah, and Hemchandra Goswami [1] [2].

### What is the Hemkosh?

Hemchandra Barua's Assamese dictionary published in 1900 — the first dictionary of the language [1] [2].

### When was the Assam Sahitya Sabha founded?

In 1917 at Sibsagar, with Lakshminath Bezbaruah as its first president [1] [2].

### Which Assamese writer won the Jnanpith Award?

Birendra Kumar Bhattacharya in 1979 for *Iyaruingam* — the same year his novel *Mrityunjay* won the Sahitya Akademi Award, the first by an Indian [1] [2].
