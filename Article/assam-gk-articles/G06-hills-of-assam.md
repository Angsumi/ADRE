## The Four Physiographic Divisions

Standard Assam geography divides the state into **four physiographic units** — a classic ADRE question [1] [2]. First, the **Brahmaputra valley plains**, the broad alluvial lowland between the Himalayan foothills and the Meghalaya plateau, where most of the population lives. Second, the **Barak valley plains**, the smaller southern plain of Cachar, Karimganj, and Hailakandi. Third, the **hill regions and plateaus** — the Karbi Anglong and Dima Hasao highlands — the state's only true hill country. Fourth, the **northern foothills or bhabar zone**, the porous forested fringe along the Arunachal and Bhutan borders where the parks of Nameri, Sonai Rupai, and Manas sit [1].

## The Karbi Anglong Plateau: Wedge Between Two Valleys

The **Karbi Anglong Hills** (historically the **Mikir Hills**, home of the Karbi people) form an upland mass of roughly 10,434 sq km — the largest district in Assam — that separates the Brahmaputra valley from the Barak valley [1] [2]. The plateau is isolated hydrographically: the **Kapili river** and its headstreams cut it off on the Hamren (west) side, while the **Dhansiri**, rising in the Naga hills, defines its eastern edge; the Kopili flows north through it into the Brahmaputra [1] [3]. The terrain hosts **Garampani** — one of Assam's oldest sanctuaries, famous for hot water springs — plus **Nambor** and **Marat Longri** reserves, and the district's tea-forests of Golaghat's southern frontier [2] [3].

## Dima Hasao and the Barail Range

**Dima Hasao** (formerly North Cachar Hills) is Assam's **only hill district in the southern region**, covering about 4,888 sq km — second largest in the state — and holding **Haflong, Assam's only hill station** [2] [3]. South of it runs the **Barail range**, the ancient forested barrier between Assam's Cachar plain and the Dimapur lowlands of Nagaland; the range also extends toward Manipur, framing the upper Barak catchment [1]. These hills are biodiversity vaults: the region shelters species found nowhere else in the state's plains.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the physiographic divisions of Assam?

Four: the Brahmaputra valley plains, the Barak valley plains, the hill regions and plateaus (Karbi Anglong and Dima Hasao), and the northern bhabar foothills [1] [2].

### What are the Karbi Anglong Hills?

The Mikir Hill plateau — Assam's largest district (~10,434 sq km) — lying between the Brahmaputra and Barak valleys, bounded by the Kapili and Dhansiri rivers [1] [3].

### What is Dima Hasao?

The former North Cachar Hills district — Assam's only hill district in the southern region, second largest by area, and home to Haflong, the state's only hill station [2] [3].

### Where is the Barail range?

Along southern Assam's boundary with Nagaland and toward Manipur, separating the Cachar plain from the Dimapur lowlands [1].

### Which is the only hill station of Assam?

Haflong, situated in the Dima Hasao district [3].

### Which rivers isolate the Karbi Anglong plateau?

The Kapili river on the Hamren (western) side and the Dhansiri river on the eastern (Naga hills) side [1] [3].
