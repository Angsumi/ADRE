For Assam's job seekers, the natural comparison is between the state's own **Assam Direct Recruitment (ADRE)** and the national **Staff Selection Commission (SSC)** exams — chiefly **SSC CHSL** (10+2 clerical posts like LDC and DEO) and **SSC MTS** (multi-tasking staff) [1] [2]. Both are objective, both recruit in volume, but ADRE's **domicile filter and Assam GK advantage** create a structural edge for Assam candidates that national exams simply cannot offer. This guide lays out the full comparison.

## Quick Answer: ADRE vs SSC at a Glance

| Parameter | ADRE (SLRC) | SSC (CHSL/MTS) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Level | State (Assam only) | National (all India) |
| Domicile required | Yes — born in Assam or 10-year residency | No — open to all India |
| Age limit | 18–40 (G3), 18–38 (G4) | CHSL 18–27; MTS 18–25 |
| Vacancies (Assam) | ~40,000–50,000 per round | Varies; national pool split across states |
| Exam structure | 5 objective papers × 100 marks | Tier-based: CBT I, CBT II/descriptive, skill test |
| Negative marking | 0.25 per wrong | 0.50 per wrong (Tier I) |
| Distinctive subjects | Assam GK (history, geography, culture, administration) | National GA, English, Quant, Reasoning |
| Posting | Assam only | Anywhere in India |
| Typical in-hand pay | Rs 20,000–40,000 | Rs 21,000–30,000 |

## The Domicile Advantage: ADRE's Decisive Edge

The single biggest strategic difference: **ADRE is restricted to Assam residents** — candidates must be born in Assam or document ten years of residency — which cuts the competitor pool to the state alone [1] [2]. **SSC is open to all of India**: every Assam candidate competes against the entire national applicant base for CHSL and MTS seats, and successful MTS/CHSL candidates can be posted anywhere in the country. For an Assam-resident candidate, ADRE is the **home-ground exam** — the Assam GK block (typically a third of the General Studies paper) is material that national candidates find alien [2].

## Age: ADRE Wins Decisively for Older Candidates

SSC's limits are youth gates: **CHSL closes at 27**, **MTS at 25** (with standard relaxations) [1] [2]. ADRE runs to **38–40**. A 29-year-old Assam graduate has effectively zero SSC options but a full ADRE Grade 3 eligibility window — one of the strongest reasons Assam candidates prioritise ADRE in their late twenties and thirties [1] [2].

## Syllabus Overlap and Distinct Content

The shared terrain is real: **English, quantitative aptitude, and general awareness** appear in both, so one preparation partially serves both [1] [2]. The divergence: SSC emphasises **reasoning** and national-level general awareness; ADRE replaces reasoning with **Papers III–V** (Science & Technology, Social Science, Mathematics/Assamese) and stacks its GA with **Assam-specific** content — Ahom history, geography, culture, administration, and current schemes [2]. Practically, an ADRE-focused candidate needs only a reasoning module and national-GA brush-up to sit SSC; an SSC-focused candidate needs the entire Assam GK corpus — a much larger addition — to sit ADRE.

## Pay, Posting, and Career Logic

SSC CHSL pays comparably to ADRE Grade 4 (roughly Rs 21,000–30,000 in hand), but postings are **national** — an Assam candidate may land a posting outside the state [1] [2]. ADRE guarantees **Assam postings** at Grade 4 and Grade 3 levels, with Grade 3 paying up to Rs 40,000+ — above typical SSC entry bands [1] [2]. Career-wise, ADRE Grade 3 offers departmental promotion ladders within Assam's administration; SSC offers central-government mobility and pension-architecture benefits of central service.

## Which Should You Prioritise?

If you are **under 25**, run both in parallel — the shared English-quant-GA core makes dual preparation efficient [1] [2]. If you are **25–40**, ADRE is your primary exam: SSC's age gates are closing while ADRE's remain open, and your Assam GK knowledge is a live advantage [1] [2]. Verify eligibility for both with the [eligibility checker](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/eligibility-checker/), build the Assam GK core through the [history](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/history/) and [geography](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/geography/) hubs, cover the national-GA gap from the [current affairs](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/current-affairs/) section, and drill mixed papers on the [mock test](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/mock-test/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is ADRE better for Assam residents?

Domicile restriction shrinks the competition to Assam alone, Assam GK gives locals a content advantage, and postings are guaranteed within the state [1] [2].

### What are SSC's age limits?

CHSL: 18–27 years; MTS: 18–25 years, with standard category relaxations — far narrower than ADRE's 38–40 [1] [2].

### Is the syllabus similar?

Partially — English, quantitative aptitude, and general awareness overlap; SSC adds reasoning, ADRE adds Science, Social Science, Math/Assamese, and Assam GK [1] [2].

### Is there negative marking in both?

Yes — ADRE deducts 0.25 per wrong answer; SSC Tier I deducts 0.50 per wrong answer [1] [2].

### Can I prepare for both together?

Yes — one English-quant-GA core covers most of both; add reasoning for SSC and the Assam GK corpus for ADRE [1] [2].

### Which guarantees Assam posting?

ADRE — all posts are in Assam departments; SSC posts candidates anywhere in India [1] [2].
