The single most common question among Assam Direct Recruitment candidates is simple: **Grade 3 or Grade 4 — which should I apply for?** The answer decides your preparation depth, your pay, and your career ceiling. **Grade 3** covers graduate-level clerical, technical, and executive posts at Pay Levels 5–8, while **Grade 4** covers HSLC/HS-level support and service posts at Pay Levels 2–4 [1] [2]. Both are conducted by the **State Level Recruitment Commission (SLRC)** under the same notification, but they differ in nearly every measurable way. This guide compares them head to head.

## Quick Answer: Grade 3 vs Grade 4 at a Glance

| Parameter | Grade 3 | Grade 4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Education | Bachelor's degree (some posts need specific streams or computer skills) | HSLC / HS pass / honours (post-dependent) |
| Age limit | 18–40 years | 18–38 years |
| Pay levels | Levels 5–8 (entry basic Rs 22,700–35,400) | Levels 2–4 (entry basic Rs 14,000–25,500) |
| Typical in-hand salary | Rs 30,000–40,000+ | Rs 20,000–27,000 |
| Representative posts | Junior Assistant, Accounts Junior, LDC, technical assistants | Junior Grade Assistant, Driver, Peon/Class IV, support staff |
| Exam papers | Papers I–V, all five; professional/skill test for some posts | Papers I–V; medical test for driver posts |
| Competition | Higher (graduate pool) | Very high volume of applicants, lower cutoffs |

## The Core Difference: Education and Post Type

**Grade 3** is the graduate track: every post requires a **bachelor's degree**, with certain posts specifying commerce, science, arts, or computer literacy as an additional filter [1] [2]. Posts include **Junior Assistant**, **Accounts Junior**, **Lower Division Assistant**, and various technical and specialist assistants across departments. **Grade 4** is the school-level track: posts require **HSLC, HS pass, or an honours pass** depending on the post — Driver (honours), Junior Grade Assistant (HSLC/HS), and Class IV service posts — plus a valid driving licence where applicable [1] [2].

## Pay and Career Progression

Under Assam's revised pay matrix, **Grade 3 posts sit at Pay Levels 5–8**, with entry basics around **Rs 22,700–35,400** plus dearness allowance, house rent, and transport — putting in-hand salaries typically in the **Rs 30,000–40,000** band [1] [2]. **Grade 4 posts sit at Levels 2–4**, with entry basics from **Rs 14,000 to Rs 25,500** and in-hand salaries around **Rs 20,000–27,000** [1] [2]. Promotion pathways differ accordingly: Grade 3 clerical cadres promote into senior clerical and supervisory gazetted-support roles, while Grade 4 cadres promote within the support hierarchy — many Grade 4 employees later qualify for Grade 3 through **departmental promotion exams**, which is why Grade 4 is often called the "foot-in-the-door" grade.

## The Exam Itself

Both grades write the same **five-paper objective structure** — Paper I (English), Paper II (General Studies, with heavy [Assam GK](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/) weight), Paper III (Science & Technology), Paper IV (Social Science), and Paper V (Mathematics or Assamese, post-dependent) — each of 100 marks, with **0.25 negative marking** [1] [2]. The difference is calibration: Grade 3 papers demand graduate-level depth (especially Papers III–V), while Grade 4 papers are set at the HS level. Certain Grade 3 posts additionally require a **professional qualifying test** (typing or computer skills), and driver posts in Grade 4 add a **medical fitness test** [1] [2].

## Which Grade Should You Apply For?

Apply for **Grade 3** if you are a recent graduate under 35 with strong English and subject knowledge — the pay gap and promotion trajectory justify the harder competition. Apply for **Grade 4** if you are a school-pass candidate, above 35 (Grade 4's 38 limit still covers you when Grade 3's 40 limit is exhausted by graduates), or seeking a first government job with departmental-promotion ambitions [1] [2]. Note that SLRC permits **multiple applications**: candidates commonly apply to both grades in the same cycle and let their prelims score decide. Use the [Grade 3](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/grade-3/) and [Grade 4](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/grade-4/) detail pages for post lists, check your fit with the [eligibility checker](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/eligibility-checker/), and test yourself on the [mock test](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/mock-test/) before applying.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the main difference between ADRE Grade 3 and Grade 4?

Education and pay: Grade 3 requires a bachelor's degree and pays at Levels 5–8; Grade 4 requires HSLC/HS and pays at Levels 2–4 [1] [2].

### Which grade pays more?

Grade 3 — entry basics of Rs 22,700–35,400 versus Grade 4's Rs 14,000–25,500, translating to roughly Rs 10,000 more per month in hand [1] [2].

### Are the age limits different?

Yes — Grade 3 is 18–40 years; Grade 4 is 18–38 years, both with standard relaxations for SC/ST/OBC/MOBC, PwD, and ex-servicemen [1] [2].

### Is the exam the same for both grades?

Same five-paper structure and marking scheme, but Grade 3 papers are calibrated to graduate level and Grade 4 papers to HS level; some Grade 3 posts add a typing/computer qualifying test [1] [2].

### Can I apply for both grades?

Yes — SLRC allows applications to multiple grades in the same recruitment cycle [1] [2].

### Can Grade 4 employees become Grade 3?

Yes, through departmental promotion examinations conducted after joining service [1] [2].
