Every serious Assam job-seeker eventually faces the same fork in the road: the **civilian clerical-administrative track (ADRE, run by the SLRC)** or the **police track (constable and equivalent posts, run by the SLPRB)**. Both recruit in lakhs, both test overlapping subjects, but they differ in conducting authority, physical requirements, pay structure, and daily work life [1] [2]. This guide compares them directly so you can plan a dual strategy or commit to one.

## Quick Answer: ADRE vs Assam Police at a Glance

| Parameter | ADRE (SLRC) | Assam Police (SLPRB) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conducting body | State Level Recruitment Commission | State Level Police Recruitment Board |
| Posts | Grade 3/4 clerical, technical, support posts | Constable, driver, band, equivalent police posts |
| Age limit | 18–40 (G3), 18–38 (G4) | 18–31 years (constable) |
| Physical test | Medical only (driver posts) | PET mandatory: 1.6 km run, height and chest standards |
| Written exam | 5 papers × 100 marks, objective, 0.25 negative marking | Written: 100 MCQs, 50 marks, +0.5 per correct, no negative marking |
| Subjects | English, GS (heavy Assam GK), Science, Social Science, Math/Assamese | English/Assamese, GK incl. Assam, Arithmetic, Reasoning |
| In-hand pay | ~Rs 20,000–40,000 (post-dependent) | ~Rs 23,000–28,000 plus risk/field allowances |
| Nature of work | Office, departmental, daytime | Field duty, shifts, law enforcement |

## Conducting Bodies and Notifications

**ADRE** is conducted by the **State Level Recruitment Commission (SLRC)** under the SLRC Act, 2012, in large multi-lakh notifications (roughly 40,000–50,000 posts per round: ~40,000 in ADRE 1.0 of 2021, ~50,000 in 2.0 of 2023–24, and around 41,000 in 3.0 of 2025) [1] [2]. **Assam Police** recruitment is conducted by the separate **State Level Police Recruitment Board (SLPRB)** — a dedicated board for police posts — with notifications typically for several thousand constables at a time [2]. The two boards run **independent timelines**: a candidate can pursue both simultaneously without conflict.

## The Physical Divide

The starkest difference is physical. **ADRE** has **no physical efficiency test** — only a medical fitness check for driver and certain service posts [1] [2]. **Assam Police** mandates the full physical pipeline: height and chest measurements (constable standards differ for general, SC/ST/OBC, and hill candidates), the **physical endurance test** (1.6 km run within a time limit), and a medical board [2]. If you clear running standards comfortably, police adds a qualifying stage you control; if not, ADRE removes that variable entirely.

## Written Exam Structure

ADRE's five-paper, 500-mark structure with **0.25 negative marking** tests English, General Studies (with a dominant Assam GK block), Science, Social Science, and Mathematics/Assamese [1] [2]. The police written exam is shorter and kinder to guessers: **100 multiple-choice questions carrying 50 marks** (+0.5 per correct, **no negative marking**) across English/Assamese, General Knowledge including Assam, Arithmetic, and Reasoning [2]. The subject overlap is substantial — Assam GK, English, and arithmetic serve both exams — which is why candidates commonly prepare once and apply twice.

## Pay, Work Life, and Career

Police constables enter around **Rs 23,000–28,000** in hand, with additional risk, uniform, and field allowances, plus a structured IPS-track promotion ladder (constable → ASI → SI → Inspector) [2]. ADRE Grade 4 pays similarly (~Rs 20,000–27,000), while **ADRE Grade 3 reaches Rs 30,000–40,000+** — the highest civilian non-gazetted band — with clerical promotion ladders toward senior clerical roles [1] [2]. Work life splits accordingly: police means shifts, field duty, and law-enforcement stress; ADRE means office hours, departments, and administrative routine [1] [2].

## Which Should You Choose?

Choose **ADRE** if you want office work, are near the upper age limit (ADRE Grade 3 runs to 40 — police runs to 31), or prefer exam-only selection [1] [2]. Choose **police** if you are under 30, physically fit, and want allowances plus a disciplined uniformed career [2]. The smartest move, statistically, is **both**: the syllabus overlap is large enough that one preparation covers roughly 70% of both exams — the Assam GK core, English, and arithmetic are identical terrain. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who conducts ADRE and who conducts police recruitment?

ADRE is conducted by the State Level Recruitment Commission (SLRC); police recruitment by the State Level Police Recruitment Board (SLPRB) [1] [2].

### What is the age limit difference?

ADRE Grade 3 allows up to 40 and Grade 4 up to 38; Assam Police constable recruitment caps at 31 — a decisive difference for older candidates [1] [2].

### Does ADRE have a physical test?

No — only a medical check for driver and certain service posts; Assam Police requires a full PET with running, height, and chest standards [1] [2].

### Is there negative marking in the police exam?

No — the police written exam carries +0.5 per correct answer with no negative marking; ADRE deducts 0.25 per wrong answer [1] [2].

### Can I prepare for both exams together?

Yes — the overlap (Assam GK, English, arithmetic) covers roughly 70% of both syllabi; police adds reasoning, ADRE adds science, social science, and deeper Assam GK [1] [2].

### Which pays more?

ADRE Grade 3 (Rs 30,000–40,000+) beats police constable (~Rs 23,000–28,000 plus allowances); ADRE Grade 4 and constable pay are comparable [1] [2].
