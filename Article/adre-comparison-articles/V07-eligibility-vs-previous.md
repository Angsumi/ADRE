Between **ADRE 1.0 (2021)**, **ADRE 2.0 (2023–24)**, and **ADRE 3.0 (2025)**, the headline eligibility parameters — age bands, education thresholds, domicile logic — have stayed remarkably stable, while the **documentation, digitisation, and post-specific conditions** have tightened noticeably [1] [2]. Candidates returning for a second attempt, or comparing older notifications, need to know exactly what changed and what did not. This guide breaks it down.

## Quick Answer: Eligibility Across the Three Rounds

| Parameter | ADRE 1.0 (2021) | ADRE 2.0 (2023–24) | ADRE 3.0 (2025) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grade 3 age | 18–40 | 18–40 | 18–40 |
| Grade 4 age | 18–38 | 18–38 | 18–38 |
| Grade 3 education | Bachelor's degree | Bachelor's degree | Bachelor's degree + computer literacy for many posts |
| Grade 4 education | HSLC / HS / honours (post-wise) | HSLC / HS / honours (post-wise) | Same, post-wise |
| Domicile proof | Born in Assam or residency documents | Born in Assam, or land records, or 10-year residency | Same three routes, stricter verification |
| Relaxations | SC/ST/OBC/MOBC/PwD/ESM standard | Same, ESM up to 45 in some posts | Same framework |
| Application mode | Online via SLRC portal | Online via SLRC portal | Online only, Aadhaar seeding mandatory |
| Application fee | ~Rs 272 (G4) / ~Rs 472 (G3) incl. charges | Rs 272 (G4) / Rs 472 (G3) | Similar, unified fee structure |

## What Has Not Changed: The Stable Core

The **age architecture** is constant across all three rounds: **18–40 for Grade 3** and **18–38 for Grade 4**, measured against the notification's cut-off date, with standard relaxations for SC, ST, OBC/MOBC, PwD, and ex-servicemen (ESM relaxation extending the ceiling in several posts) [1] [2]. The **education thresholds** are equally stable: a **bachelor's degree** for every Grade 3 post and **HSLC/HS/honours depending on the post** for Grade 4 (driver posts requiring an honours pass plus a valid licence) [1] [2]. The **domicile principle** — Assam-born candidates eligible by right, others eligible through documented residency — has been the consistent gatekeeper since 1.0 [1].

## What Changed: Documentation and Digitisation

The shifts are procedural but consequential. **ADRE 2.0 formalised the three domicile routes** — birth in Assam, ancestral land records, or ten years of continuous residency with supporting certificates — replacing 1.0's looser documentary practice, and **ADRE 3.0 tightened verification** of the same routes through Aadhaar-linked checks [1] [2]. **Aadhaar seeding became mandatory** in 3.0, and the application moved to a fully unified portal with consolidated post codes, eliminating 1.0/2.0-era ambiguities in post selection [2]. The **computer-literacy condition** — self-declared and sometimes tested — gained prominence in 3.0 for graduate posts, reflecting the digitised nature of modern clerical work [1] [2].

## Application Fees and Attempts

Fees stabilised at approximately **Rs 272 for Grade 4 and Rs 472 for Grade 3** (inclusive of bank charges) from 2.0 onward, with women and certain category candidates eligible for concessions as notified [1] [2]. There is **no attempt limit** in any round — a candidate rejected or absent in 1.0 or 2.0 retains full eligibility for 3.0 and beyond, provided age and documents hold [2].

## What This Means for You

If you were eligible in 2.0, you are almost certainly eligible in 3.0/2026 cycles — the checklist to update is documentation, not qualification [1] [2]. Before applying: confirm your **domicile route** (birth certificate, land records, or residency proof), ensure your **Aadhaar matches your educational certificates** name-wise, verify the **cut-off date age calculation**, and check post-specific annexures for computer or licence conditions. Run your profile through the [eligibility checker](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/eligibility-checker/), review the post lists on the [Grade 3](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/grade-3/) and [Grade 4](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/grade-4/) pages, and confirm the current notification on the [exam date](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/exam-date/) page before submitting.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Did the age limits change between ADRE rounds?

No — Grade 3 has remained 18–40 and Grade 4 18–38 across 1.0, 2.0, and 3.0, with standard category relaxations [1] [2].

### What domicile proof does ADRE accept?

Three routes: birth in Assam, ancestral land records in Assam, or ten years of continuous residency with supporting documents [1] [2].

### What changed in ADRE 3.0 eligibility?

Documentation tightening — Aadhaar seeding, unified portal, consolidated post codes, and stronger domicile verification — not the age or education rules [1] [2].

### Is computer knowledge now required?

For many Grade 3 graduate posts, yes — computer literacy is a declared requirement and is sometimes tested as a qualifying skill [1] [2].

### Is there an attempt limit?

No — ADRE has no attempt limit; candidates can apply in every round while within the age ceiling [1] [2].

### What are the application fees?

Approximately Rs 272 for Grade 4 and Rs 472 for Grade 3 (inclusive of bank charges), stable since 2.0, with notified concessions [1] [2].
