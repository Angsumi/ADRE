**ADRE** and the **APSC Combined Competitive Examination (CCE)** represent the two ends of Assam's government job spectrum: mass recruitment into **non-gazetted Grade 3/4 posts** (SLRC) versus selection into the **gazetted state civil services** (ACS-rank officers via APSC) [1] [2]. Candidates often wonder whether to treat them as alternatives or as a ladder — the honest answer is both. This guide compares every dimension.

## Quick Answer: ADRE vs APSC at a Glance

| Parameter | ADRE (SLRC) | APSC CCE |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conducting body | State Level Recruitment Commission (SLRC) | Assam Public Service Commission (APSC) |
| Posts | Grade 3/4 non-gazetted clerical, technical, support | ACS, AAS, AFS — gazetted state civil services |
| Selection stages | Prelims + Mains + skill/medical tests | Prelims (400) + Mains (1500) + Interview (180) |
| Exam type | Objective MCQ, 5 papers × 100 marks | Prelims objective; Mains descriptive (essay, GS, Ethics, Assam paper) |
| Negative marking | 0.25 per wrong answer | Prelims 0.33 per wrong answer |
| Education | Bachelor's (G3) or HSLC/HS (G4) | Bachelor's degree |
| Age limit | 18–40 (G3), 18–38 (G4) | 21–38 years |
| In-hand pay | Rs 20,000–40,000+ | Rs 56,000+ (Level 10+, ACS) |
| Frequency | Large rounds every 2–3 years (~40–50k posts) | Every 2–3 years (~50–150 posts) |

## The Posts and the Pay Ceiling

APSC's CCE feeds Assam's **gazetted officer cadre** — the Assam Civil Service (ACS), Assam Administrative Service, and Assam Financial Service — entry pay at **Level 10 (Rs 56,100 basic)** with authority over districts, sub-divisions, and departments [1] [2]. ADRE feeds the **non-gazetted workforce**: Junior Assistants, Accounts Juniors, and support staff at Levels 2–8 [2]. The pay gap is structural, but so is the vacancy gap: APSC recruits **dozens to low hundreds** per cycle, ADRE recruits **tens of thousands** — making ADRE the realistic first government job for most candidates and APSC the long-game upgrade.

## Pattern and Stages

ADRE's selection runs **Prelims + Mains** across five objective papers (English, GS with heavy Assam GK, Science, Social Science, Mathematics/Assamese), each 100 marks, with 0.25 negative marking, followed by skill or medical tests for specific posts [1] [2]. APSC's CCE runs **three stages**: Prelims (two objective papers, 200 + 200 marks), Mains (six descriptive papers of 250 marks each — essay, three GS papers, Ethics, and an Assam-specific paper where **30–35% of GS questions compulsorily relate to Assam**), and a **180-mark interview** [1] [2]. Descriptive answer-writing, not MCQ speed, is the APSC skill; the entire ADRE skill set transfers to APSC Prelims — but not to Mains without separate preparation.

## Eligibility and Attempts

Both require a **bachelor's degree** (ADRE Grade 4 relaxes to HSLC/HS) [1] [2]. ADRE's upper age limit of **40** is more forgiving than APSC's **38**, and APSC adds attempt-limit and category-specific constraints that ADRE does not impose [1] [2]. Preparation timelines differ too: ADRE rewards **3–6 months** of focused study; APSC CCE typically demands **1–2 years** of sustained effort across prelims, mains, and interview skills [1].

## The Smart Ladder Strategy

Because ADRE and APSC share the **Assam GK core** — history, geography, culture, administration, and current affairs — a candidate who masters the ADRE syllabus has already built 60–70% of the APSC Prelims foundation [1] [2]. The practical ladder: **take ADRE first** (higher probability, sooner salary, no interview risk), then pursue APSC from inside the system, where study time and departmental knowledge compound your chances. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the main difference between ADRE and APSC?

Posts and stature: ADRE recruits non-gazetted Grade 3/4 staff in lakhs; APSC CCE recruits gazetted ACS-rank officers in dozens [1] [2].

### Which exam is easier?

ADRE — objective papers, 3–6 months preparation, no interview. APSC CCE adds descriptive Mains and a 180-mark interview over 1–2 years of preparation [1] [2].

### Is Assam GK useful for both?

Absolutely — it dominates ADRE's GS paper and is compulsory for 30–35% of APSC Mains GS questions; it is the shared core of both exams [1] [2].

### Can I apply for both?

Yes — they are run by different commissions (SLRC and APSC) with independent notifications [1] [2].

### Which has the better age limit?

ADRE Grade 3 allows up to 40 years versus APSC's 38 — marginally more forgiving for older graduates [1] [2].

### What is the pay difference?

APSC ACS officers start at Level 10 (Rs 56,100 basic, Rs 70,000+ in hand); ADRE Grade 3 tops out around Rs 30,000–40,000 in hand [1] [2].
