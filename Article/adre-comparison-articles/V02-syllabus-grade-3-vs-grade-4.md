The **ADRE syllabus** is officially the same five-paper framework for both grades — but the **calibration is not**. Grade 3 papers are set at **graduate depth**, Grade 4 papers at **HS depth**, and the topics that expand or contract between the two are precisely predictable [1] [2]. If you are preparing for both (as most candidates should), knowing exactly where the depth shifts saves you dozens of study hours. This guide walks through each paper.

## Quick Answer: Syllabus Comparison Table

| Paper | Subject | Grade 3 depth | Grade 4 depth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | English | Graduate comprehension, precise grammar, formal letter | HS-level comprehension, basic grammar |
| II | General Studies | Heavy Assam GK + national current affairs depth | Core Assam GK + basic India facts |
| III | Science & Technology | 10th–12th + graduate science, applied tech | Class 10-level general science |
| IV | Social Science | Graduate-level history, geography, polity, economics | HS-level history, geography, civics |
| V | Mathematics / Assamese | Post-dependent: higher math or literary Assamese | Basic arithmetic or HS Assamese |
| Extra | Professional test | Typing/computer skill for some posts | Medical test for driver posts |

## Paper I: English

The paper's skeleton — comprehension, grammar, vocabulary, writing — is identical, but Grade 3 passages are longer and more analytical, vocabulary questions reach C1-level (phrases, idioms, formal registers), and writing tasks expect structured formal letters [1] [2]. Grade 4 stays at B1–B2 level: straightforward comprehension, tenses, prepositions, and simple paragraph writing. The practical implication: **Grade 3 candidates must read editorials daily; Grade 4 candidates need solid grammar drills**.

## Paper II: General Studies — Where Assam GK Dominates Both

General Studies carries the heaviest **Assam GK** weight in the entire exam for both grades — Assam history ([Ahom Kingdom](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/history/), [Lachit Borphukan](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/history/lachit-borphukan/)), geography ([rivers](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/geography/rivers-of-assam/), [districts](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/geography/districts-of-assam/), [national parks](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/geography/national-parks-of-assam/)), culture ([Bihu](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/culture/bihu/)), administration, and [government schemes](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-administration/government-schemes/) routinely make up the largest single question block [1] [2]. The difference is breadth: Grade 3 adds deeper national current affairs, Indian polity and economics at graduate level, while Grade 4 keeps India-general questions at HS textbook depth. **Our data shows roughly 35–40% of GS questions are Assam-specific in both grades** — the Assam GK core is non-negotiable for everyone.

## Papers III and IV: The Depth Gap Is Here

This is where the syllabus genuinely diverges. **Grade 3 Science & Technology** covers class 10–12 physics, chemistry, and biology plus applied technology (IT, space, environment, health-tech); **Grade 4** covers class-10 general science only [1] [2]. **Grade 3 Social Science** reaches graduate history (medieval India, Assam's full dynastic arc), analytical geography, Indian polity and economics; **Grade 4** stays at HS history-geography-civics [1] [2]. Grade 4 candidates can safely skip NCERT class 11–12 depth; Grade 3 candidates cannot.

## Paper V and the Extras

Paper V adapts to the post: mathematics-heavy posts get arithmetic and algebra, language posts get Assamese literature and grammar. Grade 3 math reaches class 12 level (algebra, geometry, data interpretation); Grade 4 math is commercial arithmetic (percentages, ratios, profit-loss, time-work) [1] [2]. Additionally, several **Grade 3 posts require a professional qualifying test** (typing at 35 wpm English or 30 wpm Assamese, or computer skills), while **Grade 4 driver posts add a medical fitness test** — check the post-specific annexure before planning [1] [2].

## How to Prepare When Targeting Both Grades

Study to **Grade 3 depth** and you automatically cover Grade 4 — the reverse is not true [1] [2]. Build the [Assam GK core](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/) first (it is the common denominator), then layer graduate depth for Papers III–V only if applying to Grade 3. Use the full [ADRE syllabus](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/syllabus/) page, practise both grade levels on the [mock test](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/mock-test/), review [previous year question papers](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/previous-year-question-paper/), and track both preparations in the [study planner](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/study-planner/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are the syllabi officially different for Grade 3 and Grade 4?

The five-paper framework is the same; only the difficulty calibration differs — graduate depth for Grade 3, HS depth for Grade 4 [1] [2].

### Which paper differs the most between the grades?

Papers III (Science) and IV (Social Science) — Grade 3 requires class 11–12 and graduate depth; Grade 4 requires class 10–HS depth [1] [2].

### How much of the exam is Assam GK?

Roughly 35–40% of General Studies questions across both grades are Assam-specific, making Assam GK the single highest-yield topic [1] [2].

### Does Grade 3 have any extra test?

Yes — certain Grade 3 posts add a professional qualifying test (typing or computer skills) [1] [2].

### Can I prepare once and sit both grades?

Yes — preparing to Grade 3 depth covers Grade 4 entirely; you only need to add the professional test skill for specific Grade 3 posts [1] [2].

### What level of English is needed for Grade 4?

HS level (B1–B2): straightforward comprehension, tenses, prepositions, and simple paragraph writing [1] [2].
