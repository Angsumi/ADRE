**Author:** AxomRank | **Updated:** August 2026 | **Reading time:** 9 minutes

Assam's direct recruitment has evolved through four distinct regimes — the pre-ADRE SEPA exams (up to ~2015), **ADRE 1.0 (2021)**, **ADRE 2.0 (2023–24)**, and **ADRE 3.0 (2025)** — and each changed the exam pattern materially [1] [2]. Candidates who study old question papers risk preparing for a pattern that no longer exists. This guide traces every change so you know exactly what ADRE 3.0 demands and why.

## Quick Answer: Pattern Evolution at a Glance

| Regime | Structure | Marks | Negative marking | Vacancies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-ADRE (up to ~2015) | Single written paper (GS + language + post-specific) | 100–150 marks | Varies by notification | Smaller batches |
| ADRE 1.0 (2021) | Single written test + professional qualifying test (Grade 3) | 150 marks (100 GS/English + 50 professional) | 0.25 per wrong | ~40,000 posts |
| ADRE 2.0 (2023–24) | Prelims + Mains, five papers | 5 papers × 100 = 500 marks | 0.25 per wrong | ~50,000 posts |
| ADRE 3.0 (2025) | Prelims + Mains, five papers (refined) | 5 papers × 100 = 500 marks | 0.25 per wrong | ~41,000 posts |

## Pre-ADRE: The Single-Paper Era

Before the SLRC era consolidated recruitment, Assam's Grade 3/4 exams were conducted by SEPA as **single written papers** mixing General Studies, English/Assamese, and post-specific content, typically worth 100–150 marks with interview or skill tests for select posts [1] [2]. The pattern was simple but inconsistent between notifications, and vacancy batches were small. The creation of the **State Level Recruitment Commission (SLRC Act, 2012)** — used at scale from ADRE 1.0 onward — professionalised and standardised the process [2].

## ADRE 1.0 (2021): The Transition Pattern

ADRE 1.0 introduced the modern framework but kept a **single written test**: 100 marks of General Studies and English plus a **50-mark professional qualifying test** for Grade 3 posts (typing at 35 wpm English / 30 wpm Assamese, or computer skills), with Grade 4 having no professional test [1] [2]. Negative marking arrived at **0.25 per wrong answer** — the same rate retained ever since [1] [2]. Roughly **40,000 posts** were recruited in this round.

## ADRE 2.0 (2023–24): The Five-Paper Revolution

ADRE 2.0 replaced the single test with the **Prelims + Mains, five-paper structure** that defines the exam today: **Paper I (English), Paper II (General Studies), Paper III (Science & Technology), Paper IV (Social Science), Paper V (Mathematics or Assamese, post-dependent)** — each 100 objective marks, 500 marks total, 0.25 negative marking, plus skill or medical tests for specific posts [1] [2]. The logic: separating subjects allows fairer calibration between Grade 3 (graduate depth) and Grade 4 (HS depth), and the five-paper design pushed roughly **50,000 vacancies** through the largest recruitment in the state's history [1] [2].

## ADRE 3.0 (2025): Refinement, Not Reinvention

ADRE 3.0 retained the five-paper architecture but refined the machinery: online-only applications through the SLRC portal with **Aadhaar seeding**, consolidated post codes, stricter document verification (domicile proof via birth-in-Assam, land records, or ten-year residency), and expanded **computer-literacy requirements** for graduate posts [1] [2]. The vacancy count settled around **41,000 posts**. The syllabus and marking scheme — including the dominant **Assam GK weight in Paper II** — remained consistent with 2.0, meaning **2.0-era preparation is directly applicable to 3.0 and beyond** [1] [2].

## What This Means for Your Preparation

Three practical conclusions follow from the pattern history. First, **ignore pre-2021 question banks for pattern practice** — the single-paper format is obsolete; only ADRE 2.0/3.0 papers reflect today's structure [1] [2]. Second, **the five papers reward subject-separated preparation**, which is exactly how the [30-day](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/30-day-study-plan/), [60-day](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/60-day-study-plan/), and [90-day study plans](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/90-day-study-plan/) phase their schedules. Third, the stable 0.25 negative marking and consistent **Assam GK core** make [previous year question papers](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/previous-year-question-paper/) from 2.0 genuinely predictive — drill them on the [mock test](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/mock-test/) and track progress in the [study planner](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/study-planner/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is ADRE 3.0 different from ADRE 2.0?

The exam structure is the same (Prelims + Mains, five papers × 100 marks); changes are procedural — Aadhaar seeding, consolidated post codes, and stricter domicile verification [1] [2].

### What changed from ADRE 1.0 to 2.0?

The biggest change in Assam recruitment history: a single 150-mark written test was replaced by a 500-mark, five-paper Prelims + Mains structure [1] [2].

### Was there negative marking in older exams?

ADRE 1.0 introduced the current 0.25-per-wrong-answer scheme; pre-ADRE exams varied by notification [1] [2].

### Are old question papers still useful?

Pre-2021 papers only for subject practice; for pattern practice, use only ADRE 2.0/3.0 papers, which reflect the current structure [1] [2].

### How many vacancies were recruited in each round?

Approximately 40,000 (ADRE 1.0, 2021), 50,000 (ADRE 2.0, 2023–24), and 41,000 (ADRE 3.0, 2025) [1] [2].

### Is the syllabus stable now?

Yes — the five-paper syllabus with heavy Assam GK weight has been consistent since 2.0, making 2.0/3.0 preparation directly reusable [1] [2].
