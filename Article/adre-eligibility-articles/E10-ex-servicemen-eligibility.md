**Ex-servicemen** — retired or released defence personnel — occupy a special place in **ADRE eligibility**, with age relaxation calculated on their **actual period of military service plus three years**, and in many posts an effective ceiling extending to **50 years** [1] [2]. For Assam's substantial veteran population, this makes ADRE one of the most accessible post-service career paths in the country. This guide explains the rules.

## Quick Answer: Ex-Servicemen Rules in ADRE

| Element | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Definition | Retired/released defence personnel (armed forces, paramilitary as notified) |
| Age relaxation (Group B/C non-gazetted) | Period of military service + 3 years |
| Effective ceiling | Varies by service length; up to ~50 years for long-serving personnel |
| Disabled defence personnel | Up to 45–50 years depending on the post |
| Proof | Discharge book / service records / pension documents |
| Domicile | Same Assam rules apply (birth, land records, or 10-year residency) |
| Education | Same grade requirements (bachelor's for Grade 3; HSLC/HS for Grade 4) |

## Who Qualifies as Ex-Servicemen

Assam's recruitment norms define ex-servicemen as personnel **retired, released, or discharged from the armed forces** (including, where notified, specified paramilitary forces) who meet the service-duration thresholds set out in the notification annexure [1] [2]. The definition matters: short-service commission officers and those discharged for disciplinary reasons may face different treatment, so the annexure's precise wording governs each round.

## The Relaxation Formula

The headline concession for Group B and C (non-gazetted) posts — which is precisely what ADRE recruits — is **period of military service plus three years** deducted from the age calculation [1] [2]. A soldier with 15 years of service applying to Grade 3 effectively gets a ceiling of 40 + 15 + 3 = **58 minus the service years already elapsed**, translating in practice to application windows far beyond the standard 40. **Disabled defence service personnel** receive fixed ceilings of up to **45–50 years** depending on the post, recognising service-related disability [2].

## Documentation: The Discharge Book Is Everything

The **discharge book (service record)** is the non-negotiable proof of ex-servicemen status — along with pension documents, release orders, and medical boarding papers where disability claims attach [1] [2]. Candidates should obtain attested copies from their unit or records office early; missing service records are the most common cause of ESM claim failure in state recruitments. The certificate must match the name on the educational and age-proof documents (the HSLC certificate remains the age baseline).

## Combining Ex-Servicemen Status with Other Criteria

Ex-servicemen must still satisfy the **domicile requirement** — Assam birth, land records, or ten-year residency — and the **educational bar** of their chosen grade; the ESM concession applies only to age and, where notified, to vacancy earmarking [1] [2]. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is ex-servicemen age relaxation calculated in ADRE?

Period of military service plus three years, applied to the upper age limit for non-gazetted posts [1] [2].

### What is the effective age ceiling for ex-servicemen?

It varies with service length; for long-serving personnel it can extend close to 50, and disabled defence personnel get fixed ceilings of 45–50 [1] [2].

### What documents prove ex-servicemen status?

The discharge book, release orders, pension documents, and service records, attested from the records office [1] [2].

### Do ex-servicemen need Assam domicile?

Yes — the domicile rule applies to all candidates; ESM status relaxes age and may carry earmarked vacancies, not domicile [1] [2].

### Are paramilitary personnel included?

Where the notification specifies — the annexure lists the eligible forces for each round [1] [2].

### Can ex-servicemen apply for Grade 3?

Yes, with a bachelor's degree (or the post's specified qualification) and the ESM age relaxation applied [1] [2].
