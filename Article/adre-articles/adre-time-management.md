**ADRE time management** decides scores as much as knowledge does. The Grade 3 papers give you 150 questions in 180 minutes — 72 seconds per question — while Grade 4 gives 135 questions in 150 minutes, a tighter 67 seconds [1] [2]. Add OMR bubbling, two or three passes through the paper, and the caution that 0.25 negative marking demands, and "knowing the answers" stops being enough: you must know how to *spend your minutes*. This guide covers time management at two levels — during the exam and during your preparation.

## Quick Answer: The Section-Wise Time Split (Paper 3, 180 Minutes)

| Section | Questions | Suggested time | Time per question | Pass strategy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| General English | 35 | 25 min | ~40 sec | First pass — fastest section |
| Reasoning & Mental Ability | 20 | 22 min | ~65 sec | First pass |
| General Mathematics | 35 | 60 min | ~100 sec | Two passes |
| Social Studies | 30 | 30 min | ~60 sec | First pass |
| General Knowledge | 30 | 25 min | ~50 sec | First pass |
| Buffer + OMR bubbling | — | 18 min | — | Third pass on flagged questions |

For Paper 4, shift roughly 15–20 minutes from English to Reading Comprehension, since that section's 25 two-mark questions deserve the most generous time-per-mark in the paper.

## The Three-Pass Method for the Exam

The single most effective time-management technique for ADRE is answering the paper in three passes rather than question-by-question order.

**Pass 1 (about 110–120 minutes): sweep the sure shots.** Move quickly through every section answering only questions you can solve within your time-per-question budget — typically the GK, English, and Reasoning sections in full, plus the easy 20–25 Maths questions. Mark anything you can do but slowly with a flag, and skip anything that looks like a time-sink immediately. This pass typically secures 100–115 questions.

**Pass 2 (about 35–40 minutes): work the flagged questions.** Return to your flagged Maths and mixed questions with the full residual budget. Because you already banked the easy marks, every minute now buys higher-value marks without gambling on unknowns.

**Pass 3 (final 15–20 minutes): OMR check and selective guessing.** Verify your bubbling for the questions you attempted, and consider guessing only on questions you can eliminate to two options — with 0.25 negative marking, a 50/50 guess is break-even, but a 1-in-3 guess is profitable [1] [2]. Never bubble all 150 just because time remains.

## Speed Techniques by Section

**Mathematics** gains speed from recognition, not calculation. Learn to identify the question type in the first five seconds — percentage, SI, time-work, or ratio — and apply its standard method rather than re-deriving. Practise the 20-second recognition drill daily: glance at a question, name the method, move on if you cannot. Long-division-free shortcuts (unit method for time-work, percentage-change multipliers for profit-loss and SI/CI) cut 20–30 seconds per question across a 35-question section.

**English** is your speed bank. Prepositions, synonyms, antonyms, and idioms should take under 20 seconds each because they are pure recall; error-spotting and voice-change take 30–40. Finishing English early is what buys Maths its generous budget.

**Reasoning** is steady: series and coding questions average 45–60 seconds, while seating arrangement and puzzles can take 3 minutes — flag the puzzles and finish them in Pass 2, never in Pass 1.

**GK and Social Studies** are recall-or-skip: you either know the fact in five seconds or you do not; spend the remaining budget on Maths instead of staring at an unknown history date.

## Preparing for Speed: The 10-Second Rule in Practice

Speed in the exam is built during preparation, with one daily habit: the **10-second rule**. During topic practice on pages like [percentages](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/percentages/), [coding-decoding](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/coding-decoding/), [prepositions](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/prepositions/), and [number series](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/number-series/), time every question; if a solved question took more than the target (40–100 seconds by section), re-solve it the next day until it falls under. This converts "I know it" into "I know it fast", which is the only form of knowledge ADRE rewards.

The other half of preparation-time management is the mock-test rehearsal. Take every [mock test](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/mock-test/) at your real exam hour, with OMR bubbling in batches of ten, using the three-pass method and the section split above. Then analyse with the [score calculator](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/score-calculator/) not just how many you got right, but **where your minutes went** — the section-wise timing breakdown in each mock analysis is your personal time-management report card. Track all of this in the [study planner](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/study-planner/).

## Time Management During Preparation (Study Schedule)

Exam-day speed depends on study-phase discipline. Three rules keep the preparation hours honest. **Fixed slots beat long sessions**: study at the same hours daily (see the daily templates in the [30-day](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/30-day-study-plan/), [60-day](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/60-day-study-plan/), and [90-day](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/90-day-study-plan/) plans) so preparation becomes routine rather than willpower. **Time-box every topic**: allocate a maximum of two study days per maths chapter and one day per reasoning family; if a topic is not finished when the box ends, move on and return to it from the error log — unfinished topics are a bigger time waster than imperfect ones. **Protect the testing phase**: in the final three weeks, mock tests and revision are non-negotiable calendar events, not "if there is time" activities.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much time should I give each section in ADRE?

For Paper 3 (150 questions, 180 minutes): English 25 minutes, Reasoning 22, Maths 60, Social Studies 30, GK 25, with an 18-minute buffer for OMR checking and a second pass. Adjust about 15 minutes toward Reading Comprehension for Paper 4 [1] [2].

### What is the best attempt order in the ADRE exam?

English first (fastest section), then Reasoning, then GK and Social Studies, then Maths, finishing with flagged questions and OMR verification. Start with your strongest section if it differs.

### How do I manage time if I am slow at Maths?

Compensate with speed elsewhere: finish English, Reasoning, and GK well under budget to give Maths 70–75 minutes. In preparation, use the 10-second recognition drill on the [most important topics](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/most-important-topics/) to cut per-question time.

### Should I attempt all 150 questions in ADRE?

No. With 0.25 negative marking, attempting 120–135 questions with high accuracy beats attempting all 150 with 25 errors. Leave genuine guesses blank, and guess only when you can eliminate to two options [1] [2].

### How can I improve my speed for the ADRE exam?

Daily timed practice on topic pages, mock tests at exam hour with OMR bubbling drills, the three-pass method, and a personal timing analysis after every mock test. Speed is a trained skill, not a talent.

### Is time management more important than syllabus coverage?

They multiply, not add: strong knowledge with poor time management wastes marks, and perfect timing with weak knowledge has nothing to spend the time on. Build coverage first (study plans), then timing (mock tests).
