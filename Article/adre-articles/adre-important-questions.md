"Which questions are most likely to come in ADRE?" is the question every aspirant asks in the final two months. The honest answer: no one can predict exact questions, but ADRE's school-level, MCQ-based pattern means the **question types repeat far more than the specific questions do** [1] [2]. This page identifies the important question types in every ADRE section, explains why each one matters, and links you directly to free practice for each — so you can convert "important questions" from a vague worry into a daily practice checklist.

## Mathematics: The Ten Question Families That Carry the Paper

Mathematics carries 30–35 marks in every paper, and the exam reliably rotates through ten question families [1] [2]. Mastering these ten families, with 30–40 practice questions each, covers the overwhelming majority of the Maths section.

## English: The Seven High-Repeat Question Types

General English (35 marks in Paper 3; plus 50 marks of Reading Comprehension in Paper 4) repeats a small set of question types with near-total predictability [1] [2].

The **preposition** question is the single most reliable English question in ADRE — fixed preposition usages repeat year after year, so drill [prepositions](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/prepositions/) first. Next come **synonyms and antonyms** ([synonyms](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/synonyms/), [antonyms](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/antonyms/)), which reward a daily ten-word habit; **idioms and phrases** ([idioms and phrases](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/idioms-phrases/)); **active and passive voice** transformation ([active and passive voice](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/active-and-passive-voice/)); **error spotting** across agreement, article, and parallelism rules ([error spotting](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/english-grammar-usage/)); and **fill in the blanks** ([fill in the blanks](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/fill-in-the-blanks/)). For Paper 4, practising one **Reading Comprehension** passage daily from week three onward is the highest-yield English activity available.

## Reasoning: Twelve Methods, One Each

Reasoning's 20 marks come from about a dozen question families, each solvable by one method. The important ones, in approximate frequency order, are [number series](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/number-series/) (difference and pattern series), [coding-decoding](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/coding-decoding/), [analogy](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/analogy/), [classification and odd one out](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/classification-odd-one-out/), [blood relations](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/blood-relations/), [direction sense](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/direction-sense-test/), [syllogism](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/syllogism/), [seating arrangement](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/seating-arrangement/), [clock problems](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/clocks/), [calendar questions](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/calendar/), [dice problems](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/dice-problems/), and [non-verbal reasoning](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/non-verbal-reasoning/). Because the syllabus is small, aim for 90 percent accuracy here — it is the most achievable perfect section in the paper.

## General Knowledge: Assam First, Then India

GK questions in ADRE are single-fact recall, which makes preparation a one-liner game. The **Assam set** is the highest priority: questions on the Ahom dynasty and its kings, the Battle of Saraighat and Lachit Borphukan, Assam's rivers and districts, national parks and sanctuaries, Bihu and Sattriya culture, Assamese literature and personalities, and state schemes appear in high density — start from [Assam GK](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/) and [Assam history](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/history/). The **India set** follows: Indian Constitution fundamentals, freedom movement milestones, Indian geography landmarks, important days, books and authors, and everyday science. The **current affairs set** is covered last and shortest: only the final six months before the exam, tracked through the monthly [current affairs](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/current-affairs/) pages.

## Social Studies: Study Through Questions, Not Pages

Social Studies has the widest syllabus for 20–30 marks, so the question-driven defence matters most: study each topic only after checking how it appeared in [previous year papers](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/previous-year-question-paper/). Recurring anchors include the freedom struggle and its Assam connections, Indian dynasties and their contributions, physical and economic geography of India and Assam, fundamental rights and directive principles, and basic economic concepts. Topic pages like [Indian history](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/indian-history/), [Indian constitution](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/indian-constitution/), and [geography of Assam](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/geography-of-assam/) keep these anchors organised.

## How to Turn Important Questions Into a Daily Drill

The drill that works: pick one question family per day, revise its method or rules for ten minutes, then solve 15–25 questions from the matching topic page. Over a month this covers every family above twice. Anchor the drill with a full [mock test](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/mock-test/) every two to three days — mock tests reveal which "important" families are still weak for you personally, which is information no generic list can give. Score each mock with the [score calculator](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/score-calculator/) to keep the 0.25 negative marking discipline honest [1] [2].

In the final three weeks, collapse the drill to the [most important topics](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/most-important-topics/) ranked list and your own mock-test error log — those two documents together define what "important questions" means for your specific exam.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which are the most important questions in ADRE?

The most repeated families: percentages, profit and loss, SI/CI, ratio, averages, time-work, speed-distance in Maths; prepositions, synonyms/antonyms, idioms, voice change and error spotting in English; series, coding-decoding, analogy and blood relations in Reasoning; and Assam history and geography in GK.

### How many practice questions should I solve per topic?

30–40 per question family for Maths and Reasoning, 15–25 per day as a running drill for English and GK, plus full [previous year papers](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/previous-year-question-paper/) in the testing phase.

### Are expected questions for ADRE available?

Exact expected questions cannot be predicted, but the question types above repeat reliably because the syllabus is school-level and MCQ-based. Type-level prediction — practising every family — is both possible and sufficient [1] [2].

### Which subject's questions should I prioritise?

Mathematics and English first (roughly half the paper), then Assam GK, then Reasoning, then Social Studies. The order follows marks per hour of reliable improvement.

### How do previous year questions help?

They show the real difficulty, framing style, and topic frequency — for example, whether history questions come from kings, battles, or the freedom movement — so your practice matches the actual exam instead of your assumptions.

### Should I practise important questions daily or weekly?

Daily. One question family per day with 15–25 questions builds the recall speed that a 150-question, 180-minute OMR exam demands; weekly bulk sessions do not.
