With a syllabus this broad — five sections across history, geography, polity, economics, mathematics, English, reasoning, and general knowledge — every ADRE aspirant eventually faces the same question: **what should I study first?** This page answers it with a ranked list of the most important topics for ADRE, organised by section and ordered by the combination of exam frequency, marks weightage, and ease of mastery [1] [2].

The ranking logic is worth stating once: a topic is "important" for ADRE when it (a) appears reliably in the exam, (b) carries enough marks to move your score, and (c) can be mastered within your available time. The lists below weight all three.

## Mathematics: Topics Ranked by Return on Effort

Mathematics (30–35 marks per paper) rewards topics in a clear hierarchy. The **foundational pair** — [percentages](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/percentages/) and [ratio and proportion](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/ratio-and-proportion/) — comes first because they underlie profit and loss, interest, mixtures, and data interpretation; weakness here contaminates every later chapter. The **direct-mark trio** — [profit and loss](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/profit-and-loss/), [simple interest](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/simple-interest/), and [compound interest](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/compound-interest/) — follows, typically contributing two to three direct questions each. The **classic arithmetic set** — [time and work](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/word-problems-arithmetic/), [speed and distance](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/speed-distance-time/), and [averages](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/averages/) — forms the third tier, and [number system](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/number-system/) with [HCF and LCM](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/hcf-and-lcm/) rounds out the must-do list. [Mensuration](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/mensuration/) matters most for Paper 3 and Paper 4 candidates; Grade 4 candidates can treat it as a second-tier topic [1] [2].

## English: The Predictable Core

English (35 marks in Paper 3; 60 combined in Paper 4) has the most predictable question pattern of any ADRE section. Study in this order: [prepositions](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/prepositions/) and [tenses](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/grammar-tenses/) (the backbone of error spotting and fill-in-the-blanks questions), then [synonyms](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/synonyms/), [antonyms](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/antonyms/), and [idioms and phrases](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/idioms-phrases/) as a daily ten-item habit, then [active and passive voice](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/active-and-passive-voice/) and [degrees of comparison](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/degrees-of-comparison/), and finally [one-word substitution](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/one-word-substitution/) and [fill in the blanks](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/fill-in-the-blanks/). Paper 4 candidates must add **Reading Comprehension** as a daily practice topic from the third week of any study plan — it is worth 50 marks alone.

## Reasoning: The Fast-Scoring Section

Reasoning's 20 marks come from the smallest syllabus in the paper, which makes it the fastest route to a high-accuracy section. Priority order: [number series](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/number-series/) and [coding-decoding](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/coding-decoding/) (the two most frequent types), then [analogy](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/analogy/), [classification and odd one out](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/classification-odd-one-out/), [blood relations](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/blood-relations/), [direction sense](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/direction-sense-test/), and [syllogism](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/syllogism/). Second-tier topics include [seating arrangement](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/seating-arrangement/), [clock problems](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/clocks/), [calendar questions](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/calendar/), and [non-verbal reasoning](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/non-verbal-reasoning/) — cover them only after the first seven are at 90 percent accuracy [1] [2].

## General Knowledge and Assam GK: Static First, Current Affairs Last

GK marks come from one-liner recall, so the ranking follows repeatability. **Assam GK is tier one**: Ahom dynasty history (kings, battles, administration), Assam geography (rivers, districts, national parks, borders), Assam culture (Bihu, Satras, Sattriya), Assamese literature and personalities, and state schemes — all structured in the [Assam GK](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/) hub with [Assam history](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/history/) at the core. **India static GK is tier two**: the [Indian Constitution](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/indian-constitution/) (fundamental rights, directive principles, important articles), freedom movement milestones, Indian geography landmarks, important days and dates, books and authors, and everyday science. **Current affairs is tier three**: only the final six months before the exam, tracked through the monthly [current affairs](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/current-affairs/) pages. Volatile world GK and niche trivia sit below all three tiers and should be studied last, if at all.

## Social Studies: Selective Coverage Wins

Social Studies (20–30 marks) has the widest syllabus for the marks it offers, so the "most important topics" filter matters most here. Study selectively through [previous year question papers](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/previous-year-question-paper/): the freedom struggle and its Assam connections, Indian dynasties and their contributions, physical and economic geography of India and Assam, Panchayati Raj and local governance, and basic economic concepts (planning, sectors, reforms). Topic pages like [Indian history](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/indian-history/), [geography of Assam](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/geography-of-assam/), and [Indian polity](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/indian-polity/) organise this selectively — depth beyond what the papers show is low-value in a 30–90 day window.

## How to Use This List With Your Study Plan

The topic list is a priority queue, not a substitute for a plan. In the [30-day](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/30-day-study-plan/), [60-day](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/60-day-study-plan/), and [90-day](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/90-day-study-plan/) study plans, this ranking determines which topics fill each week when time is tight: tier-one topics always get their full days, tier-two topics get compressed days, and tier-three topics get whatever remains. Log topic completion in the [study planner](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/study-planner/), verify each finished topic with its question bank, and re-test everything in the final three weeks using [mock tests](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/mock-test/) scored with the [score calculator](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/score-calculator/) — the mock tests convert this generic priority list into a personalised one based on your actual weak areas.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which topic is most important for ADRE?

Percentages and ratio-proportion in Mathematics — they are the foundation of half the arithmetic syllabus and questions from them appear in every paper. In GK, Assam history is the single highest-repeat content area.

### Should I study all topics or only the important ones?

For a 30–60 day window, cover tier-one and tier-two topics fully and skim tier three. For 90+ days, cover everything, but still revise the tier-one topics most often. The mock-test error log is the final arbiter of your personal priority list.

### Which section has the most important topics in ADRE?

Mathematics and English, because together they carry roughly half the marks and both have finite, fully masterable syllabi. Assam GK follows as the highest-repeat GK content [1] [2].

### How do I know if a topic is important enough to study?

Three tests: does it appear in previous year papers, does it carry marks worth the hours, and can you finish it in your remaining time? If all three are yes, it is tier one; one or two yes answers push it to tier two or three.

### What are the most important Assam GK topics for ADRE?

Ahom dynasty history, Assam geography (rivers, districts, parks), Assam culture and festivals, Assamese literature and personalities, and state government schemes — in that order of repeatability.

### Can this topic list replace the study plan?

No. The list tells you what to study first; the [30-day](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/30-day-study-plan/), [60-day](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/60-day-study-plan/), and [90-day](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/90-day-study-plan/) plans tell you when and how much. Use them together.
