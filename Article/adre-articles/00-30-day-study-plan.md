## Understanding Your Target: ADRE Exam Pattern at a Glance

A 30-day plan only works if it is matched to what the exam actually asks. The ADRE selection process consists of a written examination followed by a skill test or interview, and the written paper is OMR-based with questions in Assamese, Bodo, Bengali, English, and Hindi [1] [2].

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 | Grade 4 (Class VIII) | 135 | 135 | 150 minutes | −0.25 |
| Paper 2 | Grade 4 (HSLC) | 135 | 135 | 150 minutes | −0.25 |
| Paper 3 | Grade 3 (HSSLC) | 150 | 150 | 180 minutes | −0.25 |
| Paper 4 | Grade 3 (Bachelor's) | 150 | 175 | 180 minutes | −0.25 / −0.50 |
| Paper 5 | Grade 3 (Driver, HSLC) | 150 | 150 | 180 minutes | −0.25 |

*Sources: official exam pattern tables published by career guidance portals for ADRE 2026 *[1]* *[2]*.*

For Paper 3, the section split is Social Studies (30), General Knowledge (30), Logical Reasoning and Mental Ability (20), General Mathematics (35), and General English (35). For Paper 4, Reading Comprehension and English Language carries 25 questions of 2 marks each, making it the heaviest single section at 50 marks. For Grade 4 papers, all 135 questions come from Social Studies, English, Mathematics, General Knowledge, and Mental Ability and Logical Reasoning.

Notice what this means for your timetable: **Mathematics and English together account for roughly half the marks in every paper.** Many candidates who fail ADRE do so because they spent their preparation time almost entirely on General Knowledge. Your 30-day study plan below weights each week accordingly.

## Phase 1 (Day 1–10): Build the Foundation in Maths, Reasoning, and Assam GK

The first ten days target the two heaviest and most score-reliable sections: **General Mathematics** and **Logical Reasoning and Mental Ability**, plus the Assam-specific portion of General Knowledge, which is both high-yield and largely static.

### Why maths and reasoning first?

Unlike General Knowledge, which changes with current affairs and can never be "completed", mathematics and reasoning follow a fixed syllabus with formulas and methods that you can genuinely master in ten days. A candidate who finishes the core arithmetic topics typically gains 50–65 marks of secure, revision-proof scoring ability. General English grammar follows the same logic, which is why it is woven into Phase 1 evenings.

### Day-by-day schedule for Phase 1

| Days | Subject | Topics to cover |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1–2 | Mathematics | Number system, percentage, ratio and proportion, averages |
| Day 3–4 | Mathematics | Simple interest and compound interest, profit and loss |
| Day 5–6 | Mathematics | Time and work, time-speed-distance, mensuration basics |
| Day 7 | Mathematics | Data interpretation basics and mixed practice set |
| Day 8–9 | Reasoning | Series (number, letter), coding-decoding, analogy, blood relations |
| Day 10 | Reasoning + test | Syllogism, Venn diagrams, direction sense; 50-question topic test |

Each mathematics day follows the same rhythm: 90 minutes of concept study from your chosen book or our topic pages, 60 minutes of solving 40–50 practice questions, and 20 minutes of maintaining a formula sheet. For example, after Day 3 you should have a one-page sheet covering percentage shortcuts, and after Day 6 a one-page sheet covering the time-and-work unit method. These sheets become your primary revision material in Phase 4.

For the Assam GK component, study one compact topic each evening for 30–45 minutes in this order: **Assam history** (Ahom dynasty and its important kings), **Assam geography** (rivers, districts, national parks, borders), and **Assam culture** (Bihu, Sattriya, Satras, important Assamese personalities). Our dedicated [Assam GK](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/) section covers each of these in depth with practice questions.

## Phase 2 (Day 11–20): Complete the Syllabus and Start Topic Testing

With arithmetic and reasoning foundations in place, Days 11 to 20 finish **Social Studies**, **General English**, and the remaining static GK, while converting what you learned in Phase 1 into test speed.

### Day-by-day schedule for Phase 2

| Days | Subject | Topics to cover |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 11–12 | Social Studies (History) | Ancient and medieval India, Assam history: Ahom kingdom, Lachit Borphukan, Battle of Saraighat, freedom movement in Assam |
| Day 13–14 | Social Studies (Geography & Polity) | Geography of India and Assam, Indian Constitution, fundamental rights and duties, Panchayati Raj |
| Day 15–16 | Social Studies (Economics & Science) | Basic economic concepts, Indian economy, everyday science |
| Day 17 | English | Tenses, prepositions, subject-verb agreement |
| Day 18 | English | Synonyms and antonyms, idioms and phrases, one-word substitutions |
| Day 19 | English + GK | Voice change, narration, error spotting; static GK: books and authors, important days, countries and capitals |
| Day 20 | Integrated test | 75-question sectional test across all five subjects |

During Phase 2, your evenings shift from learning to verification: after each day's topic, solve 15–20 questions from our [previous year question papers](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/previous-year-papers/) organised by subject. Seeing how ADRE has actually asked about, say, the Ahom dynasty or profit and loss changes how you memorise the material, because you learn which facts the exam loves.

If you are writing Paper 4 (Bachelor's degree level), replace two English sessions with **Reading Comprehension practice**: solve one long RC passage daily from Day 15 onward, because that section alone is worth 50 marks, the largest single block in the entire exam [1] [2].

## Phase 3 (Day 21–27): Mock Tests, PYQs, and Weak-Topic Repair

This is the phase that separates candidates who pass from candidates who merely study. For seven days you simulate the real exam every single day, analyse honestly, and fix the gaps the analysis reveals.

The daily routine is simple: take a full-length [ADRE mock test](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/mock-test/) at the same time of day as your real exam (ADRE begins in the morning for most batches), then spend two hours analysing it. Analysis means sorting every wrong answer into one of three buckets: a **silly mistake** (you knew it but misread), a **concept gap** (the method was unfamiliar), or an **unstudied topic** (you never saw it). Silly mistakes improve with OMR practice; concept gaps go back into the next day's study; unstudied low-weight topics should generally be abandoned.

| Day | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Day 21 | Mock test 1 + full analysis; revise formula and grammar sheets |
| Day 22 | Solve one ADRE previous year paper under timed conditions |
| Day 23 | Mock test 2 + analysis; redo every question you got wrong |
| Day 24 | Assam current affairs sprint: last 6 months only |
| Day 25 | Mock test 3 + analysis; second pass on weak topics |
| Day 26 | Solve another previous year paper; OMR bubbling speed drill |
| Day 27 | Mock test 4 + final weak-topic list (maximum 10 topics) |

By Day 27 you should have a stable attempt strategy. Given the 0.25 negative marking, our general guidance is to attempt around 120–130 of the 150 questions for Paper 3 and Paper 4 papers, leaving only the questions you would truly guess. The exact safe-attempt number depends on your mock test accuracy, and you can calculate it precisely with our [ADRE score calculator](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/tools/score-calculator/).

## Phase 4 (Day 28–30): Rapid Revision and Exam-Day Readiness

The final three days contain no new learning. Their only job is to move everything you know into fast-recall form and to rehearse the exam-day routine.

On Day 28, revise your formula sheet, grammar rules sheet, and Assam history timeline from start to finish, then skim the [most important ADRE topics](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/most-important-topics/) page we maintain for last-minute focus. On Day 29, revise static GK (important days, books and authors, Assam geography one-liners) and the last six months of Assam and India current affairs. On Day 30, do one light 50-question practice set only to keep your hand moving, and prepare your exam logistics: admit card, ID proof, two pens, and the exam centre route. Check the latest [admit card](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/admit-card/) and [exam date](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/exam-date/) pages for your exact centre and timing.

Sleep matters more in this phase than any study material. Candidates who stay up until 2 a.m. revising consistently score worse than those who sleep early, because ADRE rewards accuracy and calm decision-making under negative marking far more than last-minute memorisation.

The plan above assumes a working day of about five hours. Here is a template you can scale up or down.

## Subject-Wise Weightage and How to Allocate Your 150 Hours

Over 30 days at five hours per day you have roughly 150 study hours. Allocate them by expected marks per hour of effort, not by personal preference.

| Subject / section | Approx. marks | Suggested hours | Priority logic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| General Mathematics | 30–35 | 35–40 | Fixed syllabus, fully masterable, highest ROI |
| General English (+ RC for Paper 4) | 35–50 | 30–40 | Grammar rules are finite; RC improves fast with daily practice |
| Reasoning & Mental Ability | 20 | 18–22 | Small syllabus, quick to master |
| General Knowledge & Current Affairs | 30–35 | 25–30 | Focus on Assam GK and last 6 months current affairs |
| Social Studies | 20–30 | 30–35 | Broad syllabus; study through PYQ-driven topic lists |

Notice that Social Studies gets substantial hours despite moderate marks, because its syllabus is unusually wide; the defence against that is to study it selectively through previous year questions rather than cover-to-cover reading.

## Common Mistakes That Kill a 30-Day ADRE Preparation

Even a well-built plan collapses under a few predictable errors. The most damaging is **collecting too many books**: one solid book per subject plus our topic pages and question banks is enough, and switching resources mid-plan resets your progress. The second is **ignoring negative marking discipline**: attempting 150 of 150 questions with 25 wrong answers nets you less than attempting 125 with 15 wrong; always calibrate your attempt count on mock tests, not on anxiety. The third is **studying GK endlessly while skipping maths practice**: GK gives a false sense of preparation because reading feels like studying, whereas question-solving exposes real gaps. The fourth is **skipping the error log**: candidates who record every mock test mistake and re-solve it score 10–15 marks higher in the real exam than those who just take test after test.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is 30 days enough to prepare for the ADRE exam?

Yes, if you study 4–6 hours daily with a structured plan like the one above and already have school-level fundamentals in mathematics and English. Candidates with weaker basics should use the [ADRE 60-day study plan](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/60-day-study-plan/) instead.

### Which section should I prioritise in a 30-day ADRE study plan?

General Mathematics and General English, because together they carry roughly half the marks in every ADRE paper and both have finite, fully masterable syllabi. Assam GK should follow, since it is high-scoring and static.

### What is the negative marking in ADRE?

0.25 marks are deducted for each wrong one-mark question, and 0.50 marks for each wrong two-mark question. This applies to all papers from Grade 4 Paper 1 through Grade 3 Paper 5 [1] [2].

### Should I solve previous year papers before or after finishing the syllabus?

Start subject-wise PYQ solving from Day 11 onward (Phase 2), and shift to full timed papers in Phase 3. Solving PYQs early tells you exactly how ADRE frames questions from each topic.

### How many hours should I study per day for ADRE in one month?

Five hours per day is the target used in this plan, roughly 150 hours over 30 days. Working professionals can compress to three hours by moving GK and English revision to commute time, at the cost of extending the plan slightly.

### Can I prepare for ADRE Grade 3 and Grade 4 with the same 30-day plan?

The structure is identical; only the paper level changes. Grade 4 candidates (Papers 1–2) write 135 questions in 150 minutes at a slightly easier level, while Grade 3 candidates (Papers 3–5) write 150 questions in 180 minutes. Adjust question speed drills accordingly [1] [2].

### What should I revise in the last 3 days before ADRE?

Formula sheets, grammar rules, the Assam history timeline, Assam geography one-liners, and the last six months of Assam and national current affairs. No new topics should be started after Day 27.
