The final week before the ADRE exam is the most misunderstood phase of preparation. Candidates either cram new topics until 2 a.m. and collapse on exam day, or stop studying entirely and go in cold. The right approach is neither: the final seven days are for **rapid recall activation** — moving everything you already know into fast-access memory — plus one or two light mock tests and complete exam logistics. This guide gives you a day-by-day plan for the last week, assuming you have followed any of our [30-day](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/30-day-study-plan/), [60-day](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/60-day-study-plan/), or [90-day](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/90-day-study-plan/) plans up to this point.

One principle governs everything below: **in the last seven days, revision beats learning, and confidence beats coverage** [1] [2].

## Quick Answer: The Final 7-Day Plan

| Day | Focus | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 7 before | Maths + Reasoning recall | Formula sheets full pass; redo 20 weakest maths questions |
| Day 6 | English recall | Grammar checklist pass; vocab list skim; one RC passage (Paper 4) |
| Day 5 | Assam GK deep recall | Assam history timeline, geography one-liners, culture facts |
| Day 4 | Light mock test | One 100-question sectional test + error-log pass; no full mock |
| Day 3 | India static GK | Important days, books and authors, constitution basics, science facts |
| Day 2 | Current affairs + final log pass | Last 6 months of Assam and national current affairs; re-read error log |
| Day 1 | Logistics + rest | Admit card, ID, pens, centre route, early sleep; only 30 minutes of light skimming |

## What NOT to Do in the Last Week

The last-minute mistakes that cost ADRE candidates marks are remarkably consistent. **Do not start new topics.** A half-learned topic in the final week does two damages: it crowds out revision of the 80 percent you already know, and under the 0.25 negative marking, half-learned topics generate the confident-but-wrong guesses that cost 0.25 marks each [1] [2]. **Do not take full-length mock tests after Day 4** — a poor score three days before the exam destroys confidence with no time to repair it, and a great score invites complacency. **Do not stay up past midnight.** Recall speed, not knowledge volume, decides a 150-question OMR exam, and sleep deprivation attacks recall directly. **Do not discuss "what will come" with anxious peers**; the syllabus is fixed and you have already seen its question patterns in [previous year papers](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/previous-year-question-paper/).

## Day-by-Day: The Recall Activation Schedule

**Days 7–6 (Maths, Reasoning, English).** Begin with the highest-conversion sections. Pass your mathematics formula sheets end to end — [percentages](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/percentages/), [profit and loss](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/profit-and-loss/), [simple interest](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/simple-interest/), [compound interest](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/compound-interest/), [ratio and proportion](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/ratio-and-proportion/), [time and work](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/word-problems-arithmetic/), [speed and distance](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/speed-distance-time/), [averages](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/averages/), [number system](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/number-system/), and [mensuration](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/mensuration/) — and re-solve the 20 questions that gave you the most trouble across your mock tests. Then run the English grammar checklist: [prepositions](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/prepositions/), [tenses](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/grammar-tenses/), [active and passive voice](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/active-and-passive-voice/), and the [synonyms](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/synonyms/), [antonyms](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/antonyms/), and [idioms](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/idioms-phrases/) lists. Paper 4 candidates read one RC passage only, to keep the skill warm.

**Days 5–4 (Assam GK and one light test).** Assam GK is the last-minute section with the best marks-per-minute return: the Ahom dynasty timeline, Lachit Borphukan and the Battle of Saraighat, Assam's rivers, districts and national parks, Bihu and Satra culture, Assamese literature and personalities, and state schemes — all consolidated in the [Assam GK](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/) hub and [Assam history](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/history/). On Day 4, take a light 100-question sectional test from the [mock test](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/mock-test/) collection at exam hour, analyse it calmly, and close the error log for the final time.

**Days 3–2 (India GK, current affairs, error log).** India static GK in one pass: [Indian Constitution](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/indian-constitution/) basics, freedom movement milestones, important days and dates, books and authors, countries and capitals, and everyday science. Then the current affairs sprint — only the last six months of Assam and national current affairs from the monthly [current affairs](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/current-affairs/) pages, nothing older. Close both days with a full re-read of your error log: the mistakes you made in mock tests are the highest-probability predictions of what you might repeat in the exam.

**Day 1 (Logistics and rest).** Prepare the physical exam kit: admit card (downloaded and printed, plus a phone copy), valid photo ID, two black ballpoint pens, and the exam centre route with travel time buffer — check the [admit card](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/admit-card/) and [exam date](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/exam-date/) pages for your centre and reporting time. Eat light, avoid new information entirely after early evening, and sleep at least 7 hours. Thirty minutes of light formula-sheet skimming in the morning is fine; studying after that point raises anxiety, not marks.

## Managing Anxiety in the Final Week

Last-minute anxiety is normal and manageable. Three practical rules work reliably: keep your day structured hour by hour (an idle last week is when anxiety grows), keep every activity in the plan light and familiar (nothing new, nothing timed to panic), and protect sleep as the top priority — candidates who sleep well and revise lightly outperform stressed crammers in accuracy-driven exams like ADRE, where the 0.25 negative marking punishes the rushed guess more than the honest skip [1] [2].

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I study 7 days before the ADRE exam?

Only what you already know: formula sheets, grammar checklists, Assam GK one-liners, your error log, and the last six months of current affairs. No new topics, no full mock tests after Day 4, and sleep at least 7 hours nightly.

### Should I take mock tests in the last week of ADRE preparation?

One light sectional test on Day 4 is enough. Full-length mock tests should end at least four days before the exam so a bad score cannot shake your confidence and a good one cannot make you complacent.

### Is it too late to start ADRE preparation one week before the exam?

It is late, but not hopeless. Focus exclusively on high-repeat material: the [most important topics](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/most-important-topics/), Assam GK, English grammar basics, and two to three [mock tests](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/mock-test/) with honest analysis. Target a safe 100–110 rather than a heroic 140.

### How much should I revise current affairs in the last week?

Only the last six months of Assam and national current affairs, as one-liners, across Days 3–2. Older current affairs have poor recall-to-effort return at this stage.

### What should I carry to the ADRE exam centre?

Admit card (printed plus phone copy), valid photo ID, two black ballpoint pens, a water bottle, and a simple watch. Check the [admit card](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/admit-card/) page for the official instructions on your admit card.

### Can last-minute revision really improve my ADRE score?

Yes, if it is recall activation rather than new learning: a clean final-week pass of formula sheets, grammar rules, and your error log typically converts 5–10 already-known-but-slow topics into reliable marks.
