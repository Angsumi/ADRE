Everything you studied for months converts into marks in a single sitting — and the candidates who planned their **ADRE exam day** in advance typically bank 10–15 more marks than equally prepared candidates who did not. This guide covers the complete exam-day sequence: the morning routine, the exam kit, the first five minutes in the hall, the three-pass answering method, OMR technique under ADRE's 0.25 negative marking, and what to do when things go wrong [1] [2].

## Quick Answer: The Exam-Day Timeline

| Time | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 6:00 AM | Wake, light breakfast, 20-minute formula-sheet skim (nothing new) |
| 7:00 AM | Kit check: admit card, ID, two black pens, bottle, watch |
| 7:30 AM | Leave for centre with 45+ minutes of travel buffer |
| 8:15 AM | Arrive, settle, restroom, calm breathing |
| In hall | Admit-card details, three-pass method, OMR in batches of 10 |
| Last 15 min | OMR verification + selective two-option guesses only |

## The Exam Kit and Morning Routine

The night before, pack a transparent pouch: the **admit card** (downloaded from the official site, printed, plus a phone copy — check the [admit card](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/admit-card/) page), a valid **photo ID**, two working **black ballpoint pens** and a spare, and a simple **watch** if allowed. Confirm your centre and reporting time on the [exam date](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/exam-date/) and [important dates](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/important-dates/) pages, and plan to arrive 45 minutes early — a panicked sprint to the gate is the worst possible warm-up.

Eat a light, familiar breakfast; avoid new foods and excessive caffeine. Wake at least 90 minutes before you need to leave, and spend only the final 20 minutes of that window on a light skim of your formula sheets and Assam GK one-liners. The morning is for activation, not learning — and if you skipped studying the night before, that was correct.

## The First Five Minutes in the Hall

When the question paper arrives, spend the first minutes on setup, not on answering. Verify the paper code matches your admit card, check that the OMR sheet's printed details match yours, and read the instructions panel for any round-specific changes. Then make your **section attack plan**: for Paper 3, English → Reasoning → GK → Social Studies → Maths with a final buffer, exactly as rehearsed in your [mock tests](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/mock-test/); for Paper 4, protect the largest block of time for Reading Comprehension. Candidates who decide their order in the hall waste the decision's 60 seconds; candidates who rehearsed it start Pass 1 immediately [1] [2].

## The Three-Pass Answering Method

**Pass 1 — sweep the sure shots.** Move briskly through the paper answering only what you can solve within the section's time budget: finish English, Reasoning, GK, and Social Studies, and bank the easy 20–25 Maths questions. Flag anything solvable-but-slow; skip anything that looks like a time-sink without a second thought. Target: 100–115 questions answered.

**Pass 2 — work the flags.** Spend the residual budget on flagged Maths and mixed questions. Because the easy marks are already banked, every minute now buys the highest-value marks available.

**Pass 3 — OMR verification and disciplined guessing.** In the last 15–20 minutes, verify your bubbling and consider guessing **only** on questions reduced to two options. Under the 0.25-per-wrong-answer scheme, a 50/50 guess breaks even and a 1-in-3 guess is profitable; a blind 1-in-4 guess loses 0.25 marks every time [1] [2].

## OMR Technique: Where 5 Marks Hide

OMR errors are the silent score-killers of offline exams. Bubble in **batches of ten** — solve ten questions on the question paper, then fill ten circles at once — rather than one-by-one (slow) or page-by-page at the end (the method that produces catastrophic row-shifts). Keep the question number visible while bubbling, align the OMR row with the question number before each batch, and never leave bubbling to the final five minutes. If you skip a question, skip its bubble too, and re-check alignment after every skip. Five marks is a realistic estimate of what clean OMR technique is worth across 150 questions.

## Attempt Strategy Under Negative Marking

Your target attempt count was fixed during mock testing — approximately **120–135 of 150** for Papers 3–5 and **115–125 of 135** for Papers 1–2, at 85 percent-plus accuracy [1] [2]. On exam day, obey that number. If you reach your target with 20 minutes left, stop: additional attempts are guesses the marking scheme will tax. If anxiety tempts you to exceed the number, remember the arithmetic — four wrong answers erase one earned mark. Score your mock rehearsals with the [score calculator](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/score-calculator/) so the target feels like a proven strategy rather than a guess.

## Handling Problems on the Day

If you meet an unfamiliar question type, flag it and move on — one strange question is worth 1 mark, panic is worth the whole paper. If time is running short, protect GK and English completion over unfinished Maths puzzles: their questions finish in under a minute each. If you realise you mis-bubbled a batch, fix it in Pass 3 rather than mid-exam, when stress magnifies the correction error. And if the paper feels harder than expected, remember the relative game: the cut-off moves with the paper, so your preparation, not the paper's difficulty, decides your rank.

## After the Exam

Do not compare answers with friends outside the hall — it changes nothing and destroys your calm before the skill test or interview stage. Rest, eat, and if your paper is followed by a skill test or interview, shift preparation to that stage; the [important questions](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/important-questions/) and [most important topics](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/most-important-topics/) material keeps your knowledge warm meanwhile.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I do in the first 5 minutes of the ADRE exam?

Verify the paper code against your admit card, check the OMR sheet details, read the instructions panel, and start your rehearsed section order — English or Reasoning first for most candidates.

### How should I fill the OMR sheet in ADRE?

In batches of ten: solve ten questions, then fill ten circles, keeping question numbers aligned with OMR rows. Never bubble one-by-one, and never leave bubbling to the final five minutes.

### How many questions should I attempt on ADRE exam day?

About 120–135 of 150 (or 115–125 of 135 for Grade 4 papers) at 85 percent-plus accuracy, with guessing limited to two-option questions. Do not attempt all questions — the 0.25 negative marking punishes blind guesses [1] [2].

### Which section should I attempt first in ADRE?

English, then Reasoning, then GK and Social Studies, then Mathematics with a final buffer — unless your strongest section differs; starting with your strength builds early momentum.

### What if the ADRE paper is harder than expected?

Stay with the three-pass method and your attempt count. Difficulty shifts the cut-off for everyone; your rank depends on executing your preparation better than others, not on solving the hardest questions.

### What should I avoid doing after the ADRE exam?

Comparing answers outside the hall. It cannot change your score and only damages your confidence before the skill test or interview stage.
