The **ADRE 60-day study plan** on this page is the balanced choice for most aspirants: two months is long enough to cover the entire syllabus properly, yet short enough to keep momentum. This guide gives you a full 8-week timetable, week-by-week milestones, subject priorities matched to the official ADRE exam pattern, and a testing-and-revision system for the final two weeks [1] [2].

At four hours per day, this plan gives you roughly **240 study hours**, which is close to the sweet spot for going from a school-level baseline to a competitive ADRE score. If you have only one month, use the [ADRE 30-day study plan](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/30-day-study-plan/); if you have three months or more, use the [ADRE 90-day study plan](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/90-day-study-plan/).

## Quick Answer: The 60-Day Plan in One Table

| Phase | Weeks | Focus | Weekly study hours |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Phase 1 — Core mastery | Week 1–3 | Mathematics and Reasoning to completion; Assam GK habit starts | 25–30 |
| Phase 2 — Syllabus completion | Week 4–5 | English, Social Studies, and India-level static GK | 25–30 |
| Phase 3 — Testing | Week 6–7 | Mock tests, previous year papers, weak-topic repair | 28–32 |
| Phase 4 — Final revision | Week 8 | Short notes, error log, Assam current affairs, OMR rehearsal | 20–25 |

The golden rule carries through all four phases: **Mathematics and English receive first priority every single week**, because together they carry roughly half the marks in every ADRE paper.

## Know Your Paper Before You Start

ADRE has five papers, and your 60-day plan is identical in structure but differs in depth and speed training.

| Paper | Post level | Questions | Marks | Duration | Negative marking |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 | Grade 4 (Class VIII) | 135 | 135 | 150 min | −0.25 |
| Paper 2 | Grade 4 (HSLC) | 135 | 135 | 150 min | −0.25 |
| Paper 3 | Grade 3 (HSSLC) | 150 | 150 | 180 min | −0.25 |
| Paper 4 | Grade 3 (Bachelor's) | 150 | 175 | 180 min | −0.25 / −0.50 |
| Paper 5 | Grade 3 (Driver, HSLC) | 150 | 150 | 180 min | −0.25 |

*Sources: official exam pattern tables published by career guidance portals [1] [2].*

For Paper 3 the sections are Social Studies (30), General Knowledge (30), Logical Reasoning and Mental Ability (20), General Mathematics (35), and General English (35). For Paper 4, Reading Comprehension and English Language adds 25 two-mark questions worth 50 marks — the largest single block in the exam — so Paper 4 candidates start daily RC practice from Week 3, not Week 6. Before fixing your plan, confirm your paper and eligibility with the [ADRE syllabus](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/syllabus/) page and the [eligibility checker](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/eligibility-checker/).

## Week-by-Week 60-Day Timetable

### Weeks 1–3: Core Mastery (Days 1–21)

The first three weeks build the two most score-reliable pillars: Mathematics and Reasoning. Unlike GK, these syllabi are fixed and fully masterable, so early investment here pays throughout the plan.

| Week | Focus | Milestone by week end |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Number system, percentage, ratio and proportion, averages, HCF/LCM | One-page formula sheet; 150+ questions solved |
| Week 2 | Simple interest, compound interest, profit and loss, time and work, time-speed-distance | Second formula sheet; sectional test ≥ 60% |
| Week 3 | Reasoning: series, coding-decoding, analogy, blood relations, direction, syllogism | All reasoning methods learned; mixed reasoning test |

During these three weeks, run a **daily 45-minute Assam GK slot** in the evening: week 1 covers Assam history (Ahom dynasty, important kings and battles), week 2 covers Assam geography (rivers, districts, parks, borders), and week 3 covers Assam culture and personalities. The [Assam GK](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/) section and [Assam history](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/history/) page structure this for you.

### Weeks 4–5: Syllabus Completion (Days 22–35)

With the arithmetic engine running, weeks 4 and 5 complete English, Social Studies, and India-level static GK.

| Week | Focus | Milestone by week end |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 4 | English: tenses, prepositions, subject-verb agreement, synonyms/antonyms, idioms; Social Studies: Indian and Assam history via PYQs | Grammar rules sheet; history notes from PYQ patterns |
| Week 5 | English: active/passive voice, narration, error spotting; Social Studies: geography, polity, economics; India static GK | Full syllabus first pass complete; vocabulary list started |

While studying each Social Studies topic, verify immediately how ADRE has asked it by solving matching questions: [Indian history](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/indian-history/), [Indian constitution](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/indian-constitution/), [geography of Assam](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/geography-of-assam/), and [Assam personalities](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/personalities-of-assam/). This question-verification habit is what keeps the broad Social Studies syllabus from swallowing your calendar.

Paper 4 candidates add one Reading Comprehension passage per day from Week 3 onward and two per day in these weeks.

### Weeks 6–7: Testing and Repair (Days 36–49)

The testing phase converts coverage into marks. Take a full-length [ADRE mock test](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/mock-test/) every two to three days at your real exam time, and spend two hours analysing each one.

| Day | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Day 36 | Mock test 1 + complete analysis + error log |
| Day 38 | Mock test 2 + redo every wrong question |
| Day 40 | Solve one full [previous year paper](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/previous-year-question-paper/) timed |
| Day 42 | Mock test 3 + weak-topic repair from formula and grammar sheets |
| Day 44 | Assam current affairs sprint (last 6 months from [current affairs](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/current-affairs/)) |
| Day 46 | Mock test 4 + second PYQ paper + OMR bubbling drills |
| Day 48–49 | Mock test 5 + final weak-topic list (maximum 12 topics) |

Use the [score calculator](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/score-calculator/) after every test to fix your safe attempt count. The benchmark for Papers 3 and 4 is about 120–135 of 150 attempts with at least 85 percent accuracy under the 0.25 negative marking [1] [2].

### Week 8: Final Revision (Days 50–60)

No new topics enter the plan after Day 49. Week 8 runs three rounds of rapid revision: formula sheets, grammar rules, and the Assam history timeline on Days 50–53; static GK one-liners and the last six months of Assam and India current affairs on Days 54–57; and a light 50-question practice set, final error-log pass, and exam logistics (admit card, ID, pens, centre route) on Days 58–60. Check [important dates](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/important-dates/) and the [admit card](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/admit-card/) page for exact deadlines and centres.

## The 4-Hour Daily Template

| Time block | Activity | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Session 1 (morning) | Core subject: concepts and worked examples | 90 minutes |
| Session 2 (morning) | Question practice on the same topic | 45 minutes |
| Session 3 (afternoon) | Second subject per the weekly focus | 60 minutes |
| Session 4 (evening) | Assam GK / current affairs / English vocabulary | 45 minutes |
| Wrap-up (night) | Revise the day's notes; update error log | 15 minutes |

Working candidates can compress to 2.5–3 hours by moving Session 4 into commute or lunch time; the plan then extends naturally by one to two weeks without structural change. Track everything in the [study planner tool](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/study-planner/).

## Hour Allocation Across the 240 Hours

| Subject / section | Approx. marks | Suggested hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General Mathematics | 30–35 | 60–70 |
| General English (+ RC for Paper 4) | 35–50 | 45–55 |
| Reasoning & Mental Ability | 20 | 25–30 |
| General Knowledge (Assam + India static + current affairs) | 30–35 | 45–55 |
| Social Studies | 20–30 | 45–55 |

The counterintuitive entry is Social Studies receiving as many hours as GK despite fewer marks — its syllabus is far wider, and the defence is to study it strictly through previous year question patterns rather than exhaustive reading.

## How This Plan Differs From the 30-Day and 90-Day Versions

The 30-day plan compresses Phases 1 and 2 into a single foundation fortnight and tolerates no diagnostic time; the 60-day plan gives every phase its natural length and adds a full second mock test cycle. The 90-day plan adds a slow-burn first month for candidates rebuilding school-level basics. If after Week 2 of this plan your diagnostic mock scores are above 65 percent, you are comfortably on track for a strong result.

## Common 60-Day Mistakes

The most expensive 60-day error is **front-loading GK**: candidates who spend their first month reading GK books arrive at Week 6 with weak Maths and no mock test experience, exactly when the plan needs testing. The second is **unanalysed mock tests**: taking five mock tests without sorting errors into silly mistakes, concept gaps, and unstudied topics yields no improvement. The third is **resource hopping**: switching books in Week 5 resets the note-making system that week 8 depends on.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is 60 days enough to prepare for ADRE from scratch?

Yes, for candidates with basic school-level Mathematics and English. The plan assumes about 4 hours of daily study (240 hours total). Candidates with weaker basics should start with the [90-day plan](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/90-day-study-plan/).

### What should I study in the first week of a 60-day ADRE plan?

Number system, percentage, ratio and proportion, and averages, with 150+ practice questions and a formula sheet. Simultaneously begin a daily 45-minute Assam history slot.

### How many mock tests should I take in 60 days of ADRE preparation?

Five to six full-length mock tests in the final three weeks, plus topic-wise tests after each subject block. Quality of analysis matters more than quantity; every test must produce an error-log entry and a repair session.

### Can working professionals follow this 60-day ADRE plan?

Yes, at 2.5–3 hours daily by moving GK and English revision to commute time, which typically extends completion by one to two weeks. The phase structure remains valid.

### Which section is most important in a 60-day ADRE study plan?

Mathematics, followed by English, because together they carry about half the marks in every paper and both have finite syllabi. GK should never be studied in blocks; it works as a daily habit instead.

### What do I revise in the last 10 days of the 60-day plan?

Formula sheets, grammar rules, Assam history timeline, Assam geography one-liners, your error log, and the last six months of Assam and national current affairs. Nothing new.
