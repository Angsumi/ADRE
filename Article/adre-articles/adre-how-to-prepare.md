**Author:** AxomRank | **Updated:** August 2026 | **Reading time:** 10 minutes

"How do I prepare for the ADRE exam?" is the most common question we receive from Assam government job aspirants, and the honest answer is that ADRE is one of the most **self-study-friendly** competitive exams in India. The syllabus never goes beyond school textbooks, the questions are straightforward multiple choice, and the paper language can be Assamese, Bodo, Bengali, English, or Hindi [1] [2]. This guide answers the question completely, in the exact order a beginner should follow, from Day 1 to exam day.

## Quick Answer: The 7 Steps to Prepare for ADRE

| Step | Action | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Check your eligibility (age, qualification, category) | No point preparing for a post you cannot apply to |
| 2 | Identify your paper (1–5) and download the syllabus | Determines exactly what you study |
| 3 | Take one diagnostic mock test | Shows your starting point and weak subjects |
| 4 | Choose a study plan (30 / 60 / 90 days) | Converts "studying" into a calendar with deadlines |
| 5 | Study subjects in the right order (Maths → Reasoning → English → GK → Social Studies) | Maximises marks per hour of effort |
| 6 | Solve topic questions and previous year papers as you go | ADRE is won by question practice, not reading |
| 7 | Finish with full mock tests, short-note revision, and OMR drills | Converts knowledge into exam-day marks |

Steps 3 to 7 are where most candidates need guidance, so the rest of this article expands each one.

## Step 1: Confirm Your Eligibility First

Before opening a single book, verify that you meet the ADRE eligibility criteria for your target post: age limit (with category-wise relaxation), educational qualification, and domicile requirements. A two-minute check with our free [ADRE eligibility checker](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/eligibility-checker/) prevents the most painful outcome in government exam preparation — studying for months and then discovering you cannot apply. Grade 4 posts are open from Class VIII level upward, while Grade 3 posts range from HSSLC pass up to Bachelor's degree depending on the post [1] [2].

## Step 2: Know Exactly Which Paper You Will Write

ADRE has five papers, and each has a different pattern, so your preparation must be paper-specific.

| Paper | Who writes it | Questions | Marks | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 | Grade 4, Class VIII level | 135 | 135 | 150 min |
| Paper 2 | Grade 4, HSLC level | 135 | 135 | 150 min |
| Paper 3 | Grade 3, HSSLC level | 150 | 150 | 180 min |
| Paper 4 | Grade 3, Bachelor's degree | 150 | 175 | 180 min |
| Paper 5 | Grade 3, Driver (HSLC) | 150 | 150 | 180 min |

Every paper shares five to six core sections: Social Studies, General Knowledge, Logical Reasoning and Mental Ability, General Mathematics, and General English. Paper 4 adds Reading Comprehension as a separate 50-mark block, and Paper 5 adds a 20-question Road Transport section [1] [2]. Read the full section-wise breakdown on our [ADRE syllabus](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/syllabus/) page, then study the subject-specific syllabus pages: [Mathematics](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/syllabus/mathematics/), [English](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/syllabus/english/), [Reasoning](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/syllabus/reasoning/), [History](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/syllabus/history/), [Geography](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/syllabus/geography/), and [Polity](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/syllabus/polity/).

## Step 3: Take a Diagnostic Mock Test on Day One

It feels counterintuitive to take a test before studying, but it is the fastest way to personalise your preparation. Take one free [ADRE mock test](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/mock-test/) right now, without preparation, and record the subject-wise results. Most beginners score 40–55 percent, and the subject-wise breakdown tells you where to begin: if Mathematics shows 25 percent and GK shows 60 percent, your first two weeks should be almost entirely Mathematics and Reasoning.

## Step 4: Pick a Study Plan That Matches Your Timeline

This is the step that converts intention into results. We maintain three calendar-bound plans, and the right one depends only on how many months remain before the exam.

| Your situation | Use this plan |
| --- | --- |
| Exam in ~1 month, or revision-focused | [ADRE 30-day study plan](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/30-day-study-plan/) |
| Exam in ~2 months, moderate preparation | [ADRE 60-day study plan](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/60-day-study-plan/) |
| Starting from scratch, exam 3+ months away | [ADRE 90-day study plan](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/90-day-study-plan/) |

Every plan follows the same structure: foundation weeks for Mathematics and Reasoning, completion weeks for English, GK, and Social Studies, a testing phase of mock tests and [previous year papers](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/previous-year-question-paper/), and a final revision-only week. Whichever you choose, log daily progress in our [study planner tool](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/study-planner/) so a missed day is visible and recoverable.

## Step 5: Study Subjects in the Right Order

The biggest structural mistake in ADRE preparation is starting with General Knowledge. GK is pleasant to read but impossible to complete, while Mathematics, Reasoning, and English have finite syllabi that reward daily drilling. The proven order is:

**Mathematics first** (30–35 marks per paper, fully masterable), **Reasoning second** (small syllabus, quick wins), **English third** (grammar rules are finite; Paper 4 candidates start Reading Comprehension practice early), **Assam GK and static GK as a daily 45-minute habit** throughout, and **Social Studies last and selectively**, studied through [previous year question papers](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/previous-year-question-paper/) rather than cover-to-cover reading.

The Assam GK habit deserves emphasis: Assam history, geography, culture, current affairs, and personalities appear in every paper and change very little year to year. Our [Assam GK](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/) hub and the [Assam history](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/history/) section make this habit easy to maintain.

## Step 6: Turn Every Topic Into Question Practice

Reading a chapter on percentages and being able to solve 10 percentage questions under a time limit are different achievements, and only the second one is tested. After each study session, solve 20–40 questions from the matching topic page — for example [simple interest](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/simple-interest/), [profit and loss](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/profit-and-loss/), [prepositions](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/prepositions/), [coding-decoding](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/coding-decoding/), or [history of Assam](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/history-of-assam/) — before moving to the next topic. This question-first loop is what separates 110-mark candidates from 90-mark candidates.

## Step 7: The Final Month Is for Testing, Not Learning

In the last 30 days, stop adding new topics completely. Instead, run the testing-and-revision cycle: a full-length [mock test](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/mock-test/) every two to three days at actual exam time, honest analysis of every wrong answer, targeted repair of weak topics from the [most important ADRE topics](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/most-important-topics/) list, and daily revision of short notes — the same material condensed in our [free notes](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/free-notes/) section. Also rehearse the OMR bubbling mechanics: with 150 questions in 180 minutes and 0.25 negative marking, candidates who never practised bubbling strategy lose 5–10 marks purely to marking errors and panic guesses [1] [2].

## A Realistic Daily Routine

The routine below works for a full-time aspirant and scales down for working candidates by shrinking the evening block.

| Time | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Morning (2 hours) | Core subject study: concepts + worked examples |
| Late morning (1 hour) | Question practice on the morning topic |
| Afternoon (1 hour) | Second subject (rotating per the study plan) |
| Evening (45 minutes) | Assam GK, current affairs, or English vocabulary |
| Night (15 minutes) | Revise the day's notes; update error log and planner |

Working professionals can merge the evening block into commute or lunch time, since GK one-liners and vocabulary need no desk or silence.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I prepare for ADRE without coaching?

Yes. The syllabus is at the school level and all question material is available free on this site: syllabus pages, topic-wise practice, mock tests, previous year papers, and free notes. Coaching adds little beyond structure, which this guide provides.

### How many hours per day should I study for ADRE?

Five hours per day sustains a 90-day plan; four hours sustains 60 days; and the 30-day plan assumes 5–6 hours daily. Quality and consistency matter more than raw hours — a fixed daily slot beats irregular marathon sessions.

### Should I prepare differently for Grade 3 and Grade 4?

The subject list is nearly identical; only the depth changes. Grade 4 (Papers 1–2) questions sit at Class VIII–HSLC level with 135 questions in 150 minutes, so speed practice matters more. Grade 3 (Papers 3–5) demands HSSLC-to-degree-level depth and, for Paper 4, serious Reading Comprehension practice.

### What books should I use to prepare for ADRE?

Start with NCERT or SEBA school textbooks for Social Studies and Science, one standard quantitative aptitude book for Mathematics, one English grammar book, and one concise GK annual. Our [ADRE best books](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/best-books/) page gives subject-wise recommendations.

### How do I handle the negative marking in ADRE?

Attempt questions you are confident in and leave genuine guesses blank. A reliable benchmark from mock testing: attempt roughly 120–130 of 150 questions with at least 85 percent accuracy rather than attempting all 150. Calibrate your exact number using the [score calculator](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/score-calculator/).

### When should I start current affairs preparation?

Keep it as a light daily habit from day one (10–15 minutes), then concentrate Assam and national current affairs revision in the last 60 days. Our [current affairs](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/current-affairs/) section organises this month by month.
