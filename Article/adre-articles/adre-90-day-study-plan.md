The **ADRE 90-day study plan** is the complete-preparation option: three months lets you rebuild school-level fundamentals, cover the entire syllabus without panic, and run the deepest possible testing and revision cycle before exam day. This guide gives you a 12-week timetable with weekly milestones, paper-specific adjustments, and the revision system that turns three months of work into maximum marks under ADRE's 0.25 negative marking [1] [2].

This plan is right for you if you are starting from scratch, balancing a full-time job, or rebuilding fundamentals after years away from textbooks. If the notification is already out, switch to the [ADRE 60-day study plan](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/60-day-study-plan/) or the [ADRE 30-day study plan](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/30-day-study-plan/).

## Quick Answer: The 90-Day Plan in One Table

| Phase | Weeks | Focus | Weekly hours |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Phase 1 — Fundamentals | Week 1–4 | Rebuild Maths and English basics; start Assam GK habit | 15–20 |
| Phase 2 — Syllabus mastery | Week 5–8 | Complete Reasoning, GK, and Social Studies at exam depth | 20–25 |
| Phase 3 — Testing | Week 9–11 | Mock tests, previous year papers, attempt-strategy calibration | 22–28 |
| Phase 4 — Final revision | Week 12 | Short notes, error log, current affairs, exam logistics | 15–20 |

At roughly 3.5 hours per day this plan delivers about **315 study hours** — the most comfortable runway available for a first-time ADRE attempt.

## Week-by-Week 90-Day Timetable

### Weeks 1–4: Fundamentals (Days 1–28)

The first month deliberately moves slower than the shorter plans, because weak fundamentals compound into every later phase.

| Week | Focus | Milestone by week end |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Number system, fractions, decimals, basic operations; English parts of speech | Comfort with school-level arithmetic; grammar basics clear |
| Week 2 | Percentage, ratio and proportion, averages; tenses and subject-verb agreement | First Maths formula sheet; daily Assam GK slot begins |
| Week 3 | Simple interest, profit and loss, time and work; prepositions and articles | Sectional Maths test ≥ 55%; Assam history notes started |
| Week 4 | Speed-time-distance, mensuration basics; synonyms, antonyms, vocabulary habit | Complete diagnostic [mock test](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/mock-test/) to set baseline |

From Week 1 onward, keep a **daily 30–45 minute Assam GK slot**: history in Weeks 1–2, geography in Week 3, culture and personalities in Week 4, using the [Assam GK](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/assam-gk/) hub. Because you have three months, Assam GK gets covered twice over the plan — once leisurely here, once intensively in Phase 3.

### Weeks 5–8: Syllabus Mastery (Days 29–56)

Phase 2 completes the full syllabus at exam depth and is where the plan earns its "complete roadmap" claim.

| Week | Focus | Milestone by week end |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 5 | Reasoning complete: series, coding-decoding, analogy, blood relations, direction, syllogism, seating arrangement | All reasoning methods mastered; mixed test ≥ 65% |
| Week 6 | Mathematics completion: compound interest, mixture, partnership, data interpretation | Second formula sheet; full Maths syllabus done |
| Week 7 | Social Studies: Indian and Assam history, geography of India and Assam, polity basics — driven by [previous year papers](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/previous-year-question-paper/) | Social Studies first pass via PYQ patterns |
| Week 8 | India static GK + Assam current affairs foundation; English completion (voice, narration, error spotting, RC daily for Paper 4) | Full syllabus first pass complete |

The rule that protects Phase 2: every Social Studies and GK topic is verified against how ADRE actually asked it. Work through matching topic pages such as [Indian constitution](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/indian-constitution/), [geography of Assam](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/geography-of-assam/), [history of Assam](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/history-of-assam/), [percentages](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/percentages/), [profit and loss](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/profit-and-loss/), and [prepositions](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/topics/prepositions/) as you finish each topic.

### Weeks 9–11: Testing and Calibration (Days 57–84)

The testing phase runs a full mock test every two days, always at your real exam time, followed by two hours of structured analysis.

| Day | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Day 57 | Mock test 1 + error classification (silly mistake / concept gap / unstudied) |
| Day 59 | Mock test 2 + redo every wrong question + formula sheet pass |
| Day 61 | Full previous year paper timed; OMR bubbling in batches of ten |
| Day 63 | Mock test 3 + second error-log repair session |
| Day 65 | Assam and India current affairs intensive sprint (last 6–8 months) |
| Day 67 | Mock test 4 + weak-topic list trimmed to maximum 15 topics |
| Day 69–75 | Alternate full mock tests and previous year papers; attempt strategy locked |
| Day 77–84 | Mock tests 5–6; final weak-topic pass; accuracy benchmark ≥ 85% |

Calibrate your attempt count with the [score calculator](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/score-calculator/). Under the 0.25 negative marking, the discipline that separates 130-plus scorers is attempting roughly 120–135 of 150 questions (or 115–125 of 135 for Grade 4) and never exceeding that number on exam day [1] [2].

### Week 12: Final Revision (Days 85–90)

Nothing new enters after Day 84. Revise formula sheets, grammar rules, and the Assam history timeline on Days 85–87; static GK one-liners and current affairs on Days 88–89; and a single light 50-question set plus exam logistics (admit card, ID proof, pens, centre route, sleep schedule) on Day 90. Watch the [important dates](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/important-dates/) and [admit card](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/admit-card/) pages for official deadlines.

## The Daily 3.5-Hour Template

| Time block | Activity | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Session 1 | Core subject concepts and examples | 75 minutes |
| Session 2 | Question practice on the same topic | 45 minutes |
| Session 3 | Second subject per weekly focus | 60 minutes |
| Session 4 | Assam GK / current affairs / vocabulary | 30 minutes |
| Wrap-up | Revise the day's notes; update error log and [study planner](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/study-planner/) | 10 minutes |

The plan tolerates 2.5 hours on busy days because its Phase 1 is deliberately cushioned; missed days in Weeks 1–4 can be absorbed, but missed testing days in Weeks 9–11 cannot.

## Why Three Months Wins: The Revision Advantage

The real benefit of 90 days is not coverage but **revision depth**. This plan supports four full revision cycles of your short notes (end of Weeks 4, 8, 11, and 12) versus two in the 60-day plan and one in the 30-day plan. Because ADRE rewards recall speed under time pressure more than one-time understanding, the candidate who revised four times almost always out-scores the candidate who studied more once. Condense your revision material using the [free notes](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/adre/free-notes/) approach: one page per topic, built as you study.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is 3 months enough to crack ADRE from zero?

Yes, comfortably. The 90-day plan assumes 3.5 hours daily (about 315 hours) and includes a fundamentals phase specifically designed for candidates starting from scratch or returning to study after a long gap.

### Can I follow the 90-day plan while working full time?

Yes. The plan works at 2.5–3 hours daily for the first eight weeks; you may lose one cushion week, but Phases 3 and 4 (testing and revision) should stay intact at full intensity.

### What is different in the 90-day plan compared to 60 days?

A four-week fundamentals phase that rebuilds school-level Maths and English, a slower syllabus phase, an extra week of testing, and a fourth revision cycle — the difference that most often moves scores from 100 to 130+.

### When should I start current affairs in a 90-day ADRE plan?

Light monthly reviews from Week 1 using the [current affairs](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/current-affairs/) pages, then a concentrated Assam and India sprint in Weeks 10–11, and a final revision pass in Week 12.

### How many mock tests should I attempt in 90 days?

Six full-length mock tests in Weeks 9–11, plus sectional tests after each subject block in Weeks 1–8. Every mock test must end with two hours of error analysis using the [score calculator](https://Angsumi.github.io/ADRE/tools/score-calculator/).

### Should Grade 4 candidates follow the same 90-day plan?

The structure is identical; Grade 4 candidates (Papers 1–2) work at Class VIII–HSLC depth and should add speed drills, since 135 questions in 150 minutes is the tightest time budget of any paper.
